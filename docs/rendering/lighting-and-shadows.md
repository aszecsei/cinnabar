# Lighting and shadows

Why the renderer is clustered forward with a prepass, the prepass's outputs, the visibility buffer that was measured against it, lights and their clusters, area lights with linearly transformed cosines, and shadows as maps or traced masks.

**In this document:** [Clustered forward](#clustered-forward) · [Prepass](#prepass) · [Visibility buffer](#visibility-buffer) · [Lights and clusters](#lights-and-clusters) · [Area lights](#area-lights) · [Shadows](#shadows)

## Clustered forward

The renderer has one shading path: forward shading with a prepass.

- **Many shading models cost nothing.** Each material's pixel shader runs its own shading model. A deferred path would have to pack every model's inputs into one G-buffer and branch on a model ID.
- **Transparent surfaces** use the same light loop as opaque ones.
- **The prepass gives the screen-space passes their inputs:** depth, a normal and a velocity for each pixel. Ambient occlusion and screen-space GI then run before the forward pass, and the forward pass applies them to indirect light only, which a deferred or a plain forward path gets wrong or gets a frame late.
- **The clusters serve fog too.** The fog volume's froxels and the light clusters share their depth slices, so fog reads the same light lists.
- **The prepass removes overdraw.** The forward pass tests depth with `Equal` and shades each pixel once.

### Prepass

- Writes depth, the normal in the world (folded into the octahedron, `NORMAL_FORMAT`, `RG16_Float`) and the velocity: how far the surface moved on the screen since last frame, in UV (`VELOCITY_FORMAT`, `RG16_Float`), from the instance's last pose and last frame's camera and projection, without the camera's jitter. The pass clears the velocity to `VELOCITY_NONE` where it draws nothing, which temporal AA then moves with the camera.
- **The IDs stay in the forward pass.** It shades each visible pixel once, so it writes each pixel's ID as the prepass would, and a game's own draws in `world_draw` write theirs in the same target.
- The normal is the material's normal, normal maps included. The prepass's pixel shader returns only the normal, so DXC drops the parts of `evaluate` that only the other surface inputs need; the opacity still discards for a masked material.
- The forward pass places vertices with the same code, `clip_position` in `material_vertex.hlsli`, whose result is `precise`, so its depths equal the prepass's for its `Equal` test.

### Visibility buffer

A visibility buffer writes each pixel's triangle in a pass of its own, then shades the pixels in compute, so no pixel shader runs on the 2x2 quads that small triangles leave mostly empty (see Burns and Hunt in the [References](README.md#references)). The renderer has one as a prototype on the [mesh shader path](gpu-scene.md#mesh-shader-path), to measure it against the forward pass. `Quality.visibility_buffer` turns it on, and no preset does: the editor's viewport toggle Visibility Buffer does (see [Quality](quality.md#quality)). The decision is in the README's [Decisions](README.md#decisions): clustered forward stays the shading path.

**How it draws** (`lib/engine/render/visibility.odin`):

- **Which bins.** The camera's opaque bins on the mesh shader path whose pipelines have the visibility passes compiled (`begin_visibility`, `Scene_Frame.bin_visibility`). The view model, impostors, billboards, patches, particles, transparent surfaces and a game's own draws stay forward. A material makes its visibility pipelines only after a frame first asks for them, and no bin waits for them.
- **The visibility pass,** after the prepass, and a late one after the late prepass, draws those bins in place of the prepass with each material's `Visibility` pass (see [Materials](materials.md#permutations)). It writes the prepass's normal and velocity, and `VISIBILITY_FORMAT`, an `RGBA32_Uint` target: the draw's index plus 1, the instance ID, the meshlet, and the triangle with whether it is a front face and the pipeline that drew it. The mesh shader gives these to each triangle as a per-primitive attribute, and the amplification shader's payload carries the instance ID. A masked material discards as in its prepass.
- **The stencil** keeps the two paths apart. The visibility pass draws with `VISIBILITY_STENCIL` (bit 2) as its reference and sets it. Every other prepass draw clears it, so a pixel that a later draw covers is the forward pass's. The forward pass tests both bits, so it skips the visibility buffer's pixels. Nothing else reads bit 2.
- **Binning** (`visibility_bin.hlsl`): four compute passes, as hit shading's: clear, count, offsets, scatter. Each 2x2 quad goes into the bin of each pipeline that one of its pixels has, at most four, and each bin's dispatch is indirect.
- **Shading** (`material_shade.hlsli`, the material's `cs_shade`): a pass clears the scene's colors and IDs, and each pipeline shades its bin, 16x16 threads a group, a 2x2 quad of threads for each quad of pixels. A thread rebuilds its pixel's triangle with `place_vertex`, as the mesh shaders place it, and takes its barycentrics where the camera's ray through the pixel's center meets the triangle's plane, which is the rasterizer's perspective-correct interpolation. It fills `Material_Inputs` as the forward pass does, runs `evaluate`, and shades with `forward_color_at`, the forward pass's lighting, at the prepass's depth. It writes the color and the entity's ID. The world pass then loads both and draws the rest.
- **Derivatives.** A pixel of the quad that is not the material's takes the quad's first triangle of the material, so where one triangle covers the quad, `ddx` and `ddy` of the real UV are exact. Where two triangles of the material share the quad, the quad is mixed, and `cn_sample` takes the analytic derivatives of `uv0` at the pixel instead, from the barycentrics a pixel to the right and a pixel down. These are wrong for a material that scales or computes its UVs, or samples with `uv1`, in mixed quads only.

**Measuring.** The forward pass counts its quads when the frame asks (`Frame_Desc.shading_stats`, the viewport's Shading Stats): a lane of each quad adds to the quad's count for the `Quad_Overdraw` view, and each wave adds its quads and its pixels to `Shading_Stats`, which comes back as the culling stats do. The pixels over four times the quads are the quads' efficiency, and the rest are helper lanes. The shading counts the same, and its mixed quads. The forward pixel shader has `[earlydepthstencil]`, which writes no depth, so only the pixels that it shades count. The editor shows the counts in the Resource Monitor, and a capture's sidecar has each pass's GPU time (see [Capture](../editor/capture.md#sidecar)). `editorctl`'s `viewport` and `camera` commands set the mode and the view, so a script measures each view in each mode (see [Capture](../editor/capture.md#editorctl)).

**Measurements.** On an RTX 5090 at 1320x800, High quality with temporal AA, in the Intel sample (see `samples/intel/README.md`). Each value is the median of five captures, or three for Jungle Ruins, at least two runs each. The pass times are the profiler's GPU spans, which can overlap their neighbours (see [Profiling](../core/profiling.md#gpu-timestamps)). The forward pass's cost is its prepass and world pass; the visibility buffer's is what is left of the prepass, the visibility passes, the binning, the shading and what is left of the world pass.

| View | Forward quad efficiency | Visibility buffer's mixed quads | Forward prepass + world (ms) | Visibility buffer: visibility + binning + shading + world (ms) | GPU frame, forward → visibility buffer (ms) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1: Sponza's nave, the scene's camera | 56 % | 45 % | 0.13 + 0.14 | 0.23–0.25 + 0.09 + 0.11 | 4.23 → 4.46–4.73 |
| S2: Sponza's upper gallery, looking down | 59 % | 36 % | 0.12 + 0.13 | 0.22 + 0.11 + 0.11 | 3.99–4.10 → 4.23–4.64 |
| S3: Sponza's outer wall, large triangles | 99 % | 1 % | 0.06 + 0.06 | 0.11 + 0.04 + 0.06 | 2.10 → 2.14–2.22 |
| S4: Sponza's curtains, close | 59 % | 43 % | 0.07 + 0.08 | 0.10 + 0.07 + 0.10 | 3.89–3.93 → 4.08–4.14 |
| J1: Jungle Ruins, the scene's camera | 33 % | 60 % | 21.1–31.7 + 20.1–27.0 | 39.5–39.8 + 0.24 + 0.18 + 2.5 | 46.3–63.6 → 47.2–47.6 |
| J2: Jungle Ruins' ground cover, close | 35 % | 59 % | 6.3–8.4 + 6.8–8.0 | 9.6–10.1 + 0.29 + 0.27 + 2.2 | 17.9–21.1 → 17.2–17.6 |
| J3: Jungle Ruins from above | 30 % | 57 % | 96–150 + 90–126 | 182–183 + 0.29 + 0.18 + 2.8 | 190.9–280.6 → 190.2–191.0 |

What they show:

- **Quad overdraw is real.** The forward pass's lanes are 30 to 60 % helper lanes wherever triangles are small: Sponza's curtains and Jungle Ruins' foliage, at the level of detail's 1 pixel of error. The visibility buffer shades 1.6 to 2.1 times fewer quads there.
- **Shading is not where the frame's time goes.** In Sponza, the forward pass's world pass is 0.08 to 0.14 ms. The visibility buffer's shading of the same pixels is 0.06 to 0.11 ms, a fifth cheaper on the nave and dearer on the curtains, where it rebuilds a triangle for each pixel. The binning adds 0.04 to 0.12 ms, so the shading as a whole is not cheaper.
- **The visibility pass costs more than the prepass that it replaces:** 1.5 to 2 times the prepass of the same bins in Sponza and in Jungle Ruins' faster runs. Writing constants in place of the per-primitive IDs costs the same, so the IDs' attribute is not the cause; the extra 16-byte target and the pass's own work are what is left. So every Sponza view is 2 to 12 % slower with the visibility buffer.
- **Jungle Ruins is bound by geometry,** and there the forward pass draws its millions of meshlets twice, once in the prepass and once in the world pass. The visibility buffer draws them once, but its visibility pass costs about as much as both. The frame is the same as the forward pass's best runs and up to a third faster than its slowest. The forward pass's times on Jungle Ruins came in two groups from one run to the next, with the same draws and the same image; the visibility buffer's did not. The cause is not known.
- **Mixed quads are common:** 36 to 45 % of the visibility buffer's quads in Sponza's dense views and 57 to 60 % in Jungle Ruins. Their samples take `uv0`'s analytic derivatives, so the dense foliage's texture levels differ a little from the forward pass's: it is slightly greyer. Sponza's images match the forward pass's.

**The decision.** Clustered forward stays the shading path. The visibility buffer removes the quad overdraw, but in these scenes the overdraw is not the cost: Sponza's shading is a tenth of a millisecond, and Jungle Ruins' frame is its geometry. The prototype stays behind its toggle, off, with the measurements, so the comparison can be made again on new content or hardware. It is worth another look when a visibility pass costs no more than the prepass, or when content is bound by the forward pass's second pass over the geometry, which a cheaper geometry pass would help either way.

### Lights and clusters

- **Light components:** `Directional_Light`, `Point_Light` and `Spot_Light`, and the [area lights](#area-lights) `Quad_Light`, `Disk_Light` and `Line_Light` (see [Components](assets.md#components)). Each frame, `extract_lights` writes them relative to the camera into the upload ring (`Light_Record`), the directional ones first, at most `MAX_LIGHTS` (1024), of which `MAX_DIRECTIONAL_LIGHTS` (4). The editor's hidden entities light nothing.
- **A light's falloff:** its color times its intensity. A punctual light falls off with the square of the distance, windowed to 0 at its range: `saturate(1 - (d / range)^4)^2 / d^2`. A spot light's cone also falls off from 1 at its inner angle to 0 at its outer angle, squared.
- **Clusters:** a compute pass (`cluster_lights.hlsl`) assigns the punctual and area lights to clusters: tiles of `CLUSTER_TILE` (64) pixels, in `CLUSTER_SLICES` (32) depth slices, exponential from the near plane to `CLUSTER_FAR` (500 m); the last slice reaches on to infinity. A thread for each cluster tests each light's sphere against the cluster's box in view space, so a spot light counts as its whole sphere. It takes room for its lights in one index list with an atomic add, and writes its offset and count. The list has room for `CLUSTER_AVERAGE_LIGHTS` (32) lights per cluster on average; a cluster that finds it full gets fewer. The clusters' buffers live in the `Renderer` and grow with the frame's size.
- **The forward pass** finds its pixel's cluster from its position and its depth (`cluster_of`), and calls the shading model's `shade_direct` for each directional light, then for each light of its cluster, with the light's shadow (`material_lighting.hlsli`). It calls `shade_area` for an area light. Without the cluster pipeline, the forward pass leaves the punctual and area lights out.
- The directional lights are not clustered. Every pixel loops over them.
- **The sun:** an `Environment` in Atmosphere mode can name a directional light as its sun. The air between the camera and the sun then dims and colors that light's own light, to 0 below the horizon (see [The sun](sky.md#the-sun)).

### Area lights

- **Shapes:** `Quad_Light` is a rectangle, `Disk_Light` a disk or an ellipse, and `Line_Light` a tube with round ends, or an ideal line when its radius is 0. A quad or a disk shines along its entity's +z, or from both faces when it is two-sided. A line is along its entity's +x.
- **Intensity** is a point light's: an area light gives what a point light of the same intensity would give along the light's +z, so a bigger light spreads the same light over more of its surface. Its radiance is its color times its intensity over the area that it shows along its normal: 4·hx·hy for a quad, π·rx·ry for a disk, 4·h·r + π·r² for a tube, and 2·h for an ideal line, whose radiance is per meter of its width.
- **The record** (`Light_Record`) holds an area light's middle, its normal in `direction`, its half size along its +x in `extent`, and in `extent_y` a quad's half height, a disk's radius along its +y, or a line's radius. Its range is the component's range plus its shape's bounding radius, so the clusters' sphere test covers the whole shape. Its range's window starts from its middle.
- **Shading:** linearly transformed cosines (`ltc.hlsli`). A lobe that is a clamped cosine transformed by a 3x3 matrix integrates over a shape as the cosine does over the shape transformed by the matrix's inverse. `shade_area` calls `ltc_integral` with Lit's specular inverse from `ltc_ggx`, and with the identity for Lambert's diffuse, then multiplies by the light's radiance.
  - A quad is a polygon, clipped to the horizon and integrated edge by edge.
  - A tube is the polygon of its outline from the point: the hull of its ends' spheres, an arc of four edges for each, joined by their tangents. End on, the outline is the near sphere's.
  - An ideal line has a closed form, and a disk is the sphere with its ellipse's form factor, clipped to the horizon by a table (Heitz and Hill, 2017).
- **The tables:** two 64x64 `RGBA32_Float` textures (`ltc_ggx.bin`, which `lights_init` uploads). The first holds the inverse matrices, by roughness across and by sqrt(1 − cos θ) of the view down. The second holds the specular's magnitude and Fresnel term, and the horizon-clipped sphere's form factor.
- **Fitting:** `just ltc-fit` fits the tables (`tools/ltc_fit`). For each texel, Nelder–Mead finds the matrix whose lobe best matches `shade_direct`'s GGX specular without Fresnel, first about the specular's average direction, then tilted. The tool prints how much of the light each fit puts in the wrong place: little except at grazing views, where linearly transformed cosines cannot follow GGX. Run it after a change to Lit's specular, and commit the table.
- **Fog** sees an area light as a point light at its middle, whose light a quad or a disk sends out with the cosine of its face.

### Shadows

| Light | With `Ray_Query` | Without |
|---|---|---|
| Directional | A screen-space mask: a compute pass traces from the prepass depth | Cascaded shadow maps |
| Spot, point | A mask for the few most important; maps for the rest | A shadow atlas |
| Quad, disk, line | As a point light at the light's middle | As a point light at the light's middle |

- **Which lights:** the lights that cast shadows take them in order: the directional lights, then the punctual lights by how much light they bring to the view (their brightest channel over their squared distance), leaving out those whose spheres miss the view (`assign_shadows`). With `Ray_Query` and traced shadows in the [quality](quality.md#quality), the first `MAX_MASK_LIGHTS` (4) take the mask's channels. The others take shadow maps while the atlas has room. Only one directional light has cascades; another without a mask channel has no shadow.
- **The shadow mask** is a screen-space texture, `RGBA8_Unorm`, with one channel for each traced light. A compute pass (`shadow_mask.hlsl`) rebuilds each pixel's position from the prepass's depth, where the camera's jitter put the pixel's center, moves it off the surface along the prepass's normal by a hair, a tenth of a millimeter per meter of depth plus two thousandths of a millimeter per meter from the render origin, which is up to 1.5 km away, and traces one ray toward each light with `RayQuery` from the same hair along the ray, accepting the first hit, against the instances that cast shadows (`RAY_MASK_SHADOWS`). An offset along the normal moves a shadow's edge across the surface by the offset over the tangent of the light's angle to the normal, so two surfaces that meet at a crease disagree on where the shadow crosses their seam by as much; an offset along the ray moves nothing, so the hair is only what the depth's precision needs. A light records its channel, and the forward pass reads the channel at its pixel. The shadows are hard. This is the sample's ray-traced shadow, made general.
- **Acceleration structures:** the mask traces the ray scene, which traced shadows request (see [Ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene)). A mesh's bottom-level structure holds all its triangles, opaque. The residency table creates it when the mesh becomes ready, once the first frame has traced (see [GPU residency](assets.md#gpu-residency)); that frame also creates the top-level structure and gives every resident mesh its own. The frame that next traces builds them, then the top-level structure, before the prepass. When the quality turns tracing off, they stay.
- **The atlas:** one `D32_Float` texture, a frame's transient, as large as the level's `Shadow_Params` say (`SHADOW_PARAMS`; 4096 at High, see [Quality: Levels](quality.md#levels)). A cascade takes a cell (1024 pixels at High), from the first cells; a spot light takes a slot (512 at High), four to a cell, and a point light six slots, one for each face of its cube.
- **Cascades:** the level's, up to `SHADOW_CASCADES` (4), which cover the view to `SHADOW_DISTANCE` (100 m), split between even and exponential by `SHADOW_SPLIT_LAMBDA` (0.75), unless the volumes' `Shadows` settings give others (see [Settings](frame.md#settings)). Each is an orthographic view of the bounding sphere of its slice of the view's frustum. The sphere's radius is rounded up to sixteenths of a meter, and its center snaps to the cascade's texels in the render origin's space, so the shadow's edges stay still as the camera moves and turns. Depth is clamped, not clipped, and culling has no plane toward the light, so casters between the light and the view still cast.
- **Spot and point lights:** a perspective view with reversed depth from `SHADOW_NEAR` (5 cm), as wide as the spot light's cone or 90° for each cube face, culled at the light's range. An area light's shadows are a point light's from its middle, so they are hard.
- **The depth pass:** the shadow maps are one render pass with a piece for each view, each with its own viewport of the atlas. Each draws its view's bins with each material's depth pipeline, so masked materials cast the right shadows. The pipelines have a depth bias and a slope bias against acne, `SHADOW_DEPTH_BIAS` and `SHADOW_SLOPE_BIAS`.
- **Sampling:** the forward pass moves the point half a texel of the view toward the light, against the map's depth precision, and along its normal by half a texel times the sine of the light's angle to the normal, against the taps' spread where the light grazes the surface. A normal offset moves the edge across the surface by the offset over the tangent of that angle, so the sine keeps a crease's seam within half a texel, and the casters' slope bias covers the rest. It projects the moved point and takes the level's bilinear comparisons with a comparison sampler (`Frame_Constants.shadow_filter`): one, the hardware's 2x2; four a half texel apart, which filter a 3x3 neighbourhood; or nine a texel apart, which filter 4x4. The taps stay inside the view's rectangle. A directional light picks the first cascade that reaches the pixel's depth; beyond the last, it casts no shadow. A point light picks the face of the direction from the light.
- The ray-tracing pipeline path stays the sample's alternative (see [GPU](../gpu/ray-tracing.md#ray-tracing)). Every ray-traced path has the shadow map fallback: a device without `Ray_Query`, and the lights after the mask's four, take shadow maps.
