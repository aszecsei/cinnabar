# Ray scene

The GPU scene's top-level acceleration structure, which every feature that traces shares, and hit shading, which evaluates the materials at a feature's ray hits (`lib/engine/render/ray_scene.odin`). Both need `Ray_Query`. The traced shadow mask and the traced fog injection trace the structure. Read [GPU scene](gpu-scene.md#gpu-scene) and [GPU: Ray tracing](../gpu/ray-tracing.md#ray-tracing) first.

## Acceleration structure

- **Requests.** A feature that traces calls `request_ray_scene(f)` while the frame begins, before `add_ray_scene_passes`; the traced shadows do in `assign_shadows`. The request is false without `Ray_Query`. The first request makes the top-level structure, `Ray_Scene.accel`, and has every resident mesh make its bottom-level structure (`residency_enable_accels`). When no feature traces, the structures stay, so tracing again builds nothing new.
- **The build.** In a frame with a request, `add_ray_scene_passes` builds the meshes' structures that became ready, then the top-level structure from the frame's instances, right after the GPU scene's passes and before the prepass. Every pass after it can trace `ray_scene_accel(f)`. The build reads only the upload ring and the meshes' buffers.
- **Instances.** Each `Mesh_Renderer` whose mesh has a structure, except the view model's, is an instance, in the render origin's space, with its slot as its ID, and opaque. Its 8-bit mask says what sees it: `RAY_MASK_SHADOWS` when its `Mesh_Renderer` casts shadows, and `RAY_MASK_SURFACES` when it has an opaque or masked submesh, which hit shading evaluates. An instance with neither is left out. The shadow mask and the fog trace `RAY_MASK_SHADOWS` only, so they see the shadow casters, as the shadow maps do.
- **Limits.** Instance sets, patch surfaces and particles are not in the structure. Each mesh's structure is its level 0, as authored, without a material's displacement or a billboard's turn.

## Hit shading

A client traces the structure in its own pass and writes a `Hit_Record` for each ray. `add_hit_shading(f, batch)` then gives a `Hit_Surface` for each ray that hit: the material's surface at the hit, evaluated by the material's own `evaluate`.

- **Records** (`ray_scene.hlsli`). `trace_hit(accel, ray, mask, spread)` traces the nearest hit and gives its record: the slot with a front-face bit, or `HIT_MISS`; the primitive; the barycentrics as unorm16; the ray's direction folded into the octahedron; the distance; and the ray cone's width at the hit, which is `spread` per meter. The client stores it with `store_hit`, and later reads the surface with `load_hit_surface`. A `Hit_Surface` (32 bytes) has the position relative to the render origin, the shading normal and the triangle's normal toward the ray's side, the diffuse albedo as unorm8 with flags, and the emission and roughness as halves.
- **The hit table.** Once anything traces, `build_scene` writes a `Hit_Instance` for each slot up to the scene's highest, and a `Hit_Submesh` for each of its submeshes' draw items, but not the impostors': the submesh's primitives, from its first index over 3, its material's block in the material table, and its bin. A mesh's structure is one geometry of all its triangles, so a hit's primitive index is the mesh's, and the submesh is the one whose range holds it.
- **Bins.** Bin 0 is the fallback's (`hit_default.hlsl`), a grey surface with the triangle's normal. Each material pipeline's hit pass has the bin after the pipeline's index, so at most `MAX_HIT_BINS` (257). A transparent or additive material's submesh takes bin 0 with `HIT_TRANSLUCENT`, and so does one whose material has no hit pass yet, while it compiles or in a blob from before the pass. Drawing never waits for a hit pass. Only an instance with a submesh outside bin 0 by its blend has `RAY_MASK_SURFACES`.
- **The binning** (`hit_bin.hlsl`) runs in four passes, a dispatch each: clear the bins' counts, count each ray's hit in its bin after finding its submesh, give each bin its first ray and its dispatch's arguments in one thread, and scatter each ray into its bin's range.
- **The shading** is one pass. For each bin the frame's table uses, and always bin 0, it binds the bin's pipeline, sets the camera's frame constants, pushes the bin's `Hit_Push`, and dispatches indirectly by the bin's count, `HIT_GROUP_SIZE` (64) rays to a group.
- **The hit pass** (`material_hit.hlsli`, the `cs_hit` entry): it reads the ray's three corners from the mesh's buffers (`read_vertex`), interpolates them, and places them by the instance's pose. Then it builds the `Material_Inputs`, with the view back along the ray, front-facing from the hit, and a two-sided material's normal turned toward the ray, loads the block, and calls `evaluate`. The albedo is the shading model's `shade_indirect` from a white surrounding with no specular light, `base_color * (1 - metallic)` for `Lit`; the emission is `shade_emission`; and the roughness is the model's `roughness` input, or 1. A model without lighting gives its emission only. A masked material's hole sets `HIT_SURFACE_CUTOUT`, but its ray stops there anyway, because the structures are opaque.
- **Texture levels.** A hit has no derivatives, so `cn_sample` and `cn_sample_grad` take one level: the ray cone's (Akenine-Möller et al., [*Texture Level of Detail Strategies for Real-Time Ray Tracing*](https://link.springer.com/chapter/10.1007/978-1-4842-4427-2_20), Ray Tracing Gems, 2019), half the log of the triangle's UV area over its area in meters, plus the log of the cone's width over the cosine of the ray's angle to the triangle, plus half the log of the texture's texel count. A cone of width 0 samples the finest level. Parallax gives the UV as it is. A material's own HLSL that calls `Sample` or `ddx` compiles at hits, but its derivatives are its neighbouring rays', which mean nothing; materials sample through `cn_sample` (see [Materials](materials.md#materials)).
- **Batches.** A frame shades at most `MAX_HIT_BATCHES` (4) clients' batches. Each has its own buffers in `Ray_Scene.scratch`, which grow to its rays. `add_hit_shading` is false, and adds nothing, without a request, a hit table, the camera's frame constants, or the binning's and the fallback's pipelines, which compile only with `Ray_Query`.
- **Queues.** A client chooses the queue; the binning and the shading run there, after its trace pass.
