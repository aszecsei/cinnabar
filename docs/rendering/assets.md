# Assets and components

How the renderer gets GPU resources for the assets it draws, how components refer to assets, and the renderer's own components.

**In this document:** [GPU residency](#gpu-residency) · [Mesh pages](#mesh-pages) · [Level 0](#level-0) · [Asset fields](#asset-fields) · [Components](#components)

The renderer draws shader, texture and mesh assets, which the cooker makes (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/shaders.md#shaders)).

## GPU residency

The asset runtime keeps blobs on the CPU. The renderer's residency table, in `Renderer`, gives the GPU resources of the assets that it draws.

- **By GUID.** `render.resident_texture(&residency, guid)`, `render.resident_mesh(&residency, guid)` and `render.resident_instance_set(&residency, guid)` return the GPU handles and whether they are ready. The first call loads the asset, creates its resources and streams its data with `gpu_stream_texture` and `gpu_stream_buffer` (see [GPU](../gpu/queues-streaming-readback.md#streaming)). Each later call is a lookup.
- **Until a resource is ready**, a texture gives the default for its usage (white, a flat normal, black) and a mesh draws nothing. Nothing waits for a stream.
- **Cube maps and arrays:** the table creates a texture as its blob says: 2D, a cube map or an array, each with a read view of that type (see [Textures](../content/kinds/textures.md#cube-maps-and-arrays)). `resident_texture` takes the dimension that its reader wants, 2D by default, since a shader that reads a texture through another type is undefined. A texture of another dimension is never ready, so its reader draws its default, and it logs that once for each version of the texture. `resident_texture_of` takes either of two dimensions, for a reader such as the [Texture sky](sky.md#texture) that handles both. Materials, sprites, particles and surfaces read 2D textures.
- **Hot reload.** When `asset.poll` swaps a blob, the table streams the new data into new resources, and switches to them only when their transfers are done, so the old version draws until then. Then it destroys the old resources.
- **Shaders** are not in the table. A `render.Shader_Pipeline` names its shader by the GUID in the shader's `.meta` file. `render.sync_graphics_pipeline`, `sync_compute_pipeline` and `sync_ray_tracing_pipeline`, called each frame, load the shader, create the pipeline from the blob's entries, and update it when `asset.poll` swaps the blob. On a code reload, `force` updates every pipeline with the new descriptions. A shader change therefore needs no code reload: the daemon cooks it, and the next frame's pipeline compiles from it.
- **Lifetime.** Resident resources have the `Game` scope. A soft restart destroys them with the old game, and loads the assets again, as it loads every asset again (see [Content pipeline](../content/blobs-and-loading.md#loading)).
- **The table** is `render.Residency`: a fixed array of `MAX_RESIDENT` (4,096) entries in `Game_State`, found by GUID and kind through a map. `residency_update`, once a frame before drawing, swaps in what finished streaming and starts streaming each asset whose blob `asset.poll` swapped. A mesh keeps its position, attribute and index buffers, its meshlets' three buffers (the records, their vertices and their triangles, see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)), its meshlet nodes for a mesh that has them, its skin stream for a skinned mesh (see [GPU scene](gpu-scene.md#skinned-meshes)), and the index, meshlet and node ranges of its first `MAX_SUBMESHES` submeshes; 16-bit indices are padded to whole words. It is ready when every buffer has streamed in: six, and one more each for nodes and a skin. **A mesh with pages has none of the six:** its meshlets are in the pool of [mesh pages](#mesh-pages), it is ready when its root pages are there, and its positions, attributes and indices are its [level 0](#level-0), which comes when something asks. An instance set keeps its instances and its cells, each a buffer (see [GPU scene](gpu-scene.md#instanced-meshes)).
- **Failures:** an asset that is not cooked, or whose blob is not of its kind, is logged once and tried again every `RESIDENT_RETRY_FRAMES` frames, or when a newer blob arrives.
- **Unused assets** stay resident. The pages of a paged mesh do not: the least recently used leave when the pool is full (see [Mesh pages](#mesh-pages)).
- **Acceleration structures:** with `Ray_Query` (`Residency.accels`), a mesh that becomes ready gets a bottom-level acceleration structure of all its triangles, a paged mesh when its [level 0](#level-0) is in, which the renderer builds the next frame that a feature traces (`unbuilt_accels`, see [Ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene)).
- **Live meshes:** `render.set_live(&residency, guid, blob)` makes a mesh blob in memory resident under a GUID in place of the asset's blob, and `clear_live` lets the asset stream again, whatever its version. A live blob streams and swaps in as an asset's does; one that arrives while another streams waits for it, and the newest wins. The editor's mesh files draw this way while they are edited (see [Mesh editing](../editor/mesh-editing.md#live-meshes)), baked by the game with `format.mesh_bake` and cut into level-0 meshlets by `render.build_simple_meshlets` (`meshlets.odin`): meshlets of at most 64 vertices and 124 triangles in index order, each with its sphere and a normal cone as conservative as meshoptimizer's, with an error of 0 and no parent, so the cluster LOD selection always takes them. A live mesh gets an acceleration structure as any mesh does, built again after each step.

## Mesh pages

A mesh cooked with pages (see [Content: Pages](../content/kinds/meshes.md#pages)) is not resident whole. Its head is in memory, and of its pages only the ones that a view's levels of detail need are on the GPU, in one pool under a budget. The code is `mesh_pages.odin`, after Nanite's streaming.

- **The pool** is one buffer of slots of `MESH_PAGE_SIZE`, 128 KiB, each of which holds one page as the cooker wrote it. Its size is the budget `Quality.mesh_pool`, in MiB (see [Quality](quality.md#quality)). When the budget changes, the pool is made again and every page leaves, so the roots come back in the frames after, and meshes draw nothing until theirs is in. Until a frame that executed has copied the table for the new pool, `resident_mesh` gives no paged mesh as ready (`Table_Sync`): the GPU's table still names slots of the pool that went.
- **The page table** is one buffer of an entry for each page of each resident paged mesh, `MAX_MESH_PAGES` (65,536) in all: the page's slot, or `MESH_PAGE_ABSENT`, and the page's first meshlet in its mesh. A mesh has a range of the table for as long as it is resident, which its `Mesh_Record` names (`pages`, `page_count`, with `MESH_RECORD_PAGED`). The table's entry of a page is the page's name on both sides: shaders read the slot from it, and the feedback has a word for it. When a mesh goes, its range waits `MESH_TABLE_COOL_FRAMES` (8) frames before another mesh takes it, in which the feedback of the frames that drew it comes back.
- **A page's state** is one union with one writer, `change_page`: absent, `Page_Reading` while the host reads its block from the blob's file (see [Core: File reads](../core/platform.md#file-reads)), `Page_Copying` in the frame that copies it into a slot, and `Page_Resident`. The writer keeps what follows from the state with it: the slot's owner, the table's entry, the count of open reads and each dependency's count of dependents.
- **Feedback.** The selection ([Mesh shader path](gpu-scene.md#mesh-shader-path)) writes one word for each table entry: 1 for a resident page that it lists meshlets from, and for an absent page whose group it wants, the group's projected error as a float's bits, raised with `InterlockedMax`. So a page is listed once however many groups, instances and views want it, at the most that any of them does. Every view's walk writes it, the extra views' too. The next frame's own view copies it to a readback ring before it zeroes it, and the CPU reads it a frame or two after that.
- **Each frame**, `mesh_pool_update` reads the feedback: a resident page that a view used is stamped with the frame, and an absent page that a view wants is listed with its priority. Every root is listed above everything else, always. It sorts the list by priority, ends the reads of pages that nothing has wanted for `MESH_PAGE_FORGET_FRAMES` (30) frames, and starts reads for the most wanted, at most `MESH_PAGE_READS` (32) open at once. A read is not started while the blob's file has another time than the head had: the cooker wrote it again, and the new version streams.
- **Loading.** `add_stream_pages_pass`, the first pass of the frame's own view, takes from the list the pages whose blocks are at hand, most wanted first, at most `MESH_PAGE_LOADS_PER_FRAME` (16, so 2 MiB): a root's block is in the head, and another page's is its finished read. It checks the block's `fnv32a` against the table's. A page loads only when every page that it depends on has a slot. A dependency is wanted at least as much as its dependents, because its group's error is larger, so it sorts first and loads first, in the same frame or an earlier one. The blocks are decompressed with LZ4 on the job threads, each straight into the frame's upload ring, and checked with `format.mesh_page_view`. A page whose bytes are wrong is logged once for its mesh, and the mesh asks for no more pages until its next version.
- **The copy is a graph pass, not a stream on the copy queue.** The pass copies the pages from the upload ring into their slots, the table's changed entries, and zeros over the feedback. The pool is one buffer that the frame's draws read while new pages are written into it, and the copy queue is not ordered with the graphics queue for parts of one buffer. In a pass, a page and its table entry change at one point of the frame, before every pass that reads them, so a slot can be given to another page in the frame that it becomes free. The pass's commit, which runs only when the frame executes, makes the pages resident ([Render graph: Commits](../gpu/render-graph.md#commits)); when the frame does not execute, the next update gives the slots back and lists the table's entries again, and the reads are still open with their blocks.
- **Eviction.** A page takes a free slot. When there is none, the resident page that no view has used for the longest leaves for it, least recently used first, but never a root, a page that a page with a slot depends on, a page of a pinned mesh, or a page that a view used in the last `MESH_PAGE_KEEP_FRAMES` (4) frames. When nothing may leave, the load waits, so a pool that is too small for a view keeps the view's coarser pages and does not trade its pages for each other each frame. A page that a view uses does not leave for a page that is wanted more: the feedback has no priority for a page that is resident.
- **The rule that keeps the cut whole** is the dependencies' (see [Content: The cut with pages missing](../content/kinds/meshes.md#the-cut-with-pages-missing)): a page has a slot only while every page that it depends on has one. Loading and eviction both keep it, and the pool's tests check it after every frame.
- **Ready.** A paged mesh is ready when its roots are resident, which takes two frames from its first use with nothing else loading. It has no meshlet buffers of its own, and no positions, attributes or indices until something asks for its [level 0](#level-0).
- **Pinned meshes.** What draws a mesh's meshlets without the selection tests every meshlet of it, and so cannot say which pages it wants: an [instanced mesh](gpu-scene.md#instanced-meshes) and the view model. `resident_pin` keeps every page of such a mesh for the frame and asks for the absent ones at the highest priority below the roots'.
- **Hot reload and live meshes.** A new version of a paged mesh takes a range of its own and streams its roots beside the old version, which draws until they are in. Then the old version's reads end and its slots are free. A blob that is swapped again while a version's roots are still loading ends that version's stream at once, as its roots were in the head that went, and the newest streams. A stream whose root page is wrong fails, and takes with it a live mesh that was let go, which must not go on drawing. A live mesh is never paged: a blob from memory has no file to read pages from.
- **Stats.** `render.mesh_stream_stats` gives the resident pages, the slots, the pages that are wanted and not resident, the bytes that the last frame copied and the bytes that every frame has. The editor's Resource Monitor shows them, the bytes as a rate.
- **Asking for dependencies.** The walk asks for a page by the groups of it whose errors show. A page depends on pages for all of its groups, some of which no view asks for, so `mesh_pool_update` lists what each wanted page depends on at the page's priority, and what that depends on.
- **Limits:** a mesh that does not fit in the table's free range is not made resident, which is logged. The pool's roots are not counted against the budget before they load: when the roots alone are more than the pool, some meshes wait for a slot that never comes. Textures are not streamed this way.

## Level 0

The paths that do not draw meshlets from the pool read a mesh's own triangles as whole buffers: vertex pulling, which every transparent material, every device without mesh shaders, the previews, the thumbnails and the mesh particles use; the acceleration structures of the [ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene); and the shading of a ray's hit. **The decision: a paged mesh has these buffers only when one of those paths asks, and they are assembled from its pages.** The blob does not store them, so nothing is on disk twice, and a mesh that only the mesh shader path draws has only its pages in memory. The code is `mesh_level0.odin`.

- **Asking.** `resident_level0(&residency, mesh)` is whether the mesh's positions, attributes and indices are resident, which a mesh without pages always has. For a paged mesh the first call starts the assembly, and the caller draws nothing of the mesh until it is true. `resident_whole_mesh` is `resident_mesh` with that. The scene's items that pull vertices ask, as do the previews and the mesh particles. While meshes have acceleration structures (`Residency.accels`), the residency asks for every paged mesh as soon as it is ready.
- **Assembling.** Level 0 is the positions and attributes of every page with level 0 meshlets, one page after another, and indices from those meshlets' triangles (`format.mesh_page_level0`). The cooker wrote the blob's vertex and index counts and each submesh's index range for that order. The pages come in order: a root's block from the head, and the others from the file, the pages that follow each other in one read of at most `LEVEL0_READ_SIZE` (8 MiB). A frame decompresses at most `LEVEL0_PAGES_PER_FRAME` (32) of them for all meshes together. When every page is in, the three buffers stream on the copy queue, as a mesh without pages streams its own, with 16-bit indices for a mesh of at most 65,536 vertices.
- **Its state** is one union with one writer, `change_level0`: not asked for, `Level0_Assembling` with the arrays on the heap and the open read, `Level0_Streaming` with the buffers, and `Level0_Ready`, when the buffers are the mesh's and its acceleration structure is made.
- **Its cost.** A vertex that two pages share is in level 0 twice. Level 0 is held beside the pool: with ray queries on, every paged mesh has its level 0 and the pages that the view needs. No page comes into the pool for it.
- **Failures:** a page that cannot be read, or that is not the page that the table names, is logged once, and the mesh's level 0 is tried again after `RESIDENT_RETRY_FRAMES` frames.

## Asset fields

A component refers to an asset by GUID, in a `format.Guid` field with an `asset` tag that names the kind as the cook link does, in lowercase: ``mesh: format.Guid `asset:"mesh"` ``. A field without the tag takes any asset.

- **Field kind:** the field walker (`scene.component_fields`) gives such a field the `Asset` kind and its `asset_kind`, and the editor link's `Field_Info` carries both. A fixed array of asset references, such as ``materials: [8]format.Guid `asset:"material"` ``, is a field for each element, named `materials.0` and on.
- **Scenes** write the value as `$` and 32 hex digits, or `$0` for none (see [Editor](../editor/document-and-scenes.md#scenes)), and the loader writes the GUID into the field. A component holds the GUID, never a loaded asset: the renderer resolves GUIDs through its residency table.
- **Missing assets:** the cooker warns when a scene names a GUID that no asset or sub-asset in the content has, as it does for a missing script. Field types exist only in the game DLL, so the cooker cannot check the kind; the editor offers and accepts only assets of the field's kind.
- **The properties panel** shows the asset's name, picks from the assets of the field's kind, and accepts one dragged from the content browser (see [Editor](../editor/shell-and-panels.md#properties)).
- **Scripts:** a behaviour declares an asset field of its own with `Field.asset` (see [Scripting: Values](../scripting/api.md#values)). `script.expose` skips a component's asset fields, as it skips any field type that it does not know.

## Components

- **`Camera`** (stable ID 32): the vertical field of view in radians and the near plane in meters; 0 in either means 60° or 5 cm. Also the view model's field of view and near plane, 0 for the camera's own (see [View model](layers-and-output.md#view-model)). `projection` makes it orthographic, with `orthographic_height` and `far` in meters (see [Orthographic views](frame.md#orthographic-views)). The camera's pose is its entity's presented `Transform`, and `render.camera_view` gives the view. `Game_State` holds the active camera's entity, as world-wide state (see [World](../world/README.md)). The game's view comes from it, unless an editor is linked (see [Editor](../editor/viewport.md#viewport)).
- **`Mesh_Renderer`** (stable ID 33): a mesh, a material or instance for each of its mesh's first `MAX_MATERIAL_SLOTS` (8) submeshes (`materials.0` and on), and whether it casts shadows. `render.register_components` registers both. The renderer draws every `Mesh_Renderer` through the [GPU scene](gpu-scene.md#gpu-scene).
- **`Directional_Light`** (stable ID 34): a color, an intensity, whether it casts shadows, and its disk's angular diameter in degrees (`DEFAULT_SUN_DISC`, 0.53°, for the sun). It shines along its entity's +z.
- **`Point_Light`** (35): a color, an intensity, a range in meters, whether it casts shadows, and its bulb's source radius in meters.
- **`Spot_Light`** (36): a point light in a cone along its entity's +z, with inner and outer half angles in radians.
- **A light's size** is for its [soft shadows](lighting-and-shadows.md#soft-shadows): an angular diameter or a source radius of 0 is a point. It does not change the light's shading.
- **`Quad_Light`** (39): a rectangle of light, a width and a height in meters along its entity's x and y, that shines along its +z, or from both faces when it is two-sided. Also a color, an intensity, a range beyond its shape, and whether it casts shadows (see [Area lights](lighting-and-shadows.md#area-lights)).
- **`Disk_Light`** (40): a disk of light, a width and a height across along its entity's x and y, so an ellipse when they differ. Otherwise as `Quad_Light`.
- **`Line_Light`** (41): a tube of light along its entity's x, with a length and a radius in meters; a radius of 0 is an ideal line. Also a color, an intensity, a range beyond its shape, and whether it casts shadows.
- **Defaults:** a light's color of 0 means white, a range of 0 means `DEFAULT_LIGHT_RANGE` (10 m), an outer angle of 0 means `DEFAULT_SPOT_OUTER` (45°), and an inner angle of 0 means the outer angle. An area light's width, height or length of 0 means `DEFAULT_AREA_SIZE` (1 m). An intensity of 0 is a light that is off.
- **`View_Model`** (37): a tag that makes a `Mesh_Renderer` part of the view model (see [View model](layers-and-output.md#view-model)).
- **`Surface_Renderer`** (51): a patch surface's size, material, hole mask, finest cell, height range and whether it casts shadows (see [Surfaces](surfaces.md#surfaces)).
- **`Particle_System`** (52): an effect, whether it is paused, and a scale of its rates (see [Particles](particles.md#systems)).
- **`Sprite_Renderer`** (56): a sprite sheet, the frame or the animation of it that shows, a color, a layer and an order, and whether it is lit and snapped to pixels (see [2D](2d.md#sprite-renderers)).
- **`Reflection_Probe`** (54): an influence box, its fade, a projection box, a priority, an intensity and when it captures again (see [Reflection probes](lighting-and-shadows.md#reflection-probes)).
- **The settings components** (38, 43 to 50 and 55), which volumes carry (see [Settings](frame.md#settings)).
