# Ambient occlusion

The ambient occlusion is ground-truth ambient occlusion (GTAO, after Jimenez et al.), built as Intel's [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO) builds it: three passes on the compute queue from the prepass's depth and normals, and no history (`lib/engine/render/ambient_occlusion.odin`). The `Ambient_Occlusion` settings set it (see [Settings](frame.md#settings)). Without its pipelines, or with `off`, it adds no passes, and the forward pass reads a white texture.

- **The depth** (`gtao_depth.hlsl`) is the view depth, and four levels below it, each half the size of the one before (`AO_DEPTH_LEVELS`, `R32_Float`), in one dispatch whose groups cover 16x16 pixels. A level's texel averages the four below it, each weighted by how near it is to the farthest of them, so it takes the surface behind rather than a thin one in front. The view model's pixels store their depth negated, from its near plane, and a texel takes the world's depths over the view model's unless all four are the view model's.
- **The search** (`gtao.hlsl`) looks along nine slices through each pixel's view direction, three steps on each side (XeGTAO's Ultra), for the highest horizon within `radius` (`DEFAULT_AO_RADIUS`, 1 m), and integrates the cosine-weighted visible arc between the two horizons. Occluders fade out over the last 61.5% of the radius. A step `d` pixels out reads the depth level `log2(d) - 3.3`, so a far step reads a texel that stands for the area around it instead of one pixel that a thin edge can hit or miss. The search reaches at most 256 pixels, and where that cuts it short the radius shrinks with it, so the occlusion still fades out by the search's end and does not stop at a hard edge. The visibility is raised to `power` (`DEFAULT_AO_POWER`, 2.2), and never falls below 0.03. Each pixel's estimate is noisy around its true value, above 1 too, so it is not clamped until the forward pass reads it.
- **The slices turn evenly around the view direction.** Slices spaced evenly in screen angle, as XeGTAO places them, are uneven around the view direction away from the screen's center. An open plane then looks a few percent occluded near the screen's bottom, and brighter than open at its sides. So each slice's direction turns around the view direction, and is then projected to the screen.
- **Noise.** The slices' angle and the steps' offset are the two dimensions of one point of the R2 sequence, at the pixel's place along a Hilbert curve through a 64-pixel tile, so the two are independent and spread evenly. With temporal AA, each frame moves every pixel 288 places along the sequence, and temporal AA averages the noise. Without it, as in the debug views, the noise stays where it is, so it does not crawl.
- **Positions** come from the depth with the camera's jitter, which the depth was drawn with, so a slope does not seem to bend. The view model's pixels use its projection (see [View model](layers-and-output.md#view-model)), and a step from the world onto the view model or back finds nothing.
- **Edges.** The search also writes how much each pixel's four neighbors are on its surface (`R8_Unorm`, two bits each): by each neighbor's difference in depth from the pixel, or from the plane through the pixel and the opposite neighbor if that is smaller, so a slope is not an edge. The view model and the world are never on one surface.
- **The denoiser** (`gtao_denoise.hlsl`) averages each pixel's 3x3 neighbors, each weighed by its edge, which counts only as much as the pixel's edge does for the neighbor, so the blur is the same both ways. A pixel with three or four edges lets its neighbors leak in a little, so it is not left with its own noise. It runs once with temporal AA and twice without it; the passes before the last weigh the pixel's own value less.
- **No history.** An occlusion history of its own would have to be found again after each move, and every way of finding it failed somewhere: the nearest texel turned it into blocks, a bilinear one smeared along correlated noise, and a depth test rejected it on surfaces seen edge-on in chevrons. Temporal AA already finds the history of the lit scene.
- **The forward pass** scales the indirect light, diffuse and specular, by the occlusion. Direct light is not occluded.
- With [global illumination](global-illumination.md#global-illumination), which finds the larger occluders, the ambient occlusion still scales its light, for the contact shadows that the probes are too coarse for.
- *Later:* specular occlusion from the roughness, and bent normals.
