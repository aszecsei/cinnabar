# Ambient occlusion

The ambient occlusion is ground-truth ambient occlusion (GTAO, after Jimenez et al.), built as Intel's [XeGTAO](https://github.com/GameTechDev/XeGTAO) builds it: three passes on the compute queue from the prepass's depth and normals, and no history (`lib/engine/render/ambient_occlusion.odin`). The [quality](quality.md#quality) can choose HBAO or SSAO in GTAO's place, and only the middle pass, the search, changes (see [Techniques](#techniques)). The `Ambient_Occlusion` settings set it (see [Settings](frame.md#settings)). Without its pipelines, with `off`, or with the quality's technique `Off`, it adds no passes, and the forward pass reads a white texture.

- **The depth** (`gtao_depth.hlsl`) is the view depth, and four levels below it, each half the size of the one before (`AO_DEPTH_LEVELS`, `R32_Float`), in one dispatch whose groups cover 16x16 pixels. A level's texel averages the four below it, each weighted by how near it is to the farthest of them, so it takes the surface behind rather than a thin one in front. The view model's pixels store their depth negated, from its near plane, and a texel takes the world's depths over the view model's unless all four are the view model's.
- **The search** (`gtao.hlsl`) looks along the [quality](quality.md#levels)'s slices through each pixel's view direction, with its steps on each side (`GTAO_PARAMS`, XeGTAO's presets: from one slice of two steps at Low to nine of three at Ultra), for the highest horizon within `radius` (`DEFAULT_AO_RADIUS`, 1 m), and integrates the cosine-weighted visible arc between the two horizons. Occluders fade out over the last 61.5% of the radius. A step `d` pixels out reads the depth level `log2(d) - 3.3`, so a far step reads a texel that stands for the area around it instead of one pixel that a thin edge can hit or miss. The search reaches at most 256 pixels, and where that cuts it short the radius shrinks with it, so the occlusion still fades out by the search's end and does not stop at a hard edge. The visibility is raised to `power` (`DEFAULT_AO_POWER`, 2.2), and never falls below 0.03. Each pixel's estimate is noisy around its true value, above 1 too, so it is not clamped until the forward pass reads it.
- **The slices turn evenly around the view direction.** Slices spaced evenly in screen angle, as XeGTAO places them, are uneven around the view direction away from the screen's center. An open plane then looks a few percent occluded near the screen's bottom, and brighter than open at its sides. So each slice's direction turns around the view direction, and is then projected to the screen.
- **Noise.** The slices' angle and the steps' offset are the two dimensions of one point of the R2 sequence, at the pixel's place along a Hilbert curve through a 64-pixel tile, so the two are independent and spread evenly. With temporal AA, each frame moves every pixel 288 places along the sequence, and temporal AA averages the noise. Without it, as in the debug views, the noise stays where it is, so it does not crawl.
- **Positions** come from the depth with the camera's jitter, which the depth was drawn with, so a slope does not seem to bend. The view model's pixels use its projection (see [View model](layers-and-output.md#view-model)), and a step from the world onto the view model or back finds nothing.
- **Edges.** The search also writes how much each pixel's four neighbors are on its surface (`R8_Unorm`, two bits each): by each neighbor's difference in depth from the pixel, or from the plane through the pixel and the opposite neighbor if that is smaller, so a slope is not an edge. The view model and the world are never on one surface.
- **The denoiser** (`gtao_denoise.hlsl`) averages each pixel's 3x3 neighbors, each weighed by its edge, which counts only as much as the pixel's edge does for the neighbor, so the blur is the same both ways. A pixel with three or four edges lets its neighbors leak in a little, so it is not left with its own noise. It runs once with temporal AA and twice without it; the passes before the last weigh the pixel's own value less.
- **No history.** An occlusion history of its own would have to be found again after each move, and every way of finding it failed somewhere: the nearest texel turned it into blocks, a bilinear one smeared along correlated noise, and a depth test rejected it on surfaces seen edge-on in chevrons. Temporal AA already finds the history of the lit scene.
- **The forward pass** scales the indirect light, diffuse and specular, by the occlusion. Direct light is not occluded.
- With [global illumination](global-illumination.md#global-illumination), which finds the larger occluders, the ambient occlusion still scales its light, for the contact shadows that the probes are too coarse for.
- *Later:* specular occlusion from the roughness, and bent normals.

## Techniques

`Ao_Technique` chooses the search. Every technique reads the same depth levels and normals, takes its noise from the same R2 point (`Ao_Pixel.noise`), writes the same edges, and is blurred by the same denoiser. So they differ only in how they find the occlusion, and the `radius` and `power` settings mean the same in each. What they share is in `ao_common.hlsli`: `ao_begin` readies a pixel and writes its edges, `ao_sample` reads a step's surface, `ao_falloff` fades occluders toward the radius's end, and `ao_end` writes the visibility raised to the power.

| Technique | Shader | Search | `samples` × `steps` by level (`AO_PARAMS`) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `Gtao` | `gtao.hlsl` | Slices around the view direction; integrates the cosine-weighted visible arc between the highest horizon on each side | 1×2, 2×2, 3×3, 9×3 |
| `Hbao` | `hbao.hlsl` | Directions on the screen; along each, every step that rises above the highest elevation so far occludes by how much it rises | 4×3, 4×4, 6×4, 8×6 |
| `Ssao` | `ssao.hlsl` | Points in the hemisphere over the surface; a point with a surface in front of it is occluded | 6, 8, 12, 16 points |

- **GTAO** is the most faithful: it integrates what a cosine-weighted hemisphere sees, so an open surface is 1 and a corner is as dark as it should be. It is the presets' choice.
- **HBAO** (`hbao.hlsl`), after Bavoil, Sainz and Dimitrov 2008, is the engine's own search, not NVIDIA's HBAO+ library, so it runs on every vendor and cooks like every other shader. Each direction turns by the noise, and each step moves within its gap by it. The elevation is measured from the surface's tangent plane, from the prepass's normal, raised by `TANGENT_BIAS` (0.1, as a sine) so the depth's steps on a flat surface do not occlude it. It counts the horizon's rise, not the visible arc, so it is a little stronger than GTAO in corners.
- **SSAO** (`ssao.hlsl`), a normal-oriented hemisphere after Crytek's (Mittring 2007), is the cheapest: one depth read a point, no march. Its points are a cosine-weighted Fibonacci spiral turned by the noise, nearer the pixel more often than not. A point is occluded when the depth buffer has a surface at least `DEPTH_BIAS` (0.3%) of its depth in front of it, and counts by that surface's falloff from the pixel, so a far wall behind a thin object does not occlude. It counts points, not how high the horizon rises, so it is softer and weaker than the other two.
- **Choosing:** every technique's pipeline is synced whether or not the frame uses it, so the quality can switch between them from one frame to the next. A technique whose pipeline is not compiled yet leaves the frame without ambient occlusion until it is.
