# GPU scene

The persistent buffer of every instance's pose, the render origin it is relative to, stable slots, culling into bins, the acceleration structure, and the vertex paths beside vertex pulling: the mesh shader path, billboards and impostors. Read [Render extraction](../world/render-extraction.md) first.

**In this document:** [Mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path) · [Billboards](#billboards) · [Impostors](#impostors)

The GPU scene holds every instance's record in a persistent buffer, `Gpu_Scene` in `lib/engine/render/scene.odin`: its pose and its last frame's pose as the rows of 3x4 matrices, and its entity's ID for the editor. Each frame's draw items carry the rest: the mesh, the submesh's indices and bounds, and the material.

- **Render origin.** Poses are relative to the render origin, a `World_Position` snapped to a grid of 1024 m. Snapping is an integer mask on the Q39.24 values, so the offset between two origins is exact. Shaders subtract the view's offset from the origin, so their error near the camera is about one `f32` unit at 1024 m, about 0.06 mm.
- **Rebasing.** The origin moves when the camera is more than 1.5 cells from it, so a camera on a cell's edge does not move it back and forth. A rebase needs no step of its own: every frame writes every record, last frame's pose included, relative to the current origin (see Updates below), and builds the top-level acceleration structure again when it traces.
- **Stable slots.** Each rendered entity has a slot for its whole life, at most `MAX_INSTANCES` (16 384). A free list gives slots, and a map from entity to slot, in host memory, lives in the `Renderer`. A slot whose entity has no `Mesh_Renderer` in a frame is free again. A soft restart builds them again. Culling and the acceleration structure's instance IDs index by slot.
- **Camera-relative records.** The view model's instances have slots in the GPU scene, but their rows are relative to the camera, and last frame's relative to last frame's camera, not to the render origin. The view model is within a meter of the camera, where the render origin's error would show as about a pixel of jitter at the near plane. The debug shapes are not in the GPU scene; the debug layer writes them relative to the camera every frame.
- **Updates.** Every frame writes every slot, relative to the render origin, into the upload ring, and a copy moves them into the persistent buffer: 112 bytes per instance with both poses. Last frame's pose is the slot's presented pose of the frame before, or this frame's for a new slot. Every GPU-side consumer has its final layout.
- **Draw items** are one per submesh of each visible `Mesh_Renderer` whose mesh is resident: its slot, its mesh's record, its indices, or on the [mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path) its meshlets, its material's block, its bin, and its submesh's bounding sphere. A submesh of a mesh with an [impostor](#impostors) has a second item, its impostor's. Hidden entities (`Frame_Desc.hidden`) keep their slots but have no items.
- **Culling** is a compute pass, `cull_draws.hlsl`, with a row of the dispatch for each view: the camera (`VIEW_CAMERA`), the view model (`VIEW_VIEW_MODEL`), then each shadow view (see [Shadows](lighting-and-shadows.md#shadows)). It tests each item's sphere, moved by its instance's rows and the view's offset from their space and scaled by the largest scale of its axes, the rows' columns' lengths, and grown by the material's displacement bound (see [Materials](materials.md#vertex-displacement)), against the view's six planes (a [billboard](#billboards)'s sphere is one about its instance's position); the camera's and the view model's far planes are at infinity. A shadow view takes only the items that cast shadows (the `Mesh_Renderer`'s `cast_shadows` and the material's), the view model's view only the view model's items, and every other view leaves those out. A visible item takes the next place in its bin of that view with an atomic add, and writes there both its indirect draw, whose draw ID is that place, and its draw record. An item on the mesh shader path writes a mesh draw, in a buffer of its own, since a mesh draw's record is shorter. Each view has room for every item, and each bin has the same range in every view. An item with an impostor, and the impostor's item, first test which of the two the camera draws (see [Impostors](#impostors)). Culling is by frustum only, on the graphics queue.
- **Drawing:** the prepass, the shadow maps and the forward pass each draw a bin of their view with one `cmd_draw_indirect` and the bin's count, with the bin's material's pipeline for their pass. The vertex shader reads its draw record by its draw ID, and pulls its index from the mesh's index buffer, then its vertex: every mesh has buffers of its own, so no one index buffer could be bound for a bin. On the mesh shader path, an opaque bin draws with one `cmd_draw_mesh_indirect` instead.
- **The frame's constants** (`Frame_Constants`, the pass constants of every material pass) hold the view's projection, with the camera's jitter for the camera's and the view model's copies, and last frame's without it, the camera's offset from the origin and last frame's camera's, the camera's offset from the origin again as `origin_offset`, which the view model's copy keeps where it zeroes the others, the ambient light, the cascades' splits, the records' bindless indices and their offsets in the upload ring (mesh records, material table, lights, shadow views), the clusters' grid, the jitter, the exposure buffer, which the forward pass scales its colors by, whether there is fog, with the fog volume's sampler and the share of it that the screen covers, and whether there is global illumination. Each shadow view has a copy with its own projection, which takes positions relative to the camera too, so the depth pass places vertices with the same code. The forward pass pushes the shadow atlas, the shadow mask, the ambient occlusion, the fog volume and the global illumination's diffuse and specular light (`Material_Push`), which exist only once the frame records. The fog's injection uses the camera's constants too, so a frame with fog writes them even when nothing draws.
- **The mesh shader path's constants:** each view's `Frame_Constants` has its frustum's planes and where it looks from, `cull_eye`, and the camera's level of detail, `lod_scale` and `lod_near` (see [Mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path)). Each has the camera's right and up too, and last frame's camera's, which [billboards](#billboards) face, and `Cull_Constants` has the camera's offset and level of detail, which pick between a mesh and its [impostor](#impostors).
- **The acceleration structure.** With `Ray_Query`, each instance whose `Mesh_Renderer` casts shadows is an instance of the GPU scene's top-level acceleration structure, in the render origin's space, with its slot as its ID. It is built again every frame that traces shadows.
- **Patch surfaces** (see [Surfaces](surfaces.md#surfaces)) have slots and records as meshes do, and one item each with `ITEM_SURFACE`, which `cull_draws.hlsl` skips. The item reserves `MAX_SURFACE_PATCHES` draws in its bin, so each view's draws are the bins' counts, not the items' (`Scene_Frame.draws_per_view`), and two more passes add the patches' draws: `select patches` after the scene's update, and `cull patches` after `cull draws`, both with the culling's constants.
- **Particles** (see [Particles](particles.md#ribbons)) have a slot at each system's anchor, and their items' draws are the GPU's: a ribbon's item has `ITEM_RIBBON`, and a mesh particle emitter's item of a submesh `ITEM_PARTICLES`, which `cull_draws.hlsl` skips, and whose draw draws the submesh once for each particle (see [Mesh particles](particles.md#mesh-particles)).

## Mesh shader path

With `Caps.mesh_shaders`, the opaque passes, the prepass, the shadow maps and the forward pass, draw meshlets with amplification and mesh shaders, and pick each part of each mesh's level of detail on the GPU. Without it, they pull vertices as above, at level 0, and so do the transparent passes and the material previews always. `CINNABAR_DISABLE_FEATURES=Mesh_Shaders` forces the fallback. The meshlets and their hierarchy are the cooker's (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)).

- **Pipelines.** An opaque material has mesh pipelines for the three passes beside its vertex ones, from its blob's `as_cluster` and `ms_*` entries (see [Materials](materials.md#permutations)), and draws on the path when the device has mesh shaders and the blob has all of them (`Material_Pipeline.mesh_path`). A pipeline cannot change between the kinds, so the two sets are apart. The previews draw with the vertex ones.
- **Culling.** An item of such a material has `ITEM_MESH_PATH`, and its first and count are its submesh's meshlets, of every level. The cull pass tests its submesh's sphere as before, and a visible one writes a mesh draw of an amplification group for each 32 of its meshlets (`MESHLETS_PER_GROUP`). The draw record has the count, so a group knows which of its threads have a meshlet.
- **Amplification shaders** (`material_mesh.hlsli`) test a meshlet a thread, and compact the meshlets that pass into the payload, 32 indices, with a group-shared counter, then launch a mesh group for each. A meshlet passes when:
  - **Its level is the cut's.** An error projects to `error × scale / max(distance − radius × scale, lod_near) × lod_scale` from the camera, where `scale` is the instance's largest axis scale. The meshlet draws when its own simplification's error projects to at most 1 and its group's to more. `lod_scale` is the camera's pixels per unit of error at a distance of 1, `0.5 × height / tan(fov / 2)`, over the error in pixels that may show, `Quality.lod_error` (see [Quality](quality.md#quality)). Every view has the camera's, the shadow maps' too, so a shadow is the shape of the surface that the camera sees, and the prepass and the forward pass draw the same meshlets, as their `Equal` depth test needs.
  - **Its sphere is in the view's frustum,** scaled as the item's is, and grown by the material's displacement bound, which the draw record carries.
  - **Its normal cone does not point away from the view.** From a point, the meshlet is culled when `dot(c − eye, axis) ≥ cutoff × length(c − eye) + radius`; from a direction, when `dot(direction, axis) ≥ cutoff`. The camera's views look from the camera, a spot or point light's shadow views from the light, and a cascade along the light's direction. The axis turns with the instance's rows. The test is skipped for a two-sided material, for a material that displaces its vertices, for an instance whose axes' scales differ by more than 1 %, which bends normals, and for a cone too wide to cull.
- **Mesh shaders** (`ms_forward`, `ms_prepass`, `ms_depth`), 128 threads, each write one of the meshlet's vertices and one of its triangles. They place a vertex with `place_vertex`, as vertex pulling does after it reads an index, so both paths give the same depths and the same velocities, and the pixel shaders are the same.
- **Debug views:** `Meshlets` colors each meshlet, and `Lod_Depth` colors each by its level (see [Debug views](post-processing.md#debug-views)). On the vertex-pulling path, every surface is one color, at level 0. An impostor is level 8, past every mesh's levels.
- **Shadows by ray query** trace each mesh's acceleration structure, which is level 0, so a distant surface's traced shadow may differ a little from its drawn level.

## Billboards

A billboard is a mesh that turns to face the camera: a material with `geometry = billboard` faces it wholly, and one with `geometry = billboard_axis` turns about the mesh's +y only, as a foliage card or a tree card does (see [Materials](materials.md#material)). Particles, sprites and foliage cards draw this way: a quad mesh, such as the sample's `meshes/card.mesh`, with a billboard material.

- **Placement.** `finish_vertex` (`material_vertex.hlsli`) turns the instance's rows with `billboard_rows` before it places a vertex: the mesh's +x goes along the camera's right, its +y along the camera's up, and its +z away from the camera, each axis with the instance's own scale, about the instance's position. A quad in the mesh's x and y fronting -z therefore faces the camera. An axis billboard keeps the rows' +y and turns about it until its +z points away from the camera; seen straight along its axis, it faces the camera's up. The normal and the tangent turn with the rows, so a billboard is lit as a surface facing the camera.
- **Both poses.** This frame's rows face this frame's camera, and last frame's rows last frame's camera, from `Frame_Constants.camera_right`, `camera_up`, `previous_camera_right` and `previous_camera_up`, so a billboard that turns as the camera turns has the velocity that temporal AA needs.
- **Every view faces the camera.** The shadow views' copies have the camera's axes and offset too, so a billboard's shadow is the shape that the camera sees, and the prepass and the forward pass place the same vertices. A preview's billboard faces its orbiting camera.
- **Culling.** An item of a billboard material has `ITEM_BILLBOARD`. Its sphere turns about the instance's position, so the culling tests the sphere about that position that holds it: its radius is the sphere's distance from the mesh's origin plus its radius, scaled.
- **A vertex path of its own.** A billboard draws with vertex pulling, the vertex path beside the mesh shader path: the cooker leaves out the mesh shader path's entries and the impostor path's, since a billboard is a few quads whose cone and levels would not hold once turned. Displacement works on it as on any mesh, after the turn.

## Impostors

An impostor is a mesh's octahedral impostor: views of the mesh from all around, which the cooker bakes (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#impostors)), and which the impostor path draws as one quad past the mesh's coarsest level of detail. Each pixel of the quad finds the surface in the atlas and evaluates the submesh's material there, so the impostor is textured, masked and lit by the scene's lights as the mesh is, and casts its shadow.

- **The atlas** is `IMPOSTOR_FRAMES` (8) by 8 frames, one for each direction on a grid of the octahedron, each an orthographic view of the mesh's bounding sphere, `impostor_size` texels a side. A texel holds the nearest surface's first UV set, its depth in the frame, its submesh and its normal and tangent in the mesh's space (`format.Impostor_Texel`). It is a buffer of the resident mesh (`Resident_Mesh.impostor`), which the mesh record names with the frames' size and the sphere.
- **Where it takes over.** The mesh's blob has `impostor_error`: the larger of the error of its coarsest level, which its top meshlets keep (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)), and one texel of a frame, in meters. The cull pass projects it from the camera as the mesh shader path projects a meshlet's error, from the mesh's sphere and the instance's largest scale, with the camera's `lod_scale`: where it is at most 1, past where the mesh's coarsest level and a texel are too small to show, the impostor draws, and nearer, the mesh. Every view takes the camera's choice, so a shadow is the shape that the camera sees.
- **Items.** `add_items` gives a submesh of a mesh with an impostor two items when its material has the impostor path and is opaque or masked: its own, with `ITEM_HAS_IMPOSTOR`, and its impostor's, with `ITEM_IMPOSTOR`, six vertices of a quad, whose sphere is the mesh's. Both carry the mesh's sphere and its error, and the cull pass draws exactly one. The view model's items have none. `sort_bins` puts a pipeline's impostors in a bin after its own (`Scene_Frame.bin_impostors`), which draws with vertex pulling and the material's impostor passes, `Impostor_Forward`, `Impostor_Prepass` and `Impostor_Depth`, in place of the opaque ones, also for a material on the mesh shader path.
- **The quad** (`material_impostor.hlsli`) faces the view from in front of the sphere, as wide as the sphere, so it covers the sphere's outline. A view looks from `cull_eye`: the camera, or a shadow view's light, or along a cascade's direction.
- **The pixel** follows its ray into the mesh's space through the inverse of the instance's rows. It picks one of the three frames nearest the direction that it is seen from, at random by their weights, from interleaved gradient noise that changes with the time, so temporal AA blends the views; the prepass and the forward pass make the same choice. The ray meets the frame's plane through the sphere's center, then the plane at the depth found there, twice, and the texel there is the surface. A texel without a surface, or of another submesh, discards the pixel: each submesh's quad draws its own texels with its own material.
- **What the material sees.** The point from the frame's texel and depth, the normal and tangent turned by the instance's rows, the first UV set, no second, and a white vertex color. The pixel writes its own depth, which the forward pass's `Equal` test matches, and the prepass its velocity from the instance's last pose. A shadow map's depth is one texel farther from the light, since a written depth takes no pipeline bias. The impostor has no displacement.
- **Limits.** The material's textures sample with the pixel's derivatives of the atlas's UV, which jump at a UV seam, where a lower mip shows. The views are the mesh at rest: a material's displacement and anything that changes with the instance, but its pose, are not in them. A transparent material's submesh draws its mesh at every distance.
- **The sample's** `scenes/billboards.scene`, which F7 in mygame cycles to, has sprites that face the camera, tree cards that turn about their +y, and a line of `meshes/tower.mesh` from 10 m to 800 m away, whose far towers draw as its impostor.
