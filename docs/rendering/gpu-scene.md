# GPU scene

The persistent buffer of every instance's pose, the render origin it is relative to, stable slots, culling into bins and the acceleration structure. Read [Render extraction](../world/render-extraction.md) first.

The GPU scene holds every instance's record in a persistent buffer, `Gpu_Scene` in `lib/engine/render/scene.odin`: its pose and its last frame's pose as the rows of 3x4 matrices, and its entity's ID for the editor. Each frame's draw items carry the rest: the mesh, the submesh's indices and bounds, and the material.

- **Render origin.** Poses are relative to the render origin, a `World_Position` snapped to a grid of 1024 m. Snapping is an integer mask on the Q39.24 values, so the offset between two origins is exact. Shaders subtract the view's offset from the origin, so their error near the camera is about one `f32` unit at 1024 m, about 0.06 mm.
- **Rebasing.** The origin moves when the camera is more than 1.5 cells from it, so a camera on a cell's edge does not move it back and forth. A rebase needs no step of its own: every frame writes every record, last frame's pose included, relative to the current origin (see Updates below), and builds the top-level acceleration structure again when it traces.
- **Stable slots.** Each rendered entity has a slot for its whole life, at most `MAX_INSTANCES` (16 384). A free list gives slots, and a map from entity to slot, in host memory, lives in the `Renderer`. A slot whose entity has no `Mesh_Renderer` in a frame is free again. A soft restart builds them again. Culling and the acceleration structure's instance IDs index by slot.
- **Camera-relative records.** The view model's instances have slots in the GPU scene, but their rows are relative to the camera, and last frame's relative to last frame's camera, not to the render origin. The view model is within a meter of the camera, where the render origin's error would show as about a pixel of jitter at the near plane. The debug shapes are not in the GPU scene; the debug layer writes them relative to the camera every frame.
- **Updates.** Every frame writes every slot, relative to the render origin, into the upload ring, and a copy moves them into the persistent buffer: 112 bytes per instance with both poses. Last frame's pose is the slot's presented pose of the frame before, or this frame's for a new slot. Every GPU-side consumer has its final layout.
- **Draw items** are one per submesh of each visible `Mesh_Renderer` whose mesh is resident: its slot, its mesh's record, its indices, or on the [mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path) its meshlets, its material's block, its bin, and its submesh's bounding sphere. Hidden entities (`Frame_Desc.hidden`) keep their slots but have no items.
- **Culling** is a compute pass, `cull_draws.hlsl`, with a row of the dispatch for each view: the camera (`VIEW_CAMERA`), the view model (`VIEW_VIEW_MODEL`), then each shadow view (see [Shadows](lighting-and-shadows.md#shadows)). It tests each item's sphere, moved by its instance's rows and the view's offset from their space and scaled by the largest scale of its axes, the rows' columns' lengths, and grown by the material's displacement bound (see [Materials](materials.md#vertex-displacement)), against the view's six planes; the camera's and the view model's far planes are at infinity. A shadow view takes only the items that cast shadows (the `Mesh_Renderer`'s `cast_shadows` and the material's), the view model's view only the view model's items, and every other view leaves those out. A visible item takes the next place in its bin of that view with an atomic add, and writes there both its indirect draw, whose draw ID is that place, and its draw record. An item on the mesh shader path writes a mesh draw, in a buffer of its own, since a mesh draw's record is shorter. Each view has room for every item, and each bin has the same range in every view. Culling is by frustum only, on the graphics queue.
- **Drawing:** the prepass, the shadow maps and the forward pass each draw a bin of their view with one `cmd_draw_indirect` and the bin's count, with the bin's material's pipeline for their pass. The vertex shader reads its draw record by its draw ID, and pulls its index from the mesh's index buffer, then its vertex: every mesh has buffers of its own, so no one index buffer could be bound for a bin. On the mesh shader path, an opaque bin draws with one `cmd_draw_mesh_indirect` instead.
- **The frame's constants** (`Frame_Constants`, the pass constants of every material pass) hold the view's projection, with the camera's jitter for the camera's and the view model's copies, and last frame's without it, the camera's offset from the origin and last frame's camera's, the camera's offset from the origin again as `origin_offset`, which the view model's copy keeps where it zeroes the others, the ambient light, the cascades' splits, the records' bindless indices and their offsets in the upload ring (mesh records, material table, lights, shadow views), the clusters' grid, the jitter, the exposure buffer, which the forward pass scales its colors by, whether there is fog, with the fog volume's sampler and the share of it that the screen covers, and whether there is global illumination. Each shadow view has a copy with its own projection, which takes positions relative to the camera too, so the depth pass places vertices with the same code. The forward pass pushes the shadow atlas, the shadow mask, the ambient occlusion, the fog volume and the global illumination's diffuse and specular light (`Material_Push`), which exist only once the frame records. The fog's injection uses the camera's constants too, so a frame with fog writes them even when nothing draws.
- **The mesh shader path's constants:** each view's `Frame_Constants` has its frustum's planes and where it looks from, `cull_eye`, and the camera's level of detail, `lod_scale` and `lod_near` (see [Mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path)).
- **The acceleration structure.** With `Ray_Query`, each instance whose `Mesh_Renderer` casts shadows is an instance of the GPU scene's top-level acceleration structure, in the render origin's space, with its slot as its ID. It is built again every frame that traces shadows.
- **Patch surfaces** (see [Surfaces](surfaces.md#surfaces)) have slots and records as meshes do, and one item each with `ITEM_SURFACE`, which `cull_draws.hlsl` skips. The item reserves `MAX_SURFACE_PATCHES` draws in its bin, so each view's draws are the bins' counts, not the items' (`Scene_Frame.draws_per_view`), and two more passes add the patches' draws: `select patches` after the scene's update, and `cull patches` after `cull draws`, both with the culling's constants.

## Mesh shader path

With `Caps.mesh_shaders`, the opaque passes, the prepass, the shadow maps and the forward pass, draw meshlets with amplification and mesh shaders, and pick each part of each mesh's level of detail on the GPU. Without it, they pull vertices as above, at level 0, and so do the transparent passes and the material previews always. `CINNABAR_DISABLE_FEATURES=Mesh_Shaders` forces the fallback. The meshlets and their hierarchy are the cooker's (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)).

- **Pipelines.** An opaque material has mesh pipelines for the three passes beside its vertex ones, from its blob's `as_cluster` and `ms_*` entries (see [Materials](materials.md#permutations)), and draws on the path when the device has mesh shaders and the blob has all of them (`Material_Pipeline.mesh_path`). A pipeline cannot change between the kinds, so the two sets are apart. The previews draw with the vertex ones.
- **Culling.** An item of such a material has `ITEM_MESH_PATH`, and its first and count are its submesh's meshlets, of every level. The cull pass tests its submesh's sphere as before, and a visible one writes a mesh draw of an amplification group for each 32 of its meshlets (`MESHLETS_PER_GROUP`). The draw record has the count, so a group knows which of its threads have a meshlet.
- **Amplification shaders** (`material_mesh.hlsli`) test a meshlet a thread, and compact the meshlets that pass into the payload, 32 indices, with a group-shared counter, then launch a mesh group for each. A meshlet passes when:
  - **Its level is the cut's.** An error projects to `error × scale / max(distance − radius × scale, lod_near) × lod_scale` from the camera, where `scale` is the instance's largest axis scale. The meshlet draws when its own simplification's error projects to at most 1 and its group's to more. `lod_scale` is the camera's pixels per unit of error at a distance of 1, `0.5 × height / tan(fov / 2)`, over the error in pixels that may show, `Quality.lod_error` (see [Quality](quality.md#quality)). Every view has the camera's, the shadow maps' too, so a shadow is the shape of the surface that the camera sees, and the prepass and the forward pass draw the same meshlets, as their `Equal` depth test needs.
  - **Its sphere is in the view's frustum,** scaled as the item's is, and grown by the material's displacement bound, which the draw record carries.
  - **Its normal cone does not point away from the view.** From a point, the meshlet is culled when `dot(c − eye, axis) ≥ cutoff × length(c − eye) + radius`; from a direction, when `dot(direction, axis) ≥ cutoff`. The camera's views look from the camera, a spot or point light's shadow views from the light, and a cascade along the light's direction. The axis turns with the instance's rows. The test is skipped for a two-sided material, for a material that displaces its vertices, for an instance whose axes' scales differ by more than 1 %, which bends normals, and for a cone too wide to cull.
- **Mesh shaders** (`ms_forward`, `ms_prepass`, `ms_depth`), 128 threads, each write one of the meshlet's vertices and one of its triangles. They place a vertex with `place_vertex`, as vertex pulling does after it reads an index, so both paths give the same depths and the same velocities, and the pixel shaders are the same.
- **Debug views:** `Meshlets` colors each meshlet, and `Lod_Depth` colors each by its level (see [Debug views](post-processing.md#debug-views)). On the vertex-pulling path, every surface is one color, at level 0.
- **Shadows by ray query** trace each mesh's acceleration structure, which is level 0, so a distant surface's traced shadow may differ a little from its drawn level.
