# GPU scene

The persistent buffer of every instance's pose, the render origin it is relative to, stable slots, culling into bins, the acceleration structure, and the vertex paths beside vertex pulling: the mesh shader path, billboards and impostors. Read [Render extraction](../world/render-extraction.md) first.

**In this document:** [Caster tracking](#caster-tracking) · [Mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path) · [Billboards](#billboards) · [Impostors](#impostors) · [Instanced meshes](#instanced-meshes)

The GPU scene holds every instance's record in a persistent buffer, `Gpu_Scene` in `lib/engine/render/scene.odin`: its pose and its last frame's pose as the rows of 3x4 matrices, and its entity's ID for the editor. Each frame's draw items carry the rest: the mesh, the submesh's indices and bounds, and the material.

- **Render origin.** Poses are relative to the render origin, a `World_Position` snapped to a grid of 1024 m. Snapping is an integer mask on the Q39.24 values, so the offset between two origins is exact. Shaders subtract the view's offset from the origin, so their error near the camera is about one `f32` unit at 1024 m, about 0.06 mm.
- **Rebasing.** The origin moves when the camera is more than 1.5 cells from it, so a camera on a cell's edge does not move it back and forth. A rebase writes every record, last frame's pose included, relative to the new origin (see Updates below). The top-level acceleration structure is built again every frame that anything traces.
- **Stable slots.** Each rendered entity has a slot for its whole life, at most `MAX_INSTANCES` (16 384). A free list gives slots, and a map from entity to slot, in host memory, lives in the `Renderer`. A slot whose entity has no `Mesh_Renderer` in a frame is free again. A soft restart builds them again. Culling and the acceleration structure's instance IDs index by slot. An `Instanced_Mesh_Renderer` has one slot for all of its instances (see [Instanced meshes](#instanced-meshes)).
- **Camera-relative records.** The view model's instances have slots in the GPU scene, but their rows are relative to the camera, and last frame's relative to last frame's camera, not to the render origin. The view model is within a meter of the camera, where the render origin's error would show as about a pixel of jitter at the near plane. The debug shapes are not in the GPU scene; the debug layer writes them relative to the camera every frame.
- **Updates.** The scene keeps every slot's record, relative to the render origin, in host memory: 112 bytes per instance with both poses. A frame computes the records of a chunk only if its `Transform` or `Previous_Transform` changed since two frames ago (see [ECS: Change versions](../world/ecs.md#change-versions)), or if the chunk may be between ticks: its `Transform` is newer than the last snapshot, and `alpha` moved. The two frames give a chunk that stopped moving one more write, so that last frame's pose catches up. Last frame's pose is the slot's presented pose of the frame before, or this frame's for a new slot. A record that changed makes its slot dirty. The dirty slots' records go into the upload ring with a list of their slots, and `scatter instances` (`scatter_instances.hlsl`), before `update scene`, writes each record to its slot, with a thread for each 16 bytes. So the upload is the records that changed, wherever their slots are, and a still scene uploads none. Spawns, despawns, edits from the editor and changes of activity take the same path: an edit writes its column through `get_write`, and an entity that arrives in a chunk or leaves it marks that chunk. Every GPU-side consumer has its final layout.
- **When every record is written.** A rebase, a new world, and a frame whose scatter did not run (the ring was full, the frame had nothing to draw, or the pass's pipeline had not compiled yet) make the next frame compute and scatter every slot below the highest used. The view model's records are relative to the camera, so they are computed every frame, and they are uploaded when they changed.
- **What is still per entity.** Every frame still visits each rendered entity, for its slot and its draw items, whose inputs are not columns (residency, materials, skins, what is hidden). Change versions save the poses and the upload, at most `MAX_INSTANCES` records.
- **Draw items** are one per submesh of each visible `Mesh_Renderer` whose mesh is resident: its slot, its mesh's record, its indices, or on the [mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path) its meshlets, its material's block, its bin, and its submesh's bounding sphere. A submesh of a mesh with an [impostor](#impostors) has a second item, its impostor's. Hidden entities (`Frame_Desc.hidden`) keep their slots but have no items.
- **Culling** is a compute pass, `cull_draws.hlsl`, with a row of the dispatch for each view: the camera (`VIEW_CAMERA`), the view model (`VIEW_VIEW_MODEL`), then each shadow view (see [Shadows](lighting-and-shadows.md#shadows)). It tests each item's sphere, moved by its instance's rows and the view's offset from their space and scaled by the largest scale of its axes, the rows' columns' lengths, and grown by the material's displacement bound (see [Materials](materials.md#vertex-displacement)), against the view's six planes (a [billboard](#billboards)'s sphere is one about its instance's position); the camera's and the view model's far planes are at infinity. A shadow view takes only the items that cast shadows (the `Mesh_Renderer`'s `cast_shadows` and the material's), the view model's view only the view model's items, and every other view leaves those out. A visible item takes the next place in its bin of that view with an atomic add, and writes there both its indirect draw, whose draw ID is that place, and its draw record. An item on the mesh shader path writes a mesh draw, in a buffer of its own, since a mesh draw's record is shorter. Each view has room for every item, and each bin has the same range in every view. An item with an impostor, and the impostor's item, first test which of the two the camera draws (see [Impostors](#impostors)). Culling is by frustum only, on the graphics queue.
- **An [orthographic view](frame.md#orthographic-views)** culls with a box that has a far plane, looks along one direction (`Frame_Constants.cull_eye` with w = 0), and has one level of detail at every distance (`LOD_FLAT_NEAR`).
- **Drawing:** the prepass, the shadow maps and the forward pass each draw a bin of their view with one `cmd_draw_indirect` and the bin's count, with the bin's material's pipeline for their pass. The vertex shader reads its draw record by its draw ID, and pulls its index from the mesh's index buffer, then its vertex: every mesh has buffers of its own, so no one index buffer could be bound for a bin. On the mesh shader path, an opaque bin draws with one `cmd_draw_mesh_indirect` instead.
- **The frame's constants** (`Frame_Constants`, the pass constants of every material pass) hold the view's projection, with the camera's jitter for the camera's and the view model's copies, and last frame's without it, the camera's offset from the origin and last frame's camera's, the camera's offset from the origin again as `origin_offset`, which the view model's copy keeps where it zeroes the others, the ambient light, the cascades' splits, the records' bindless indices and their offsets in the upload ring (mesh records, material table, lights, shadow views), the clusters' grid, the jitter, the exposure buffer, which the forward pass scales its colors by, whether there is fog, with the fog volume's sampler and the share of it that the screen covers, and whether there is global illumination. Each shadow view has a copy with its own projection, which takes positions relative to the camera too, so the depth pass places vertices with the same code. The forward pass pushes the shadow atlas, the shadow mask, the ambient occlusion, the fog volume and the global illumination's diffuse and specular light (`Material_Push`), which exist only once the frame records. The fog's injection uses the camera's constants too, so a frame with fog writes them even when nothing draws.
- **The mesh shader path's constants:** each view's `Frame_Constants` has its frustum's planes and where it looks from, `cull_eye`, the camera's level of detail, `lod_scale` and `lod_near`, and the list of selected meshlets, `selected_meshlets` (see [Mesh shader path](#mesh-shader-path)). Each has the camera's right and up too, and last frame's camera's, which [billboards](#billboards) face, and `Cull_Constants` has the camera's offset and level of detail, which pick between a mesh and its [impostor](#impostors).
- **The acceleration structure.** With `Ray_Query`, each instance of a `Mesh_Renderer` that casts shadows or has an opaque or masked submesh is an instance of the GPU scene's top-level acceleration structure, in the render origin's space, with its slot as its ID and a mask that says which rays see it. It is built again every frame that a feature traces. Instance sets are not in it. See [Ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene).
- **Patch surfaces** (see [Surfaces](surfaces.md#surfaces)) have slots and records as meshes do, and one item each with `ITEM_SURFACE`, which `cull_draws.hlsl` skips. The item reserves `MAX_SURFACE_PATCHES` draws in its bin, so each view's draws are the bins' counts, not the items' (`Scene_Frame.draws_per_view`), and two more passes add the patches' draws: `select patches` after the scene's update, and `cull patches` after `cull draws`, both with the culling's constants.
- **Particles** (see [Particles](particles.md#ribbons)) have a slot at each system's anchor, and their items' draws are the GPU's: a ribbon's item has `ITEM_RIBBON`, and a mesh particle emitter's item of a submesh `ITEM_PARTICLES`, which `cull_draws.hlsl` skips, and whose draw draws the submesh once for each particle (see [Mesh particles](particles.md#mesh-particles)).

## Caster tracking

The shadow caches, the atlas's [cached shadow maps](lighting-and-shadows.md#cached-shadow-maps) and the [virtual shadow map](lighting-and-shadows.md#caching)'s, keep shadows from frame to frame, so they need to know which casters changed (`lib/engine/render/casters.odin`). The frame's own view finds them in `build_scene`, after its items, while each item's bin is still its pipeline, in `Gpu_Scene.casters`.

- **A slot's shadow** is its items that cast shadows, but the view model's (`caster_bounds`): a box around their spheres, each moved by the slot's rows, scaled by the largest scale of its axes and grown by the material's displacement bound, as culling takes them, and a signature of what they draw, their indices or meshlets, pipeline, flags, bound and sphere. An instanced item's sphere is its set's sphere about its instances, grown by the submesh's reach at the instances' largest scale (`Resident_Instance_Set`), and a billboard's turns about its position. The box's sphere is in the world, a `World_Position`, so a rebase moves no caster.
- **Static and dynamic:** a slot is dynamic (`Caster_Slot.volatile`) when its pose changed in the last `SHADOW_STILL_FRAMES` (30) frames, or when its items change their shape by themselves: skinned meshes, billboards, mesh particles, and a material that reads the time and displaces its vertices or is masked (`Material_Pipeline.animated`). `scene_write_instance` says when a slot's presented pose changed, against the pose that it held, so a rebase, a full write and the catch-up of last frame's pose are not moves.
- **Changes** (`track_casters`, `Caster_Change`): a slot that starts or stops casting, whose signature changed, or that turns dynamic or static again is a change at once, from its old place to its new one. A dynamic slot reports every `SHADOW_FAR_REFRESH` (8) frames, staggered by its slot, when it moved or changes its shape by itself. A slot that was freed stops casting. The changes are of the scene frame that made them, and every view of that frame reads them.
- **Live slots:** each view, extra views too, draws the dynamic casters within its dynamic shadow distance live (`live_slots`, `caster_live`), and sets `ITEM_LIVE` on their items and `SET_LIVE` on their sets. Mesh particles have no bounds on the CPU, so they are live at any distance.
- **A view's changes** (`view_caster_changes`): the frame's, without the reports of what the view draws live, which its caches do not hold, and with a change for each slot that turned live or baked since the caches drew, which they drop where they hold it, or take where it is.
- **Tests:** `casters_test.odin` covers the changes of a still, a moving, an inherent, a changed and a freed caster, a rebase, and the live slots.

## Mesh shader path

With `Caps.mesh_shaders`, the opaque passes, the prepass, the shadow maps and the forward pass, draw meshlets with amplification and mesh shaders, and pick each part of each mesh's level of detail on the GPU. Without it, they pull vertices as above, at level 0, and so do the transparent passes and the material previews always. A mesh with pages has its level 0 only when one of these asks for it (see [Level 0](assets.md#level-0)). `CINNABAR_DISABLE_FEATURES=Mesh_Shaders` forces the fallback. The meshlets and their hierarchy are the cooker's (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)).

- **Pipelines.** An opaque material has mesh pipelines for the three passes beside its vertex ones, from its blob's `as_cluster` and `ms_*` entries (see [Materials](materials.md#permutations)), and draws on the path when the device has mesh shaders and the blob has all of them (`Material_Pipeline.mesh_path`). A pipeline cannot change between the kinds, so the two sets are apart. The previews draw with the vertex ones.
- **Culling.** An item of such a material has `ITEM_MESH_PATH`, and its first and count are its submesh's meshlets, of every level. The cull pass tests its submesh's sphere as before, and a visible one writes a mesh draw of an amplification group for each 32 of its meshlets (`MESHLETS_PER_GROUP`). An item with `ITEM_HIERARCHY` takes its range of the selected meshlets in their place (see Selection below), and its draw record has `DRAW_SELECTED`. The draw record has the count, so a group knows which of its threads have a meshlet.
- **Selection** (`select meshlets`, `meshlet_select.odin`, `select_meshlets.hlsl`) walks the trees over each level's groups of meshlets (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)), so the amplification shaders do not test every meshlet of every level:
  - **Which items.** An item has `ITEM_HIERARCHY` when it is on the path, its submesh has nodes, and it is not the view model's, whose level of detail is its own view's. Its `entry` is the submesh's first node and its `list` its count of roots. An instanced item keeps its level buckets (see [Instanced meshes](#instanced-meshes)).
  - **When.** The pass runs after `update scene` and before `cull draws`, one group of 64 threads for each such item. Every view but the view model's has the camera's level of detail, so one walk serves every view, the late view's too.
  - **The walk.** It starts at the roots, one for each level, and goes a depth of the trees at a time, through two queues in group memory. A node's error projects as a meshlet's does. A node whose error does not show has no group below it whose error shows, so the walk stops there. A leaf whose error shows is a group whose meshlets the camera may draw. The walk takes a node whose error projects to more than 0.99, a little under 1, so that rounding cannot leave out a meshlet that the amplification shaders draw.
  - **The list.** Each item's meshlets are a range of one list, `MAX_SELECTED_MESHLETS` (2^20) indices, 4 MB. The range is at the item's index in `selections`, after the list's counter, which `update scene` zeroes.
  - **Limits.** An item whose nodes the queues cannot hold (1,024 a depth), whose groups the walk cannot hold (512), or whose meshlets the list cannot hold, gets `SELECTION_NONE`. Its draw then has all of its submesh's meshlets, as without the hierarchy.
  - **Paged meshes.** A group of a mesh with pages is in a page (see [Mesh pages](assets.md#mesh-pages)). A leaf has its page, and the walk reads the page's slot from the page table. For a resident page, it lists the group's meshlets packed, the slot above bit 12 and the meshlet's index in its page below, and sets the page's word of the feedback to 1. For an absent page, it lists nothing and raises the page's word to the group's projected error, which is how much the page is wanted. The meshlets that draw in the place of an absent group's are in coarser groups, which the walk reaches by their own leaves.
- **Amplification shaders** (`material_mesh.hlsli`) test a meshlet a thread. With `DRAW_SELECTED`, each thread's meshlet is the next index in the draw's range of the list; otherwise it is the next of the submesh's. A paged mesh's meshlet is a packed word: the list has it packed, and a draw without a selection finds each of its meshlets in the page table by halving the mesh's entries on their first meshlets (`mesh_page_find` in `mesh_pages.hlsli`), and draws none of an absent page. They compact the meshlets that pass into the payload, 32 indices or packed words, with a group-shared counter, then launch a mesh group for each. Each meshlet is tested in full, so the selection draws the same meshlets as testing all of them. A meshlet passes when:
  - **Its level is the cut's.** An error projects (`lod_error` in `meshlet_lod.hlsli`, which the selection shares) to `error × scale / max(distance − radius × scale, lod_near) × lod_scale` from the camera, where `scale` is the instance's largest axis scale. The meshlet draws when its own simplification's error projects to at most 1 and its group's to more. **With pages missing,** a meshlet of a paged mesh also draws when its group's error shows and the page of the group that it was simplified from, its `refined_page`, is absent, whatever its own error is: nothing finer can draw in its place. The pool keeps a page only while the pages that it depends on are resident, so this cut still covers each submesh once (see [Content: The cut with pages missing](../content/kinds/meshes.md#the-cut-with-pages-missing)). `lod_scale` is the camera's pixels per unit of error at a distance of 1, `0.5 × height / tan(fov / 2)`, over the error in pixels that may show, `Quality.lod_error` (see [Quality](quality.md#quality)). Every view has the camera's, the shadow maps' too, so a shadow is the shape of the surface that the camera sees, and the prepass and the forward pass draw the same meshlets, as their `Equal` depth test needs.
  - **Its sphere is in the view's frustum,** scaled as the item's is, and grown by the material's displacement bound, which the draw record carries. The sphere is `f16`, which the shader decodes with `f16tof32`. Rounding only grows it, so a test keeps every meshlet that the exact sphere would, and sometimes one more that draws nothing on screen (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)). A meshlet with an infinite radius, whose sphere is too far from the mesh's origin for `f16`, passes this test and the two below.
  - **Its normal cone does not point away from the view.** From a point, the meshlet is culled when `dot(c − eye, axis) ≥ cutoff × length(c − eye) + radius`; from a direction, when `dot(direction, axis) ≥ cutoff`. The camera's views look from the camera, a spot or point light's shadow views from the light, and a cascade along the light's direction. The axis turns with the instance's rows. The test is skipped for a two-sided material, for a material that displaces its vertices, for an instance whose axes' scales differ by more than 1 %, which bends normals, and for a cone too wide to cull.
- **Mesh shaders** (`ms_forward`, `ms_prepass`, `ms_depth`), 128 threads, each write one of the meshlet's vertices and one of its triangles. A vertex's index is the meshlet's `base_vertex` plus its ref, which is half of a whole word when the meshlet has short refs, and a whole word otherwise, so no shader needs 16-bit types. A triangle is three bytes (`cluster_triangle`): the shader loads the aligned word that it starts in, and the next word only when it crosses into it, which a triangle at the third or fourth byte of a word does. The streams start at multiples of 4 bytes and are padded to them, so the second load stays in the stream. On Jungle Ruins at 1920 × 1080, at four views, the prepass and the forward pass take 1 % less time than with a `u32` for each triangle, which is less than they vary from run to run, so the second load does not show. A paged mesh's meshlet reads its record, its refs, its triangles and its vertices from its page in the pool: `load_cluster` reads the page's header, and gives the cluster's copy of the mesh's record the pool as its positions and attributes, with the page's offsets (`positions_offset`, `attributes_offset`), so `place_vertex` reads a vertex of the page as it reads one of a mesh. They place a vertex with `place_vertex`, as vertex pulling does after it reads an index, so both paths give the same depths and the same velocities, and the pixel shaders are the same.
- **Turning the selection off.** `.Meshlet_Hierarchy` in `Frame_Desc.disable`, or the viewport's toggle, makes each item test every meshlet of every level. It draws the same meshlets, so it is for profiling. A paged mesh then draws from the pages that are resident and asks for no more, as only the walk writes the feedback.
- **Debug views:** `Meshlets` colors each meshlet, and `Lod_Depth` colors each by its level (see [Debug views](post-processing.md#debug-views)). On the vertex-pulling path, every surface is one color, at level 0. An impostor is level 8, past every mesh's levels.
- **Shadows by ray query** trace each mesh's acceleration structure, which is level 0, so a distant surface's traced shadow may differ a little from its drawn level.

## Billboards

A billboard is a mesh that turns to face the camera: a material with `geometry = billboard` faces it wholly, and one with `geometry = billboard_axis` turns about the mesh's +y only, as a foliage card or a tree card does (see [Materials](materials.md#material)). Particles, sprites and foliage cards draw this way: a quad mesh, such as the sample's `meshes/card.mesh`, with a billboard material.

- **Placement.** `finish_vertex` (`material_vertex.hlsli`) turns the instance's rows with `billboard_rows` before it places a vertex: the mesh's +x goes along the camera's right, its +y along the camera's up, and its +z away from the camera, each axis with the instance's own scale, about the instance's position. A quad in the mesh's x and y fronting -z therefore faces the camera. An axis billboard keeps the rows' +y and turns about it until its +z points away from the camera; seen straight along its axis, it faces the camera's up. The normal and the tangent turn with the rows, so a billboard is lit as a surface facing the camera.
- **Both poses.** This frame's rows face this frame's camera, and last frame's rows last frame's camera, from `Frame_Constants.camera_right`, `camera_up`, `previous_camera_right` and `previous_camera_up`, so a billboard that turns as the camera turns has the velocity that temporal AA needs.
- **Every view faces the camera.** The shadow views' copies have the camera's axes and offset too, so a billboard's shadow is the shape that the camera sees, and the prepass and the forward pass place the same vertices. A preview's billboard faces its orbiting camera.
- **Culling.** An item of a billboard material has `ITEM_BILLBOARD`. Its sphere turns about the instance's position, so the culling tests the sphere about that position that holds it: its radius is the sphere's distance from the mesh's origin plus its radius, scaled.
- **A vertex path of its own.** A billboard draws with vertex pulling, the vertex path beside the mesh shader path: the cooker leaves out the mesh shader path's entries and the impostor path's, since a billboard is a few quads whose cone and levels would not hold once turned. Displacement works on it as on any mesh, after the turn.

## Impostors

An impostor is a mesh's octahedral impostor: views of the mesh from all around, which the cooker bakes (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#impostors)), and which the impostor path draws as one quad past the mesh's coarsest level of detail. Each pixel of the quad finds the surface in the atlas and evaluates the submesh's material there, so the impostor is textured, masked and lit by the scene's lights as the mesh is, and casts its shadow.

- **The atlas** is `IMPOSTOR_FRAMES` (8) by 8 frames, one for each direction on a grid of the octahedron, each an orthographic view of the mesh's bounding sphere, `impostor_size` texels a side. A texel holds the nearest surface's first UV set, its depth in the frame, its submesh and its normal and tangent in the mesh's space (`format.Impostor_Texel`). It is a buffer of the resident mesh (`Resident_Mesh.impostor`), which the mesh record names with the frames' size and the sphere.
- **Where it takes over.** The mesh's blob has `impostor_error`: the larger of the error of its coarsest level, which its top meshlets keep (see [Content pipeline](../content/kinds/meshes.md#meshlets)), and one texel of a frame, in meters. The cull pass projects it from the camera as the mesh shader path projects a meshlet's error, from the mesh's sphere and the instance's largest scale, with the camera's `lod_scale`: where it is at most 1, past where the mesh's coarsest level and a texel are too small to show, the impostor draws, and nearer, the mesh. Every view takes the camera's choice, so a shadow is the shape that the camera sees.
- **Items.** `add_items` gives a submesh of a mesh with an impostor two items when its material has the impostor path and is opaque or masked: its own, with `ITEM_HAS_IMPOSTOR`, and its impostor's, with `ITEM_IMPOSTOR`, six vertices of a quad, whose sphere is the mesh's. Both carry the mesh's sphere and its error, and the cull pass draws exactly one. The view model's items have none. `sort_bins` puts a pipeline's impostors in a bin after its own (`Scene_Frame.bin_impostors`), which draws with vertex pulling and the material's impostor passes, `Impostor_Forward`, `Impostor_Prepass` and `Impostor_Depth`, in place of the opaque ones, also for a material on the mesh shader path.
- **The quad** (`material_impostor.hlsli`) faces the view from in front of the sphere, as wide as the sphere, so it covers the sphere's outline. A view looks from `cull_eye`: the camera, or a shadow view's light, or along a cascade's direction.
- **The pixel** follows its ray into the mesh's space through the inverse of the instance's rows. It picks one of the three frames nearest the direction that it is seen from, at random by their weights, from interleaved gradient noise that changes with the time, so temporal AA blends the views; the prepass and the forward pass make the same choice. The ray meets the frame's plane through the sphere's center, then the plane at the depth found there, twice, and the texel there is the surface. A texel without a surface, or of another submesh, discards the pixel: each submesh's quad draws its own texels with its own material.
- **What the material sees.** The point from the frame's texel and depth, the normal and tangent turned by the instance's rows, the first UV set, no second, and a white vertex color. The pixel writes its own depth, which the forward pass's `Equal` test matches, and the prepass its velocity from the instance's last pose. A shadow map's depth is one texel farther from the light, since a written depth takes no pipeline bias. The impostor has no displacement.
- **Limits.** The material's textures sample with the pixel's derivatives of the atlas's UV, which jump at a UV seam, where a lower mip shows. The views are the mesh at rest: a material's displacement and anything that changes with the instance, but its pose, are not in them. A transparent material's submesh draws its mesh at every distance.
- **The sample's** `scenes/billboards.scene`, which F7 in mygame cycles to, has sprites that face the camera, tree cards that turn about their +y, a line of `meshes/tower.mesh` from 10 m to 800 m away, whose far towers draw as its impostor, and two rows of masked bushes from 20 m to 320 m away, the right one with [`alpha_coverage`](../content/kinds/textures.md#textures).

## Instanced meshes

An `Instanced_Mesh_Renderer` draws its mesh at each instance of an instance set, an `Instance_Set` asset, which a USD stage's point instancer cooks into (see [Stages](../content/kinds/stages.md#point-instancers)). A forest of millions of instances is one entity, with one slot and an item for each submesh, and the GPU culls its instances.

- **Records.** The entity's slot has its pose, which the instances' poses are relative to, and `Instance_Record.instance_set`, the set's instances. Each frame has an `Instance_Set_Record` for each set (`Scene_Frame.sets`): its buffers, its first cell in the visibility bits, its mesh's sphere, its materials' largest displacement bound, its impostor's error, its mesh's level errors (see Level buckets), and whether it casts shadows, has an impostor, or has a billboard.
- **Residency.** An instance set is resident as a mesh is (`resident_instance_set`): its instances, 32 bytes each, and its cells, each a buffer. It is ready when both have streamed in. A set draws once its mesh and the set are both ready.
- **Level buckets.** A set's visible instances in a view are in `INSTANCE_LISTS` lists: one for each of `INSTANCE_LEVEL_BUCKETS` (8) level buckets, and the impostor's. An instance's bucket is the finest level of its mesh that the camera could select for it on the mesh shader path, up to the last bucket, which takes every level past it too. For each level below the last bucket's, `Resident_Mesh.levels` has the largest parent error of the mesh's meshlets there and how far their parents' spheres reach from the mesh's center. A level's meshlets never draw where that error projects to at most 1 from the nearest point that the spheres reach, since their parents draw there instead (see `lod_selected`). `residency.mesh_levels` finds both, and each submesh's first meshlet of each bucket's level (`Resident_Mesh.level_first`), from the blob's meshlets, which the cooker orders by level within each submesh; a bucket past a submesh's last level starts at its last level.
- **Items.** `add_instanced_items` gives the entity an item with `ITEM_INSTANCED` for each submesh and each level bucket, whose `entry` is its set's index and `list` its bucket. On the mesh shader path, a bucket's item has the submesh's meshlets from its bucket's level up, so a far instance tests only the mesh's few coarse meshlets; with vertex pulling, each has the submesh's indices. When the mesh has an impostor and every submesh's material has the impostor path, each submesh has an impostor's item too, in the impostor's list, since an instance draws all of its submeshes or its impostor. Instanced items share their pipelines' bins with the other items, and the impostors' with the other impostors.
- **Culling** is `cull_instances.hlsl`, in three passes with one pipeline, before `cull draws`:
  - **Cull instances:** a group of 256 threads for each cell of each set in each view, `INSTANCE_JOB_ROW` cells to a row of the dispatch and a view to a layer. The view model's view takes no set, and a shadow view takes only a set that casts shadows. The group tests the cell's sphere, grown by the mesh's reach from its origin times the cell's largest scale. Then each thread tests an instance's sphere: the mesh's, moved by the instance's pose and the entity's, and grown by the bound. A visible instance is in its impostor's list where the impostor's error from the camera projects to at most 1, as `cull_draws.hlsl` picks for a mesh (see [Impostors](#impostors)), and in its level bucket's list nearer. Every view takes the camera's choices, so a shadow is the shape that the camera sees. The group writes the cell's bits in the view, `INSTANCE_BITS_SIZE` (160) bytes: 256 bits for the visible instances, then 4 bits for each one's list. It adds its count in each list to its set's in the view.
  - **Place instances:** a thread for each of each set's lists in each view takes a range of the views' lists of visible instances, with an atomic add on their total. The lists hold `min(instances × views, MAX_VISIBLE_INSTANCES)` indices, at most 2^25 (128 MB). A range that would pass their end is cut short, and the instances past it are not drawn.
  - **List instances:** each cell's group in each view takes places in its set's ranges for its visible instances, and writes their indices there.
- **Draws.** `cull_draws.hlsl` draws an instanced item in a view when its list there has instances. The item has one draw, whose draw record has `DRAW_INSTANCES` and the list's start (`Draw_Record.visible`). With vertex pulling, its instance count is the list's count. On the mesh shader path, its amplification groups are its meshlets, `MESHLETS_PER_GROUP` to a group, along z, and its instances along x and y, `INSTANCE_GROUP_ROW` (32 768) to a row; the count of instances is in the record's flags above `DRAW_INSTANCE_SHIFT`, so `as_cluster` knows where the last row ends. A dispatch has at most `MAX_MESH_GROUPS` (2^22) groups, as D3D12 allows, so a list that would need more draws only its first instances. `as_cluster` tests each meshlet against the instance as it does any instance's, and gives its mesh groups the instance ID in the payload. The vertex paths' `draw_instance` reads the index for the instance ID from `Frame_Constants.visible_instances`, then the instance from the set, and places the entity's rows by the instance's pose, this frame's and last frame's, so an instance's velocity is its entity's motion. The impostor path gives the instance ID to its pixels.
- **Counters.** A word for each of each set's lists in each view: the counts, the cursors that the listing takes places with, the lists' starts, then the total. `update scene` zeroes them, and `Cull_Constants.instance_counters` names them for `cull draws`.
- **Visibility bits** are at `(view × bits_cells + cell) × INSTANCE_BITS_SIZE` bytes. Each slot has a range of cells (`Gpu_Scene.slot_cells`), from a first-fit list of free ranges, and keeps it while its set's count of cells is the same. So the bits last from frame to frame. They are lost when their buffer grows. Occlusion culling keeps its own bits for each cell (see [Occlusion culling](#occlusion-culling)). There are at most `MAX_INSTANCE_CELLS` (2^18) cells, 67 million instances.
- **Picking** gives the entity, as each instance's ID is its entity's.
- **Limits.** Without mesh shaders, instanced items draw at level 0 until the impostor takes over. Instance sets are not in the acceleration structure, so with traced shadows they cast none. Only the camera culls by occlusion. An entity has a `Mesh_Renderer` or an `Instanced_Mesh_Renderer`, not both, since both would take its one slot.

## Skinned meshes

A `Mesh_Renderer` whose mesh has a skin stream (see [Content: Skins and animations](../content/kinds/skins-and-animations.md#skin-stream)) draws posed when `Frame_Desc.skins` has a `Skin_Pose` for its entity: a palette of joint matrices, from the mesh's bind pose to its posed place in its own space, and a sphere about the posed mesh. `anim.present` makes them (see [Content: Playback](../content/kinds/skins-and-animations.md#playback)). Without a pose, or until the skinning pipeline has compiled, it draws at rest as any mesh does (`render/skinning.odin`).

- **Copies.** Each skinned entity's slot has its own buffers (`Skinned_Instance`): its posed positions, last frame's posed positions and its posed attributes, which `skin_instance` makes the first frame and keeps while the slot's entity, its mesh's buffers, its vertex count and its attribute stride stay. `sweep_skins` destroys the copies of a slot that drew no pose this frame. The mesh's own buffers stay at rest, so other entities share them.
- **The pass.** `skin meshes`, before `update scene`, runs `skin_meshes.hlsl`: a row of groups for each skinned entity, 64 threads to a group, each looping over the vertices. The palettes and the jobs are in the upload ring, 48 bytes a joint. Each vertex blends its four joints' matrices by its weights, places its rest position with them, turns its normal and tangent and folds them again, keeping the bitangent's sign, and copies its other attributes. It first copies its last posed position into the previous positions, so they are last frame's without a second buffer to swap; the first frame of a copy, or one after a frame without its pose, writes the posed position into both, so the entity has no velocity then.
- **Records.** A skinned entity's items have a `Mesh_Record` of their own, not shared with the mesh's other entities, whose buffers are the copies, with `MESH_RECORD_SKINNED` and the previous positions in `previous_positions`. Every pass that reads a mesh's vertices through its record, the prepass, the shadow maps, the forward pass, the transparent passes and hit shading, reads the posed copies; `declare_skinned_reads` declares the copies for each of them. `place_vertex` reads the previous position for `finish_vertex_moved`, so the velocity is the vertex's own motion as well as its entity's.
- **Items.** A skinned entity's items have `ITEM_SKINNED`, in bins of their own (`Scene_Frame.bin_skinned`), and pull vertices, with their materials' vertex pipelines, even on the mesh shader path: the meshlets' spheres, cones and hierarchy are at rest. Each item's sphere is the pose's, and it has no impostor, which would be at rest too.
- **Ray tracing.** With a ray scene, each skinned entity has a bottom-level structure of its own over its posed positions and its mesh's indices, with `Allow_Update`. `refit skinned accels`, before the top-level structure's build, builds it the first frame and refits it after, and the entity's instance takes it (see [Ray scene](ray-scene.md#acceleration-structure)).
- **Limits.** Skinned items draw at level 0, with no meshlet culling, level of detail or impostor. A refit structure traces slower as the pose moves far from the first; nothing builds it again. An `Instanced_Mesh_Renderer`, a mesh particle or a live mesh with a skinned mesh draws at rest. Joints with a negative determinant, which mirror, are not handled.

## Occlusion culling

The camera does not draw what is behind other opaque surfaces. Culling is in two phases, with a depth pyramid (an HZB) of this frame between them (`occlusion.odin`, `occlusion.hlsli`, `hzb.hlsl`):

1. **Early.** The camera's view has `VIEW_EARLY`. It draws only the opaque items and instances that were visible last frame. The prepass draws them as usual.
2. **The pyramid.** One pass for each level builds the pyramid from the prepass's depth. Level 0 is half the frame's size, and each texel is the farthest depth of its 2×2 texels below (reversed Z, so the smallest). At an odd edge, the last texel takes the extra row or column too. The levels are `R32_Float` textures, one for each level, made again when the frame's size changes.
3. **Late.** A second view of the camera, after the shadow views (`Scene_Frame.late_view`), has `VIEW_LATE`. `cull late draws` and the late instance passes test each opaque item and instance in the frustum against the pyramid, and keep each one that it shows as visible for next frame. They draw only those that the early view did not draw. The `late prepass` draws them, and the forward pass draws both views. A [virtual shadow map](lighting-and-shadows.md#virtual-shadow-maps)'s levels cull later still, as the shadow views' first rows, after its page table is written, so the early dispatches' rows skip them (`view_skip`).

- **The test** (`sphere_occluded`) projects the eight corners of the sphere's box. It reads the level where the box is at most two texels wide, at most 2×2 texels. The sphere is hidden when its nearest depth is farther than the farthest of those texels. A sphere that crosses the near plane is never hidden.
- **What it culls.** Items in the opaque bins (`Occlusion_Constants.opaque_bins`), and instance sets with `SET_OCCLUDABLE`, whose items are all opaque. Other items and sets draw in the early view only, by frustum. Shadow views do not cull by occlusion.
- **Last frame's visibility.** Each slot has a bit, in two buffers that swap each frame, which the late view sets and next frame's early view reads (`slot_seen`, `slot_seen_next`). Each instance cell has 256 bits, one for each of its instances, in a buffer that lasts (`cell_seen`, 32 bytes for each cell). The late view first tests the cell's sphere, and its instances are all hidden when the cell is. On the mesh shader path, `as_cluster` also tests each meshlet's sphere in the late view. Each [view](frame.md#views) keeps its own bits, pyramid and tally in its `View_State` (`Occlusion_State`), so a view sees what it saw last frame, whatever the others did.
- **Stats.** `cull_draws.hlsl` and `cull_instances.hlsl` count the camera's items and instances drawn early, drawn late, and hidden, into a buffer of `CULL_STATS_WORDS` words. `select_meshlets.hlsl` adds the meshlets of every level of the items that it walks, and the meshlets that it selects for them. An item that gets `SELECTION_NONE` adds all of its meshlets to both. A copy goes to the tally's [readback ring](../gpu/queues-streaming-readback.md#readback) each frame. `cull_stats` gives the last copy that came back, or none after a frame that tallied nothing or did not execute, and the editor shows it (see [Shell](../editor/shell-and-panels.md#resource-monitor)).
- **Turning it off.** `.Occlusion_Culling` in `Frame_Desc.disable`, or the viewport's toggle, draws everything in the frustum in the early view.
- **Limits.** Something that comes into view from behind an occluder draws in the same frame's late view, since the pyramid is this frame's depth, so nothing pops in a frame late. What hides something new is not in the pyramid until it was visible a frame, so the late view draws more than it needs to then, as after a cut, when it draws most of the scene. Only the camera's prepass depth makes the pyramid, so transparent surfaces and the view model hide nothing.
