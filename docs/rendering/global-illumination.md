# Global illumination

The global illumination is screen-space radiance cascades, on the compute queue after the prepass (`lib/engine/render/gi.odin`). It replaces the constant ambient light with the light that reaches each pixel: the sky's, which nearby surfaces block, and the light that those surfaces reflect, which bounces on from frame to frame. The `Global_Illumination` settings set it, and it is on by default. Without its pipelines, or with `off`, the indirect light is the `Environment`'s ambient light. With the quality's `World_Probes` technique and `Ray_Query`, a grid of [world probes](#world-probes) gives the light that the screen does not see, and the [split cascades](#split-cascades) take the screen cascades' place.

- **Cascades** (`gi_common.hlsli`): the [quality](quality.md#levels)'s level gives their count, their probe spacing, their directions, the rays' steps and the histories' length (`GI_PARAMS`); the numbers here are High's. Five cascades. Cascade k has a probe every `spacing << k` pixels (8 to 128), on the surface at its tile's center, with `(directions << k)^2` directions over the sphere (4 across: 16 to 4096), folded into an octahedron. Each cascade therefore has about a quarter of the screen's pixels as texels, and all of them are one `RGBA16_Float` texture, each cascade in its own rows. Ultra's probes are as far apart as High's, with 8 directions across (64 to 16384), so each of its cascades has as many texels as the screen has pixels: 8 across at half the spacing would be four times as many, past the texture's size at 4K. A view so large that the cascades would not fit a texture 16384 texels on a side has its last cascades left out (`gi_fit`).
- **Intervals.** Cascade k's rays trace from one distance to the next, each interval four times as long as the one before. The first is `GI_INTERVAL_SCALE` (2) probe spacings long at the probe's depth with 4 directions across, and longer with more, as each direction is narrower: 4 spacings at Ultra, whose five cascades reach as far as six would with 4. So a cascade covers about as many pixels at every depth. For a surface 3 m away in a view 720 pixels high, the last cascade ends about 30 m away.
- **The rays** (`gi_trace.hlsl`) march their interval in the level's steps (8) through the depth buffer, from the probe's surface moved off it by two pixels. Where a step passes behind a surface by less than its thickness, the ray stops, and takes that surface's light from last frame's lit scene, where it was then. A ray that reaches its interval's end lets through what lies beyond. A ray that leaves the screen does too, or with the world probes takes their light where it left, and nothing from beyond. The rays' directions within their texels and their steps' offsets come from noise that moves every frame.
- **The merge** (`gi_merge.hlsl`), one pass for each cascade from the last to the first, adds to each texel what gets through it: the next cascade's light in the four finer directions that it splits into, at the four probes around it. Probes on the same surface count fully: their surfaces are within 3% of the view depth from the texel's tangent plane. Others count very little, and background has none. Beyond the last cascade, and where no probe of the next cascade has a surface, the light is the world probes' light from that direction at the probe's surface, or without them the ambient light's (see [Ambient light](sky.md#ambient-light)).
- **The gather** (`gi_gather.hlsl`) gives each pixel its diffuse light, the cosine-weighted mean of the first cascade's directions around its normal (`direction_weight`), where each direction counts by its solid angle too with 6 or more across; with 4, the texels are so large that the light and the cosine vary across each, and the cosine at the center alone gives the nearer mean, and its specular light, the second cascade's along its reflection, blended between the nearest directions. Each comes from the four probes around the pixel, weighted as in the merge and by how alike their normals are. Both blend into history textures by the velocity, at most the level's `history_frames` (12) frames. A history whose depth is not where this frame's point was last frame starts over. A pixel that no probe matches takes the world probes' light at its point, or the ambient light.
- **The lit scene** (`gi_lit.hlsl`): after the world pass, a half-resolution copy of the scene without its pre-exposure is kept as a history texture for the next frame's rays. It holds the indirect light too, so light bounces once more each frame. It also holds the fog in front of each surface.
- **The forward pass** reads the diffuse and specular light as `Indirect`, scaled by the ambient occlusion. The view model's pixels take the light around the camera: the world probes', or the ambient light.
- **Limits:** without the world probes, the rays see only what is on the screen, so light from behind the camera or from hidden surfaces is missing, and a surface's light changes as other surfaces come into view. Specular light is as coarse as the second cascade's 64 directions (256 at Ultra), so reflections are blurred. Thin objects can block too much, since the depth buffer has no thickness.

## World probes

With `Gi_Technique.World_Probes` and `Ray_Query`, a grid of probes in world space traces the [ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene) for the light that the screen does not see (`lib/engine/render/gi_probes.odin`, `gi_probes.hlsli`, `gi_probe_*.hlsl`). The cascades read it where they took the ambient light, so the forward pass's `Indirect` and `shade_indirect` do not change. A device without `Ray_Query`, or with `CINNABAR_DISABLE_FEATURES=Ray_Query`, has the cascades alone (see [Quality](quality.md#hardware-caps)).

- **Levels.** The probes are clipmap levels around the camera, each a box of `GI_PROBE_GRID` (32 × 16 × 32) probes. The first level's probes are 1 m apart (2 m at Low), and each level's twice as far apart as the one before; the quality's level gives the count, 2 to 4 (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS`). Every spacing is a power of two of meters, so it divides the render origin's 1024 m cell.
- **Following the camera.** Each level keeps its first probe's world cell, from the camera's `World_Position` with an arithmetic shift, so a negative position floors. The box moves when the camera is more than `GI_PROBE_RECENTER` (2) probes from its middle. A probe's storage is its world cell modulo the grid, so the box's move keeps every probe where it was, and only the probes whose cells it moved over reset: the reset pass sets their states to no updates, and the schedule updates them first. A teleport resets the whole level. Each frame gives each level's first probe relative to the camera from whole units, so it is exact anywhere.
- **Rebasing.** The levels follow the camera's world position, not the render origin, so a rebase moves no probe and resets none. The shaders add the camera's offset from the origin when they trace, as the shadow mask does.
- **Storage.** A probe keeps a tile of 6 × 6 octahedral texels with a one-texel border, in two `RGBA16_Float` textures: the radiance that reaches it from each direction with the mean distance of its rays' hits, and the cosine-weighted mean radiance around each direction with the mean squared distance. A buffer holds each probe's state: its updates, up to 255, and whether it is inside geometry. They are the renderer's own, made for the level's count of levels, about 16 MB a level.
- **Updates.** Each frame updates the level's budget of probes, 1024 to 8192 (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS`): first those that wait since their reset, the finest level's first, then each level's share of the rest in turn. The passes run on the compute queue, after the prepass and before the cascades:
  - **Trace** (`gi_probe_trace.hlsl`): each probe's rays, 32 or 64, a spherical Fibonacci set turned by a rotation that changes every update (the Halton sequence, uniform over rotations), trace the scene's surfaces with `trace_hit`, out to twice their level's box.
  - **Hit shading** evaluates each hit's material (see [Hit shading](ray-scene.md#hit-shading)).
  - **Light** (`gi_probe_light.hlsl`, with `hit_light` in `gi_hit_light.hlsli`): a miss sees the ambient light. A hit's light is its emission, the light of every directional light and of up to `GI_MAX_PROBE_LIGHTS` (16) punctual lights whose spheres reach the outermost box, the brightest by their light over their squared distance first, each shadowed by a ray toward it through the shadow casters, and the probes' light from earlier updates at the hit, so light bounces once more each update. A back face of a one-sided surface is black, and its distance negative.
  - **Screen** (`gi_probe_screen.hlsl`, with `screen_light` in `gi_hit_screen.hlsli`): a hit of a front face that the prepass's depth shows takes its light from last frame's lit scene, where it was then, as the cascades' rays do. So the probes see what the screen sees: the light of a small emissive surface, which few of a probe's rays hit, and the light that the cascades gave the surfaces around it. Only the hits off the screen depend on the probes' own rays. It is a pass of its own after the light; folded into the light's shader beside its probe lookups, it hung the GPU on an RTX 5090, for a reason not found.
  - **Update** (`gi_probe_update.hlsl`): each texel of the probe's tile blends its direction's share of the rays into what it held: the radiance in a narrow lobe, the cosine-weighted mean, and the distances in a narrower lobe. A probe takes its first update as it is, then blends more slowly, down to keeping `GI_PROBE_HYSTERESIS` (0.97) of its light. The border repeats the interior across the octahedron's folds. A probe that sees back faces with more than a quarter of its rays is inside geometry and counts for nothing.
- **Sampling** (`sample_probes`): a point moves off its surface by a quarter of the level's spacing, and takes the finest level whose box holds it, blended into the next over its outermost probe. The eight probes around it count by their trilinear weight, by whether they are in front of the surface, and by whether they see the point, from the mean and the variance of their rays' distances toward it (Chebyshev, as in Majercik et al., *Dynamic Diffuse Global Illumination with Ray-Traced Irradiance Fields*, JCGT 2019). A probe that was never updated, or is inside geometry, does not count. Where none counts, or outside every level, the cascades take the ambient light.
- **Where the cascades take it.** A cascade ray that leaves the screen takes the probes' radiance along it where it left. The merge's light beyond the last cascade, and where no probe of the next cascade matches, is the probes' radiance along the direction at the probe's surface. The gather's pixels that no probe matches, and the view model's, take the probes' mean light around the normal and their radiance along the reflection.
- **Waiting.** The probes start once their pipelines, hit shading's and the ray scene's are ready, and a frame has draw items. The screen pass starts once the lit scene has a frame of history. Until then the cascades take the ambient light, and the levels stay where they were.
- **Limits.** The ray scene has no instance sets, patch surfaces or particles, so the probes do not see foliage, terrain or water. A probe inside geometry is turned off, not moved. Static content does not let the probes settle sooner: that waits for the GPU scene to update only changed slots ([#142](https://github.com/aszecsei/cinnabar/issues/142)). Light leaks where a wall is thinner than a probe's spacing.

## Split cascades

With `Gi_Technique.World_Probes` and `Ray_Query`, once the world probes run, the split radiance cascades take the screen cascades' place, after Jack Tollenaar's [*Split Radiance Cascades*](https://www.jacktollenaar.top/articles/split-rc.html) (`lib/engine/render/gi_split.odin`, `gi_split.hlsli`, `gi_split_*.hlsl`). Their probes are in world space, on the surfaces that the screen shows, and keep their light from frame to frame, so the light does not smear as the camera moves, and a surface that comes into view has its light already. Their rays trace the ray scene, so what blocks the light need not be on the screen, and a long interval is not a few steps through the depth buffer. The forward pass reads their light as it reads the screen cascades'.

- **Levels** (`GI_SPLIT_PARAMS`; the numbers here are High's). Each level of detail is a window of `SPLIT_WINDOW` (512) cells on each axis around the camera. The first level's cells are 0.25 m (0.5 m at Low, 0.125 m at Ultra), and each level's twice the one before's; there are 5 (4 at Low). Level L holds the distances from 24 cells of level 0 times 2^L to twice that, by the Chebyshev distance from the camera, so a cell covers about as many pixels at every distance, and the last `SPLIT_BAND` (half) of each level's distances blends into the next. Past the last level, the light is the world probes'.
- **Following the camera.** Each window's first cell is a cell of `World_Position`, from the camera's with an arithmetic shift, so a render-origin rebase moves nothing. It is the camera's cell less 256, a multiple of `SPLIT_SNAP` (16), so the camera is always near the middle. Each frame gives each window's first cell relative to the camera from whole units, and how far it moved.
- **Cascades.** 4 (3 at Low). Cascade k's probe of a cell is the cell's window coordinates shifted right by k, so its cells are 2^k times as large, with `(directions << k)^2` directions folded into an octahedron: 6 across (4 at Low and Medium, 8 at Ultra), so 36 to 2304. A probe's key is its window coordinates, its level and a high bit, in 32 bits. The first cascade's interval is a direction texel's width at the probe, `directions / sqrt(4π)` cells, and each interval is four times as long as the one before.
- **Tables** (`gi_split_table.hlsl`). Each cascade has a fixed count of slots: 16384 for the first (8192 at Low, 32768 at Ultra), and a quarter as many for each after it, but at least 256 (512 at Ultra). A hash table for each cascade, of twice as many entries, linearly probed for at most 32, finds a key's slot. Each frame empties the tables, and a thread for each slot moves its key by its window's move and puts it back, or frees it when it left the window or no ray reached it for `SPLIT_LIFETIME` (60) frames; a probe keeps its slot, and its light, while it lives. Then a thread for each traced pixel claims its cell's probe at each cascade and each of its levels with a compare-and-swap, and the first to claim a key takes a free slot. A key that finds no room has no slot, and the merge and the gather take the light from beyond it.
- **Rays** (`gi_split_trace.hlsl`). Each frame traces one ray from a pixel of each tile of 2 × 2 pixels (4 × 4 at Low, 1 × 1 at Ultra), each pixel of a tile in turn. A tile widens while the rays are more than hit shading takes (`MAX_HIT_RAYS`), so Ultra at 4K traces one pixel of each 2 × 1. The ray starts at the pixel's surface, two pixels off it, not at a probe's center, and its direction is uniform over the sphere: the R2 sequence by the frame, offset for each tile. It reaches as far as the last cascade of the pixel's coarser level, and hit shading and the world probes' [light and screen passes](#world-probes) give its hit's light.
- **The deposit** (`gi_split_deposit.hlsl`) splits each ray by its hit's distance, at each of the pixel's levels: the cascade whose interval holds the hit counts the ray and adds the hit's light, in its bin of the ray's direction at the probe of the pixel's cell, and each cascade before it counts the ray as passing through. A ray past the last cascade passes through them all. The light is pre-exposed and added in fixed point, in units of 1 / `SPLIT_RADIANCE_SCALE` (1024) up to `SPLIT_RADIANCE_MAX` (64), with integer atomics and a dither that keeps dim light's mean. A back face within four pixels means that the ray started inside geometry, and the ray counts for nothing.
- **The merge** (`gi_split_merge.hlsl`), one pass for each cascade from the last to the first, a group for each 64 bins of each active probe, dispatched by the counts that the tables wrote. Each bin takes its frame's rays into its history: the share that passed through and the mean light of the rest, blended by the rays against the history's, which decays by `1 - 1 / history_frames` (12) each frame and holds at most `SPLIT_MAX_COUNT` (256) rays. Then it adds the light that passes through it from the next cascade: the four finer directions that it splits into, at the eight probes around it, three to one on each axis, each by its confidence, `rays / (rays + 8)`. Where no such probe has light, and beyond the last cascade, it is the world probes' radiance along the direction. The first cascade's probes also write their tile of an atlas, 6 × 6 octahedral texels with a border, as the world probes' are: the cosine-weighted mean of their light around each direction (`direction_weight`).
- **The gather** (`gi_split_gather.hlsl`): each pixel's diffuse light is the atlas's at the first cascade's eight probes around it, along its normal, and its specular light the second cascade's merged light along its reflection, blended between the four nearest directions. Each probe counts by its trilinear weight, by how much it is in front of the surface, as the world probes do, and by its confidence, and the levels blend by the distance. The world probes' light counts a hundredth as much as one probe everywhere, so a place that no probe has reached yet fades in from it, and it counts fully past the last level. No history follows the pixels.
- **Memory.** The tables, bins and atlas take about 19 MB at Low, 49 MB at Medium, 99 MB at High and 338 MB at Ultra, made for the level when the split first runs at it. The rays' buffers take 84 bytes for each traced pixel, with hit shading's: 74 MB at High at 1440p. The diffuse and specular histories are still made, though the split does not read them.
- **Waiting.** The split cascades start once their pipelines are ready, and run when the world probes run this frame and hit shading has room for their batch. Until then, the frame takes the screen cascades.
- **Limits.** Light leaks where a wall is thinner than a cell, since the rays from both of its sides reach the same probes. The ray scene has no instance sets, patch surfaces or particles, so the rays pass through foliage, terrain and water. Each hit that the screen does not show casts a shadow ray toward every directional light and each listed punctual light, so the hits' light costs most at Ultra.
