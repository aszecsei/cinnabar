# Global illumination

The global illumination is screen-space radiance cascades, on the compute queue after the prepass (`lib/engine/render/gi.odin`). It replaces the constant ambient light with the light that reaches each pixel: the sky's, which nearby surfaces block, and the light that those surfaces reflect, which bounces on from frame to frame. The `Global_Illumination` settings set it, and it is on by default. Without its pipelines, or with `off`, the indirect light is the `Environment`'s ambient light. With the quality's `Split_Cascades` technique and `Ray_Query`, a grid of [world probes](#world-probes) gives the light that the screen does not see, and the [split cascades](#split-cascades) take the screen cascades' place. With `Restir` and `Ray_Query`, the world probes run too, and [ReSTIR GI](#restir-gi) takes the screen cascades' place, with a [ray guide](#ray-guiding) that draws some of its rays toward the light. With `Voxel_Cones`, on any device, the [voxel cones](#voxel-cones) take their place.

- **Cascades** (`gi_common.hlsli`): the [quality](quality.md#levels)'s level gives their count, their probe spacing, their directions, the rays' steps and the histories' length (`GI_PARAMS`); the numbers here are High's. Five cascades. Cascade k has a probe every `spacing << k` pixels (8 to 128), on the surface at its tile's center, with `(directions << k)^2` directions over the sphere (4 across: 16 to 4096), folded into an octahedron. Each cascade therefore has about a quarter of the screen's pixels as texels, and all of them are one `RGBA16_Float` texture, each cascade in its own rows. Ultra's probes are as far apart as High's, with 8 directions across (64 to 16384), so each of its cascades has as many texels as the screen has pixels: 8 across at half the spacing would be four times as many, past the texture's size at 4K. A view so large that the cascades would not fit a texture 16384 texels on a side has its last cascades left out (`gi_fit`).
- **Intervals.** Cascade k's rays trace from one distance to the next, each interval four times as long as the one before. The first is `GI_INTERVAL_SCALE` (2) probe spacings long at the probe's depth with 4 directions across, and longer with more, as each direction is narrower: 4 spacings at Ultra, whose five cascades reach as far as six would with 4. So a cascade covers about as many pixels at every depth. For a surface 3 m away in a view 720 pixels high, the last cascade ends about 30 m away.
- **The rays** (`gi_trace.hlsl`) march their interval in the level's steps (8) through the depth buffer, from the probe's surface moved off it by two pixels. Where a step passes behind a surface by less than its thickness, the ray stops, and takes that surface's light from last frame's lit scene, where it was then. A ray that reaches its interval's end lets through what lies beyond. A ray that leaves the screen does too, or with the world probes takes their light where it left, and nothing from beyond. The rays' directions within their texels and their steps' offsets come from noise that moves every frame.
- **The merge** (`gi_merge.hlsl`), one pass for each cascade from the last to the first, adds to each texel what gets through it: the next cascade's light in the four finer directions that it splits into, at the four probes around it. Probes on the same surface count fully: their surfaces are within 3% of the view depth from the texel's tangent plane. Others count very little, and background has none. Beyond the last cascade, and where no probe of the next cascade has a surface, the light is the world probes' light from that direction at the probe's surface, or without them the ambient light's (see [Ambient light](sky.md#ambient-light)).
- **The gather** (`gi_gather.hlsl`) gives each pixel its diffuse light, the cosine-weighted mean of the first cascade's directions around its normal (`direction_weight`), where each direction counts by its solid angle too with 6 or more across; with 4, the texels are so large that the light and the cosine vary across each, and the cosine at the center alone gives the nearer mean, and its specular light, the second cascade's along its reflection, blended between the nearest directions. Each comes from the four probes around the pixel, weighted as in the merge and by how alike their normals are. Both blend into history textures by the velocity, at most the level's `history_frames` (12) frames. A history whose depth is not where this frame's point was last frame starts over. A pixel that no probe matches takes the world probes' light at its point, or the ambient light around its normal and the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) along its reflection, at the mip for its roughness from the prepass (`Frame.roughness`).
- **The lit scene** (`gi_lit.hlsl`): after the world pass, a half-resolution copy of the scene without its pre-exposure is kept as a history texture for the next frame's rays. It holds the indirect light too, so light bounces once more each frame. It also holds the fog in front of each surface. The [screen-space reflections](lighting-and-shadows.md#screen-space-reflections) read it too at Low and Medium, in place of a lit scene of their own.
- **The forward pass** reads the diffuse and specular light as `Indirect`, scaled by the ambient occlusion. The view model's pixels take the light around the camera: the world probes', or the ambient light and the sky's cubemap. With the [reflection probes](lighting-and-shadows.md#reflection-probes), the probes blend over the specular light below their level's roughness fade, so rough surfaces keep this light and its dynamic occlusion. Transparent surfaces never take it.
- **Reflection probes' captures.** The cascades and their history are the camera's screen, so a capture's faces cannot read them. A face takes the ambient light and the sky's cubemap, scaled by the ambient occlusion of its own depth. With the split cascades, it takes the [world probes](#world-probes)' light, and with the [voxel cones](#voxel-cones), it gathers the cones at its own pixels (see [Reflection probes](lighting-and-shadows.md#reflection-probes)).
- **Limits:** without the world probes, the rays see only what is on the screen, so light from behind the camera or from hidden surfaces is missing, and a surface's light changes as other surfaces come into view. Specular light is as coarse as the second cascade's 64 directions (256 at Ultra), so reflections are blurred where no reflection probe counts. Thin objects can block too much, since the depth buffer has no thickness.

## World probes

With `Gi_Technique.Split_Cascades` and `Ray_Query`, a grid of probes in world space traces the [ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene) for the light that the screen does not see (`lib/engine/render/gi_probes.odin`, `gi_probes.hlsli`, `gi_probe_*.hlsl`). The cascades read it where they took the ambient light, so the forward pass's `Indirect` and `shade_indirect` do not change. A device without `Ray_Query`, or with `CINNABAR_DISABLE_FEATURES=Ray_Query`, has the cascades alone (see [Quality](quality.md#hardware-caps)).

- **Levels.** The probes are clipmap levels around the camera, each a box of `GI_PROBE_GRID` (32 × 16 × 32) probes. The first level's probes are 1 m apart (2 m at Low), and each level's twice as far apart as the one before; the quality's level gives the count, 2 to 4 (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS`). Every spacing is a power of two of meters, so it divides the render origin's 1024 m cell.
- **Following the camera.** Each level keeps its first probe's world cell, from the camera's `World_Position` with an arithmetic shift, so a negative position floors. The box moves when the camera is more than `GI_PROBE_RECENTER` (2) probes from its middle. A probe's storage is its world cell modulo the grid, so the box's move keeps every probe where it was, and only the probes whose cells it moved over reset: the reset pass sets their states to no updates, and the schedule updates them first. A teleport resets the whole level. Each frame gives each level's first probe relative to the camera from whole units, so it is exact anywhere.
- **Rebasing.** The levels follow the camera's world position, not the render origin, so a rebase moves no probe and resets none. The shaders add the camera's offset from the origin when they trace, as the shadow mask does.
- **Storage.** A probe keeps a tile of 6 × 6 octahedral texels with a one-texel border, in three `RGBA16_Float` textures: the radiance that reaches it from each direction with the mean distance of its rays' hits, the cosine-weighted mean radiance around each direction with the mean squared distance, and the same mean without the light of the listed [emitters](#emitters), the hits' irradiance, which the hits read. A buffer holds each probe's state: its updates, up to 255, and whether it is inside geometry. They are the renderer's own, made for the level's count of levels, about 24 MB a level. Each [view](frame.md#views) of a frame keeps its own probes, textures and levels (`Gi_Probe_State`); the pipelines and the rays' buffers are shared.
- **Updates.** Each frame updates the level's budget of probes, 1024 to 8192 (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS`): first those that wait since their reset, the finest level's first, then each level's share of the rest in turn. The passes run on the compute queue, after the prepass and before the cascades:
  - **Trace** (`gi_probe_trace.hlsl`): each probe's rays, 32 or 64, a spherical Fibonacci set turned by a rotation that changes every update (the Halton sequence, uniform over rotations), trace the scene's surfaces with `trace_hit`, out to twice their level's box.
  - **Hit shading** evaluates each hit's material (see [Hit shading](ray-scene.md#hit-shading)).
  - **Light** (`gi_probe_light.hlsl`, with `hit_light` in `gi_hit_light.hlsli`): a miss sees the ambient light. A hit's light is its emission, the light of every directional light, each shadowed by a ray toward it, the light of one punctual light or emissive triangle that the hit picks from many (see [Many lights](#many-lights)), shadowed by one ray, and the probes' hits' irradiance from earlier updates at the hit, so light bounces once more each update. The shadow rays trace the shadow casters, and pass through translucent submeshes. The diffuse albedo takes the direct light over π and the probes' light; the specular albedo takes the probes' light too, so a metal's hit is not black, and the picked emitter's light over π, or where no probe counts, the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) along the hit's reflection at its roughness. A second probe lookup along the reflection, in this shader beside the first, hung the GPU on an RTX 5090, as the screen light did. A back face of a one-sided surface is black, and its distance negative. The split cascades and the [traced reflections](lighting-and-shadows.md#ray-traced-reflections) light their hits with the same passes (`add_hit_light_passes`), through `Hit_Light_Inputs`: the reflections take the world probes where they run, and otherwise a grid of no levels, so their hits take the ambient light and the sky's cubemap.
  - **Screen** (`gi_probe_screen.hlsl`, with `screen_light` in `gi_hit_screen.hlsli`): a hit of a front face that the prepass's depth shows takes its light from last frame's lit scene, where it was then, as the cascades' rays do. So the probes see what the screen sees: the light of a small emissive surface, which few of a probe's rays hit, and the light that the cascades gave the surfaces around it. Only the hits off the screen depend on the probes' own rays. It is a pass of its own after the light; folded into the light's shader beside its probe lookups, it hung the GPU on an RTX 5090, for a reason not found.
  - **Update** (`gi_probe_update.hlsl`): first, each ray's light is clamped by its luminance (`clamp_hit_light` in `hit_clamp.hlsli`): at this frame's exposure, it is at most the level's `radiance_max` (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS`, 16 at every level), and its color stays the same. So one ray that finds a small, bright emitter does not put a bright spot into a probe that keeps it for many updates. The split cascades' deposit clamps each channel to `SPLIT_RADIANCE_MAX` (64) in the same way. Then each texel of the probe's tile blends its direction's share of the rays into what it held: the radiance in a narrow lobe, the cosine-weighted mean, and the distances in a narrower lobe. The hits' irradiance takes the same cosine-weighted mean of each ray's light less the radiance of the listed [emitter](#emitters) that it hit, before the clamp. A probe takes its first update as it is, then blends more slowly, down to keeping `GI_PROBE_HYSTERESIS` (0.97) of its light. The border repeats the interior across the octahedron's folds. A probe that sees back faces with more than a quarter of its rays is inside geometry and counts for nothing.

- **Sampling** (`sample_probes`): a point moves off its surface by a quarter of the level's spacing, and takes the finest level whose box holds it, blended into the next over its outermost probe. The eight probes around it count by their trilinear weight, by whether they are in front of the surface, and by whether they see the point, from the mean and the variance of their rays' distances toward it (Chebyshev, as in Majercik et al., *Dynamic Diffuse Global Illumination with Ray-Traced Irradiance Fields*, JCGT 2019). A probe that was never updated, or is inside geometry, does not count. Where none counts, or outside every level, the cascades take the ambient light, and the gathers' specular light the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light).
- **Where the cascades take it.** A cascade ray that leaves the screen takes the probes' radiance along it where it left. The merge's light beyond the last cascade, and where no probe of the next cascade matches, is the probes' radiance along the direction at the probe's surface. The gather's pixels that no probe matches, and the view model's, take the probes' mean light around the normal and their radiance along the reflection.
- **Waiting.** The probes start once their pipelines, hit shading's and the ray scene's are ready, and a frame has draw items. The screen pass starts once the lit scene has a frame of history. Until then the cascades take the ambient light, and the levels stay where they were.
- **Limits.** The ray scene has no instance sets, patch surfaces or particles, so the probes do not see foliage, terrain or water. A probe inside geometry is turned off, not moved. Static content does not let the probes settle sooner: that waits for the GPU scene to update only changed slots ([#142](https://github.com/aszecsei/cinnabar/issues/142)). Light leaks where a wall is thinner than a probe's spacing.

## Many lights

A hit of the world probes', the split cascades' or the traced reflections' rays takes every directional light, and one more light that it picks from the punctual lights around it and the listed emissive triangles, by resampled importance sampling (RIS), after the NVIDIA whitepaper [*Real-Time Path Tracing in Games: Best Practices for Performance and Scalability*](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/Whitepapers/tree/main/pt_performance_whitepaper) (§8). So every light lights the hits near it, however many there are, at the cost of one shadow ray.

- **The light grid** (`lib/engine/render/hit_lights.odin`): each frame, the host lists the punctual and area lights by the cells of world space that their spheres touch. There are `LIGHT_GRID_LEVELS` (6) levels, each a window of `LIGHT_GRID_WINDOW` (512) cells on each axis around the camera, with cells of 2 m at the first level and twice as large at each after. A light is in the first level whose cells are at least as wide as its sphere, in each of that level's cells that its sphere touches, 8 at most. A light that no level's window holds, such as one too large for the last level's cells, is in the global list, which every hit sees. A light's falloff is 0 at its range, so a hit finds every light that can reach it, and finds each once. The cells are keyed as the split cascades' probes are, and hashed with lowbias32 into a table, linearly probed for at most `LIGHT_GRID_PROBE_STEPS` (32). The host checks that every key is found within them, and doubles the table, up to `MAX_LIGHT_GRID_ENTRIES` (65536), when one is not. Each level's first cell is a cell of `World_Position`, given relative to the camera from whole units, so a render-origin rebase changes nothing. A frame makes its grid once (`light_grid_upload`), and the three clients share it.
- **Candidates** (`pick_hit_light` in `gi_light_sampling.hlsli`). A hit weighs its level's `candidates`: 4 at Low, 8 at Medium and 16 at High and Ultra (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS` for the world probes and the split cascades, `REFLECTION_PARAMS[.Ray_Traced]` for the traced reflections, see [Quality](quality.md#levels)). With emitters this frame, a quarter of them, at least one, draw emissive triangles, and the rest the punctual lights.
  - **Punctual lights.** The hit's lists are its cell at each level that holds it, and the global list. When they have no more lights than the punctual candidates, each is a candidate once, weighed by its light. Otherwise each candidate is drawn uniformly from them, and weighed by its light times their count over the candidates.
  - **Emissive triangles.** Each candidate draws a triangle by its power from the [emitters](#emitters)' distribution, by a binary search, then a point uniformly over the triangle. Its weight is its light at the hit, the triangle's radiance times the cosine at the hit, the cosine at the triangle and over the squared distance, over its chance per area: the triangle's share of the power over its area, times the candidates. A one-sided triangle seen from behind gives nothing.
  - **The pick.** A candidate's light at the hit is without its shadow, and its target is its luminance. The hit keeps one candidate in proportion to the weights, and casts one shadow ray toward it, which stops short of a triangle so it does not shadow itself. The light that the hit takes is the kept candidate's, over its target, times the weights' sum: in expectation, the light of every light. A CPU mirror of the estimate checks that it is unbiased (`the_hits_estimate_is_unbiased`).
  - **Seeds.** The candidates come from lowbias32 of the ray's index and a seed: the world probes' update count, which turns their rays each update, the traced reflections' frame, and for the split cascades the frame modulo their rays' cycle (`SPLIT_RAY_CYCLE`, 16), so a still view's probes settle on fixed choices as they do on fixed rays.
- **Tested** with 64 punctual lights of mixed ranges, some on cells' edges and one larger than every level's cells: a point anywhere in a light's sphere finds that light among its candidates, once (`a_point_in_a_lights_sphere_finds_it_in_the_light_grid`). mygame's `many_lights.scene` is a low hall of 64 point lights on an 8 × 8 grid, under no sun, whose ceiling and floor take the light of every one.
- **Shadow rays through glass.** An instance with a transparent or additive submesh is not opaque in the scene's structure (see [Ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene)). The hits' shadow rays look up each of its triangles that they meet in the hit table, and pass through a translucent submesh's, at most `HIT_SHADOW_MAX_CANDIDATES` (8), after which the ray is shadowed. Each lets through its transmittance, which the [emitters](#emitters)' pool keeps for each triangle: `(1 - opacity) × lerp(1, albedo, opacity)`, so the light that reaches the hit is dimmed and tinted by the glass, as alpha blending would draw what is behind it, and the order in which the triangles come does not change the product. A triangle without a cell, or whose cell no ray has reached, lets all the light through. So a lantern's red glass dims and reddens its flame, a window lets the sun in, and a decal shadows the wall under it by its opacity. A pane modelled as a closed box counts twice, as alpha blending draws both its faces. The shadow mask and the fog force the triangles opaque.
- **Rays through glass** (`trace_hit_through` in `gi_translucency.hlsli`). The world probes', the split cascades' and the traced reflections' rays trace the instances with a surface and those with a translucent submesh (`RAY_MASK_TRANSLUCENT`), and pass through the translucent triangles in front of their hit. The triangles come in no order, so a ray keeps each that it crosses with its distance, at most `TRACE_MAX_CROSSINGS` (8), after which the next is its hit, and counts the transmittance of those nearer than its hit, or than its end on a miss. The ray's transmittance, as RGB9E5, is kept beside its hit in a buffer of the client's, and the light that the ray brings back takes it: the hits' light pass multiplies its hit's light, or the ambient light of a miss, and the traced reflections' light pass the sky's along a miss. The screen pass leaves a ray that crossed glass its hit's light, since the lit scene behind glass holds the glass's blend already. The world probes' update takes out a listed emitter's radiance times the transmittance, so an emitter behind glass still counts once. So the light behind a red window reaches the probes and the cascades dimmed and red, and a lantern's flame lights the probes through its glass. The glass's own light, the part of its color that alpha blending adds by its opacity, and an additive material's emission, are not added.

  | `gi probe light` | 0.020 → 0.030, 0.030 | 0.027 → 0.042, 0.038 | 0.033 → 0.091, 0.081 | 0.043 → 0.089, 0.088 |
  | `gi split light` | 0.026 → 0.039, 0.038 | 0.053 → 0.086, 0.101 | 0.054 → 0.099, 0.100 | 0.181 → 0.334, 0.359 |
  | `reflections hit light` | 0.006 → 0.007, 0.007 | 0.021 → 0.032, 0.046 | 0.045 → 0.077, 0.078 | 0.087 → 0.162, 0.177 |

  At Ultra, the split cascades' hit light took 0.18 ms with no emitter candidates, 0.33 ms with 4 and 0.40 ms with 8, so about half of the rise is the one shadow ray from every hit toward the light that it picks, through the lanterns' glass, where Sponza's hits cast none toward a punctual light before, and the rest the candidates' searches. So the triangles take a quarter of the candidates.
- **Limits.** One shadow ray, and not one for each light, makes the hits' light noisier where a few lights light a hit. The grid lists lights by their spheres, so a spot light's whole sphere counts. The emissive triangles are drawn by their power, not by their light at the hit, so a hit near a dim emitter draws distant bright ones too. A hit near a small, bright emitter takes its light with much variance, and the probes' and the split cascades' clamps of a ray's luminance take some of it off, as they do for a ray that hits the emitter.

## Emitters

The emitters are the emissive triangles that the hits sample (`lib/engine/render/gi_emitters.odin`, `gi_emitters.hlsl`). A material's emission is what its `evaluate` gives, from a constant, a texture or a graph, so the renderer learns it from [hit shading](ray-scene.md#hit-shading) itself: each frame a batch of rays starts at points of the frame's submeshes, and their materials are evaluated there. The emitters take the frame's first of `MAX_HIT_BATCHES`, before the world probes, on the compute queue. Only the frame's own view runs them; another view's hits sample no triangles, and their irradiance keeps the emitters' light.

- **Discovery.** Each frame, `EMITTER_DISCOVERY_SAMPLES` (8) random points of each of the next `EMITTER_DISCOVERY_SUBMESHES` (128) submeshes that hit shading evaluates, opaque or masked with its hit pass ready, are shaded, and their emitted luminance copied to one of `READBACK_COPIES` (3) readback slots, with the submeshes' keys (`Hit_Entry_Key`: the entity and its submesh). A point that the fallback shaded, or a masked material's hole, counts for nothing. When a slot comes back, `change_emitter`, the one writer of the list, lists a submesh whose brightest point emits at least `EMITTER_MIN_LUMINANCE` (1e-3), and unlists one after `EMITTER_DARK_SCANS` (4) dark scans in a row. A listed submesh that the frame's hit table no longer has, or has with another material or other primitives, is unlisted at once.
- **The list** holds at most `MAX_EMITTER_SUBMESHES` (256) submeshes and `MAX_EMITTER_TRIANGLES` (16384) triangles, and a submesh of at most `MAX_EMITTER_SUBMESH_TRIANGLES` (4096). Each listed submesh has a range of the radiance pool, a cell for each triangle, taken first fit and merged back when it is unlisted.
- **Refresh.** Each frame, up to `EMITTER_REFRESH_JOBS` (4096) triangles have a point shaded, a newly listed submesh's first, then every listed triangle in turn, and each cell blends its triangle's emission in: the mean of its first four points, then a quarter of each new one. The point's ray cone is as wide as the triangle, so its textures give the triangle's mean emission.
- **Transmittance.** Each translucent submesh whose hit pass is ready, of at most `MAX_EMITTER_SUBMESH_TRIANGLES` triangles, has a range of the pool too, which `sync_transmitters`, the one writer of those ranges, gives it while the pool has room and frees when the hit table no longer has it as it was. Each frame, `EMITTER_TRANSMIT_JOBS` (1024) of their triangles in turn have a point shaded, in the same batch, which shades translucent submeshes with their own materials, and their cells blend the transmittance in as an emitter's blend its emission. The hits' shadow rays read them (see [Many lights](#many-lights)). The cook compiles a hit pass for transparent and additive materials for it.
- **Build and scan.** The frame's listed triangles are placed relative to the camera from their instances' poses, so a moving emitter needs nothing more, with their area, their radiance from the pool, and their front, the side that their vertices' normals face. A triangle's power is its luminance times its area, twice for a two-sided material. One group of 1024 threads sums the powers into the distribution, and writes their count and their total, or a count of 0 when the total is 0, which leaves the triangles out.
- **Counted once.** An emitter lit a hit before through the probes' light, since the probes' rays saw it. A hit that samples it too would take its light twice. So the world probes keep the hits' irradiance: the update takes out each ray's listed emitter's radiance, the same that the hits sample, before the clamp, and the hits read it, while the cascades and the pixels read the whole irradiance. The split cascades' rays and the traced reflections' are not the hits' bounce, so they keep the emitters' light. The specular part of a hit takes the picked emitter's light over π, since the probes' light that it takes leaves it out.
- **Checked** in mygame's Cornell box, whose ceiling panel is an emitter, by changing the shader in the running editor: with the emitters' samples and the hits' irradiance both off, both on, and the samples on without the hits' irradiance, the `Gi_Diffuse` view's mean was the same within 0.5% at three poses. The view takes most of its light from what the screen shows and from the panel's own hits there, so it does not tell the three apart; the CPU tests check the estimate and the plan, and the device check shows only that the emitters run without harm.
- **Limits.** A triangle's emission is the mean of its points, so a texture's detail within a triangle is lost, and emission that changes from frame to frame follows within the refresh's cycle. Every material of `Unlit`'s model emits, so it can take the list's room. A translucent triangle whose submesh has no range of the pool, as one of more than `MAX_EMITTER_SUBMESH_TRIANGLES` triangles or past the pool's room, lets all the light through. An emitter that turns on waits for its submesh's turn in the discovery, a frame for each 128 submeshes, and a readback's frames.

## Split cascades

With `Gi_Technique.Split_Cascades` and `Ray_Query`, once the world probes run, the split radiance cascades take the screen cascades' place, after Jack Tollenaar's [*Split Radiance Cascades*](https://www.jacktollenaar.top/articles/split-rc.html) (`lib/engine/render/gi_split.odin`, `gi_split.hlsli`, `gi_split_*.hlsl`). Their probes are in world space, on the surfaces that the screen shows, and keep their light from frame to frame, so the light does not smear as the camera moves, and a surface that comes into view has its light already. Their rays trace the ray scene, so what blocks the light need not be on the screen, and a long interval is not a few steps through the depth buffer. The forward pass reads their light as it reads the screen cascades'.

- **Levels** (`GI_SPLIT_PARAMS`; the numbers here are High's). Each level of detail is a window of `SPLIT_WINDOW` (512) cells on each axis around the camera. The first level's cells are 0.25 m (0.5 m at Low, 0.125 m at Ultra), and each level's twice the one before's; there are 5 (4 at Low). Level L holds the distances from 24 cells of level 0 times 2^L to twice that, by the Chebyshev distance from the camera, so a cell covers about as many pixels at every distance, and the last `SPLIT_BAND` (half) of each level's distances blends into the next. Past the last level, the light is the world probes'.
- **Following the camera.** Each window's first cell is a cell of `World_Position`, from the camera's with an arithmetic shift, so a render-origin rebase moves nothing. It is the camera's cell less 256, a multiple of `SPLIT_SNAP` (16), so the camera is always near the middle. Each frame gives each window's first cell relative to the camera from whole units, and how far it moved.
- **Cascades.** 4 (3 at Low). Cascade k's probe of a cell is the cell's window coordinates shifted right by k, so its cells are 2^k times as large, with `(directions << k)^2` directions folded into an octahedron: 6 across (4 at Low and Medium, 8 at Ultra), so 36 to 2304. A probe's key is its window coordinates, its level and a high bit, in 32 bits. The first cascade's interval is a direction texel's width at the probe, `directions / sqrt(4π)` cells, and each interval is four times as long as the one before.
- **Tables** (`gi_split_table.hlsl`). Each cascade has a fixed count of slots: 16384 for the first (8192 at Low, 32768 at Ultra), and a quarter as many for each after it, but at least 256 (1024 at Ultra, whose last cascade is otherwise full on a slow walk). A hash table for each cascade, of twice as many entries, linearly probed for at most 32, finds a key's slot. Each frame empties the tables, and a thread for each slot moves its key by its window's move and puts it back, or frees it when it left the window or no ray reached it for its cascade's lifetime; a probe keeps its slot, and its light, while it lives. The lifetime is `SPLIT_LIFETIME` (600) frames while the table has room, so a surface that leaves the view has its light when it returns. When a key finds no room, by the stats of a frame or two ago, the lifetime halves each frame, to no less than `SPLIT_MIN_LIFETIME` (8): after a turn, the probes behind the camera were reached a frame ago, and until they are freed the probes in view have no slot. A table that is only nearly full keeps its probes: freeing them early brings each one back new as it returns, which flickered on Ultra, whose third cascade is about 92% full on a slow walk. Below three quarters full, the lifetime grows by one each frame, as fast as a probe ages, so growing frees nothing, and between the two it holds. Then a thread for each traced pixel claims its cell's probe at each cascade and each of its levels with a compare-and-swap, and the first to claim a key takes a free slot. A key that finds no room has no slot, and the merge and the gather take the light from beyond it.
- **Rays** (`gi_split_trace.hlsl`). Each frame traces one ray from a pixel of each tile of 2 × 2 pixels (4 × 4 at Low, 1 × 1 at Ultra), each pixel of a tile in turn. A tile widens while the rays are more than hit shading takes (`MAX_HIT_RAYS`), so Ultra at 4K traces one pixel of each 2 × 1. The ray starts at the pixel's surface, two pixels off it, not at a probe's center, and its direction is uniform over the sphere: the R2 sequence by the frame, offset for each tile, which repeats after `RAY_CYCLE` (16) frames. So a still view's probes settle on a fixed set of rays, as a radiance cascade's do. Rays that never repeat keep moving each bin's mean, and flicker across whole walls. It reaches as far as the last cascade of the pixel's coarser level, and hit shading and the world probes' [light and screen passes](#world-probes) give its hit's light.
- **The deposit** (`gi_split_deposit.hlsl`) splits each ray by its hit's distance, at each of the pixel's levels: the cascade whose interval holds the hit counts the ray and adds the hit's light, in its bin of the ray's direction at the probe of the pixel's cell, and each cascade before it counts the ray as passing through. A ray past the last cascade passes through them all. The light is pre-exposed and added in fixed point, in units of 1 / `SPLIT_RADIANCE_SCALE` (1024) up to `SPLIT_RADIANCE_MAX` (64), with integer atomics and a dither that keeps dim light's mean. A back face within four pixels means that the ray started inside geometry, and the ray counts for nothing.
- **The merge** (`gi_split_merge.hlsl`), one pass for each cascade from the last to the first, a group for each 64 bins of each active probe, dispatched by the counts that the tables wrote. Each bin takes its frame's rays into its history: the share that passed through and the mean light of the rest, blended by the rays against the history's, which decays by `1 - 1 / history_frames` (12) with each frame that rays reach the bin, and holds at most `SPLIT_MAX_COUNT` (256) rays. A new bin starts as what the world probes see along its direction from the probe, occluders and all, with nothing passing through, and counts as half a ray, so its first few rays outweigh the guess; starting it as passing through let a probe with no rays yet see through walls. A bin that no ray reaches keeps its history, so the next ray moves it by a ray's share of `history_frames` rays and does not replace it; a bin that few rays reach therefore follows a change more slowly, in proportion. Then it adds the light that passes through it from the next cascade: the four finer directions that it splits into, at the eight probes around it, three to one on each axis, each by its confidence, `rays / (rays + 8)`, whose rays decay as a bin's do, only in frames that rays reach the probe, so a probe that goes out of view does not lose its weight and jump back when it returns; and each of those bins by whether rays reached it, as one that none did holds only a new bin's guess. Where no such probe has light, and beyond the last cascade, it is the world probes' radiance along the direction from where the bin's interval ends. From the probe's center, it would hold again the occluders that the cascades already traced: with both, Sponza's interiors had a quarter to two fifths of the sky that rays from their surfaces see. The first cascade's probes also write their tile of an atlas, 6 × 6 octahedral texels with a border, as the world probes' are: the cosine-weighted mean of their light around each direction (`direction_weight`).
- **The gather** (`gi_split_gather.hlsl`): each pixel's diffuse light is the atlas's at the first cascade's eight probes around it, along its normal, and its specular light the second cascade's merged light along its reflection, blended between the four nearest directions. Each probe counts by its trilinear weight, by how much it is in front of the surface, as the world probes do, and by its confidence, and the levels blend by the distance. The world probes' light counts a hundredth as much as one probe everywhere, so a place that no probe has reached yet fades in from it, and it counts fully past the last level. Where the world probes have no light, the specular light is the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) at the pixel's roughness. No history follows the pixels.
- **Memory.** The tables, bins and atlas take about 19 MB at Low, 49 MB at Medium, 99 MB at High and 418 MB at Ultra, made for the level when the split first runs at it. The rays' buffers take 84 bytes for each traced pixel, with hit shading's: 74 MB at High at 1440p. The diffuse and specular histories are still made, though the split does not read them. Each [view](frame.md#views) of a frame keeps its own tables, bins and atlas, at its own level.
- **Debug view and stats.** With the `Gi_Probes` [debug view](post-processing.md#debug-views), the gather also draws each pixel's probe into a texture of its own, `Gi_Frame.probe_view`, not into the light, which the lit scene keeps for the next frames' rays. After the merge, the tables' counters are copied to their [readback ring](../gpu/queues-streaming-readback.md#readback), and the end of the frame takes the newest copy that came back, as the [culling stats](gpu-scene.md#occlusion-culling) are taken: `gi_split_stats` gives each cascade's probes that rays reached, its slots, and the keys that found no room, a frame or two ago, or none when the split cascades did not run last frame, or the frame did not execute. The editor's Resource Monitor shows them.
- **Waiting.** The split cascades start once their pipelines are ready, and run when the world probes run this frame and hit shading has room for their batch. Each takes its turn at hit shading (`reserve_hit_shading`) with its other uploads, before its levels or its frame move on, so a frame whose turn fails changes nothing. Until then, the frame takes the screen cascades. A frame that takes them after a frame that had the split cascades logs a warning with the reason, since the whole frame's indirect light changes.
- **Limits.** Light leaks where a wall is thinner than a cell, since the rays from both of its sides reach the same probes. The ray scene has no instance sets, patch surfaces or particles, so the rays pass through foliage, terrain and water. Each hit that the screen does not show casts a shadow ray toward every directional light and toward the light that it picks (see [Many lights](#many-lights)), so the hits' light costs most at Ultra.

## ReSTIR GI

With `Gi_Technique.Restir` and `Ray_Query`, once the world probes run, ReSTIR GI takes the screen cascades' place. It follows Ouyang et al., [*ReSTIR GI: Path Resampling for Real-Time Path Tracing*](https://research.nvidia.com/publication/2021-06_restir-gi-path-resampling-real-time-path-tracing), HPG 2021 (`lib/engine/render/gi_restir.odin`, `gi_restir.hlsli`, `gi_restir_*.hlsl`). Each pixel traces its own ray through the ray scene, and resampling reuses the light that last frame's rays and the neighbours' rays found, so each pixel has the light of many rays for the cost of one. The Ultra preset chooses it where the device has `Ray_Query`, in the split cascades' place; High keeps the split cascades. The forward pass reads its light as it reads the screen cascades'.

- **Levels** (`GI_RESTIR_PARAMS`; the numbers here are Ultra's). One ray from each tile of 1 × 1 pixels (2 × 2 at Low to High). A tile widens while the rays are more than hit shading takes (`MAX_HIT_RAYS`, `restir_fit`), as the split cascades' do, so Ultra at 4K traces one pixel of each 2 × 1 and at 8K of each 4 × 2. Each pixel of a tile is traced in turn (`split_rotation`). No preset below Ultra chooses ReSTIR GI: a quality does, or the settings file's `quality.gi = Restir`.
- **Rays** (`gi_restir_trace.hlsl`). The ray starts at the pixel's surface, `RESTIR_BIAS` (2) pixels off it, new each frame, and reaches at most 128 m (32 to 64 m below Ultra). Its direction is cosine-weighted around the normal, or where the pixel has a cell of the [ray guide](#ray-guiding), drawn from the guide with a chance of `guide_mix` (0.35), and the trace stores the pdf of that mixture at the ray's direction. It passes through glass, as the split cascades' rays do. Hit shading and the world probes' [light and screen passes](#world-probes) give its hit's light, with a light that each hit picks by resampled importance sampling (see [Many lights](#many-lights)), a new choice each frame, and the world probes' light at the hit, which carries the bounces past the first.
- **Reservoirs.** Each tile keeps a reservoir in three history textures at its count of tiles:
  - the sample point relative to the camera and its weight `W` (`RGBA32_Float`);
  - the light that the point sends back, and `M`, the count of samples that the reservoir chose it from (`RGBA16_Float`);
  - the point's normal, octahedral, the view depth of the surface that the reservoir is for, and the sample's age, negative for a sample at infinity (`RGBA16_Float`).

  A ray past its reach is a sample at infinity along its direction, with the world probes' radiance from that direction, or the ambient light's without them. The target of the resampling is the light's luminance times the cosine toward the sample, so a reservoir's diffuse light at a surface is its light times the cosine times `W`, over π.
- **The temporal pass** (`gi_restir_temporal.hlsl`) makes this frame's sample from the ray, with the weight of its target over the pdf that the trace stored, and its light clamped to a luminance of `RESTIR_RADIANCE_MAX` (4), pre-exposed. That is a quarter of the world probes' limit: a reservoir keeps a rare bright sample for many frames, and at the probes' limit the light was strewn with sparks. The sample's light over that pdf teaches the [ray guide](#ray-guiding). Then it merges last frame's reservoir at the place that the velocity gives. That reservoir counts while its surface's depth is within `RESTIR_DEPTH_TOLERANCE` (10%) of where this point was last frame, and while its sample is younger than `max_age` (50 frames). It counts as at most `temporal_max_m` (20) samples, so the light follows a change within about that many frames. Its point moves back by the camera's motion, which comes from `World_Position`, so a render-origin rebase moves nothing.
- **The spatial pass** (`gi_restir_spatial.hlsl`) merges `spatial_samples` (6; 3 at Low) neighbours within `spatial_radius` (30; 12 at Low) reservoirs, on a disk that turns each frame.
  - A neighbour counts while its surface's depth is within 10% of the pixel's and its normal within a cosine of `RESTIR_NORMAL_COS` (0.9).
  - Its sample is reconnected to the pixel's surface: its weight `W` is a density in the neighbour's solid angle, so it is multiplied by the Jacobian of the change in solid angle, the pixel's over the neighbour's. A neighbour whose Jacobian is past `RESTIR_JACOBIAN_MAX` (8) either way does not count, as one of the two surfaces then sees the sample at a grazing angle, and neither does a sample whose surface faces away from the pixel.
  - At Ultra, a shadow ray to each neighbour's sample keeps the light from leaking through a thin wall between them. It passes through glass, as the rays that found the samples did.
  - The neighbours count as at most `spatial_max_m` (80) samples together. The weights are the plain share of the samples, not the unbiased weights of the paper, which is a little dark at the edges of surfaces.
  - The result does not feed next frame's temporal pass, so neighbouring reservoirs do not grow alike.
- **The resolve** (`gi_restir_resolve.hlsl`) gives each pixel the diffuse light of its reservoir, or with larger tiles of the four nearest reservoirs, each by how near it is and whether its surface is the pixel's, evaluated at the pixel's own surface without a Jacobian. Its specular light is the world probes' radiance along the reflection, or the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) at its roughness; the [traced reflections](lighting-and-shadows.md#ray-traced-reflections), where they run, are in front of it. Both blend into the diffuse and specular histories by the velocity, at most `history_frames` (10) frames, as the screen cascades' gather does, so a frame that falls back to the screen cascades reads them as its own. The view model's pixels take the light around the camera.
- **The filter** (`gi_restir_filter.hlsl`): `filter_passes` (2; 3 at Low) à-trous steps of a 5 × 5 kernel, 1, 2 and 4 pixels apart, which stop at edges by the view depth, the normal and the luminance. The history keeps the light before the filter, so the blur does not build up from frame to frame.
- **Memory.** The reservoirs take 32 bytes a tile in each half of their history and 32 more for the spatial pass's: 199 MB at 1080p and 354 MB at 1440p at Ultra (`restir_bytes`), and the ray guide 29.9 MB (see [Ray guiding](#ray-guiding)). The rays' buffers take 84 bytes a ray, as the split cascades' do. The reservoirs are the graph's [history textures](../gpu/render-graph.md#render-graph), so a resize, a camera cut or a frame that did not run them starts them over, and the view keeps no storage of its own for them.
- **Waiting.** ReSTIR GI starts once its pipelines are ready, and runs when the world probes run this frame and hit shading has room for its batch, which it takes in the split cascades' place. Until then, the frame takes the screen cascades, not the split cascades, whose storage would stay until the view's size changes. A frame that takes them after a frame that had ReSTIR GI logs a warning with the reason.
- **Limits.** The ray scene has no instance sets, patch surfaces or particles, as for the split cascades. Below Ultra, the spatial pass casts no shadow rays, so light can leak between neighbours across a thin occluder. A ray that starts inside geometry, by a back face within four pixels, counts for nothing, and a reservoir with no samples left takes the world probes' light in the resolve. On Sponza's lower gallery walls such reservoirs make whole patches, with the hard edges of a texture, which look like masked decals just off the wall that the rays take as opaque; the split cascades lose the same rays on the arches. The forward pass scales the diffuse light by the ambient occlusion, which the rays already trace, so contact shadows are a little darker than they should be. Next to the split cascades, the light is a little darker; the clamp of the samples' light is a quarter of the world probes', and was not measured against the reference.
- **Measured.** In Intel's Sponza with its curtains on an RTX 5090, in the editor's viewport of 1051 × 418 at Ultra, the passes named `gi restir` take about 1.0 ms a frame, its hit shading and hit light 0.7 ms of it. The frame took 2.9 ms against 3.3 ms with the split cascades. In a viewport of 1280 × 720, with no anti-aliasing, its passes and the world probes' take 0.90 ms at High and 2.29 ms at Ultra, against the split cascades' 0.99 and 2.50 ms, and in 40 screenshots of a still view the p99 of each pixel's largest step is 14.5 codes at High and 9.7 at Ultra, against the split cascades' 1.7 and 1.1. After 10 s turned 180° away, the first screenshot back differs from the settled view by no more than its own noise, as the world probes keep the light behind the camera. It is not yet in the [comparison](#comparison)'s accuracy table.

## Ray guiding

With ReSTIR GI, a share of each ray's chances goes to a guide: a direction drawn from where the light at the pixel's place has come from, in place of the cosine around its normal (`lib/engine/render/gi_restir_guide.odin`, `gi_restir_guide.hlsli`, `gi_restir_guide.hlsl`). A ray that finds the bright light more often is a better candidate for the [reservoirs](#restir-gi), so the light is less noisy for the same rays. The guide follows the directional quadtrees of Müller et al., [*Practical Path Guiding for Efficient Light-Transport Simulation*](https://studios.disneyresearch.com/2017/06/19/practical-path-guiding-for-efficient-light-transport-simulation/) (EGSR 2017), with the world probes' grid in place of their spatial tree, as Dittebrandt et al. keep a directional quadtree for each cell of a grid in real time ([*Temporal Sample Reuse for Next Event Estimation and Path Guiding for Real-Time Path Tracing*](https://cg.ivd.kit.edu/publications/2020/tsr_nee_pg_rtpt/paper.pdf), EGSR 2020). Only ReSTIR GI's rays are guided (see [Other rays](#other-rays)).

- **Cells.** A cell is the space nearest one [world probe](#world-probes), at the probe's storage, in each of the probes' first `guide_levels` (2) levels (`GI_RESTIR_PARAMS`): cells of 1 m and 2 m at Medium to Ultra. So the cells follow the camera as the probes do, and a render-origin rebase moves nothing. A ray takes the finest level whose box holds its pixel's surface; a pixel outside them takes cosine-weighted rays only. Each cell keeps its key, the low bits of its probe's world cell. A cell whose key is not its probe's this frame, as one that its level's box moved over, starts again, so a frame that did not run ReSTIR GI, and so did not see the box move, leaves nothing stale.
- **Leaves and the quadtree.** Each cell has 8 × 8 leaves over the probes' octahedron, each the light that reaches the cell through it, integrated over its solid angle, and the sums of their 2 × 2 blocks above them: 4 × 4, 2 × 2 and the root, a quadtree three levels deep. The world probes' tiles are 6 × 6, which halves only once, so the guide keeps leaves of its own, and the probes' tiles are its prior (see the build).
- **The walk** (`guide_sample`). A ray walks down from the root, and takes each child by its light times a bound of the cosine at the pixel's surface over the child: the largest cosine at its four corners and its center, plus `GUIDE_HORIZON` (0.02). So a node below the surface's horizon is seldom taken: without the bound, about half of a sphere's guided rays would leave below the surface. In the leaf, it takes a point uniformly in the octahedron's square. Its pdf in solid angle (`guide_pdf`) is the walk's chance of the leaf, over the leaf's area of the square, a sixteenth of its 2 × 2, over the solid angle per area there, the cube of the direction's L1 length: 1 at the octahedron's corners and up to 5.2 between them. A walk is twelve nodes' weights, each five unfolded points.
- **The mixture.** A ray whose pixel has a cell takes the guide's direction with a chance of `guide_mix` (0.35), and a cosine-weighted one otherwise. The trace stores the mixture's pdf at the ray's direction, `(1 - mix) cos / π + mix pdf_guide`, in a buffer beside the hits, 4 bytes a ray, and the temporal pass weighs the sample by its target over that pdf, where it took π times its luminance. The cosine's share keeps every direction that the cosine reaches possible, so a guide that is wrong costs noise and never light. A CPU mirror of the walk checks that the guide's pdf integrates to 1 over a grid of latitude and longitude, and that the mixture's estimate is the light, within four standard errors, at mixes of 0, 0.5 and 1, with a guide learned from the light and with one that points away from it (`the_guided_estimate_is_unbiased`). The mixture's cost: a cosine ray that finds light where the guide has learned little weighs up to 1 / (1 - mix) times what it did. At 0.5, that doubled the sparks of light that a few rays find through openings, and the nave's lit view stepped more than without the guide; at 0.35 it steps less (see the measurements).
- **Learning** (`guide_deposit`). In the temporal pass, each ray adds its sample's luminance, clamped and pre-exposed, over the mixture's pdf, at least `GUIDE_PDF_FLOOR` (0.1), to the leaf of its direction in its pixel's cell, and counts itself in the cell. The sums are integer atomics in units of 1/64, at most 64 a ray, with a dither. A ray that started inside geometry, or that left below its surface's horizon, adds nothing. So each pixel's ray teaches every pixel of its cell: the light that one pixel found is shared with the pixels around it, as Lu et al.'s voxel path guiding shares it across shading points ([*Voxel Path Guiding*](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10584751)).
- **The build** (`gi_restir_guide.hlsl`), a group for each cell, a thread for each leaf, runs after the world probes' update and before the trace, every frame.
  - **Normalized.** Each leaf's sum over the cell's count of rays is an estimate of its light, which blends into the leaf's learned light as a running mean of at most `GUIDE_MAX_CONFIDENCE` (256) rays, so the guide follows a change in the light.
  - **The prior.** The leaf that the walk sees is its learned light blended with the world probe's radiance through the leaf, times the leaf's solid angle, by the rays that the cell has learned from against `GUIDE_PRIOR_RAYS` (32): a cell that no ray has reached, as one that just came into view, takes the probe's light, so the probes share their light with it before its own rays do.
  - **The floor.** Then `GUIDE_FLOOR` (10%) of the cell's light is spread evenly over its leaves, so the walk reaches every leaf, and a cell with no light at all takes each leaf's solid angle: uniform over the sphere. The leaves are summed into the quadtree, and the sums are emptied for this frame's rays.
- **Memory.** 640 bytes a cell for its learned light, its quadtree, its count of rays and its key, and 272 bytes for its sums: 29.9 MB for two levels (`restir_guide_bytes`), each [view](frame.md#views)'s own, made for the count of levels when ReSTIR GI first runs at it. The pdfs take 4 bytes a ray beside the rays' other buffers.
- **Debug view.** With the `Gi_Probes` [debug view](post-processing.md#debug-views), a pass after the filter cuts the screen into blocks of 64 pixels, and draws in each the walk at its center pixel: the 8 × 8 leaves of its cell, each as hot as the walk's chance of it at that pixel's normal, from blue at a sixteenth of an even share to red at sixteen times it, on a log scale, with the normal as a white dot. A block's border is its cell in a color of its own, as bright as the rays that the cell has learned from, and a block whose center has no surface or no cell is grey.
- **Measured** in Intel's Sponza with its curtains on an RTX 5090, at the Ultra preset, in the editor's scene view at 1280 × 720, with `noTemporalAa` and `noClouds`, at the [world probes'](#world-probes) four poses. ### Other rays

The engine's other rays were weighed for the guide, and are not guided:

- **The world probes' rays** are a spherical Fibonacci set that turns each update, and the update blends each texel's narrow lobe of them, with their distances for the probes' visibility. A model of one probe under a window of 12° that is 80 times as bright as its sky, with the update's lobes, gives these numbers: with 32 of its 64 rays from a guide that matches the light and each ray weighed by the mixture's density, the irradiance's error per update falls from 30% to 6%, and the radiance's from 29% to 8%, but 23% of the texels' distance lobes take fewer than half of their rays, which makes the probes' visibility noisier where it matters most, toward the dark. The update already averages about 33 updates, so its error is about an eighth of each update's. Guiding them would need the distances and the back faces from the Fibonacci rays alone, a pdf in the update for every ray, and the guide's cells before ReSTIR GI's trace; it is the next candidate.
- **The split cascades' rays** repeat a fixed cycle of 16 frames, so that a still view settles, and each bin of each probe needs rays in its own direction to tell how much light passes through it. A guide would move the cycle with the light, and leave the dark bins with rays too few to know that they are dark.
- **The traced reflections' rays** follow the GGX lobe of each pixel's roughness. A guide over the sphere alone would draw rays outside the lobe, where they count for little; it would take the product of the lobe and the guide.

## Voxel cones

With `Gi_Technique.Voxel_Cones`, voxel cone tracing takes the screen cascades' place. It follows Crassin et al., *Interactive Indirect Illumination Using Voxel Cone Tracing*, on a clipmap of 3D textures around the camera, as NVIDIA's VXGI keeps it, not on a sparse octree (`lib/engine/render/gi_voxel.odin`, `gi_voxel.hlsli`, `gi_voxel_*.hlsl`, `material_voxelize.hlsli`). The rasterizer voxelizes the scene, and the voxels light themselves. So the technique needs no `Ray_Query`, and it sees what is off the screen. No preset chooses it: a quality does, or the settings file's `quality.gi = Voxel_Cones`. The forward pass reads its light as it reads the screen cascades' light.

- **Levels** (`GI_VOXEL_PARAMS`; the numbers here are High's). There are 5 levels (6 at Ultra). Each level is a box of `res`³ voxels, where `res` is 128 (64 at Low, 96 at Medium). Level 0's voxels are 0.125 m (0.25 m at Low), and each level's voxels are twice as large as the level before. So High's boxes are 16 m to 256 m across. Each voxel size is a power of two of meters. A box's first cell is a cell of `World_Position`, from the camera's position with an arithmetic shift, so a render-origin rebase moves nothing.
- **Following the camera.** A box moves when the camera is more than `VOXEL_SNAP` (8) of its voxels from its middle. Its first cell after the move is a multiple of 8, so a box covers whole voxels of the next level. Each level's storage is toroidal: a voxel's texel is its cell modulo `res`, so the textures wrap. The cones sample them with a sampler that repeats, so the filtering works across the seam. A box that moves holds no surfaces until it voxelizes again, which it does in the same frame.
- **Storage.** Each level has five 3D textures of its own, which the renderer owns:
  - four `R32_Uint` textures, for the surfaces' albedo, normal, emission and coverage mask;
  - one `RGBA16_Float` texture, for the light that the voxels send out, premultiplied by their opacity.

  That is 24 bytes a voxel: 30 MiB at Low, 101 MiB at Medium, 240 MiB at High and 288 MiB at Ultra (`voxel_bytes`). The textures are made for the level the first time the voxel cones run at it. Each view has its own levels, boxes and turn, at its own level.
- **Voxelization** (`material_voxelize.hlsli`, the `Voxelize` material pass).
  - **When.** A level voxelizes when its box moved. One more level voxelizes each frame in turn, so a moving object is in every level within as many frames as there are levels.
  - **Views.** `begin_gi` decides which levels voxelize, before the scene's views are made. Each level that voxelizes has three orthographic views, one along each axis, after the shadow views. They cull and draw the scene's opaque bins as any view does, instance sets and patch surfaces included.
  - **Passes.** A compute pass first empties the level's surfaces. Then one render pass with no attachments draws the views at twice `res` across, so that a pixel is half a voxel. It uses the prepass's vertex or mesh stages and each material's `ps_voxelize`.
  - **Fragments.** `ps_voxelize` evaluates the material and offers the fragment to the voxel that holds it, with one atomic for each texture. The albedo is what `shade_indirect` gives from a white surrounding, as hit shading takes it, in 8 bits a channel; the normal is folded into the octahedron, in 12 bits a coordinate. Each is led by the fragment's key, a hash of the quarter of a voxel that it is in (`voxel_key`), and `InterlockedMax` keeps the largest, so a voxel holds one fragment's albedo and normal, and the same fragment's whatever the order the fragments come in. The emission is the largest RGB9E5 word, and the mask takes the fragment's half-size cube with `InterlockedOr`.
  - **Why one fragment.** Crassin and Green (*Octree-Based Sparse Voxelization Using the GPU Hardware Rasterizer*) keep running means in a compare-and-swap loop. Those take the fragments in the order that they come, so a level that voxelized the same surfaces again held slightly different means each time. With a level voxelizing each frame in turn, a still view of Sponza flickered at a spread p99 of about 1.7. The order-free voxels leave it at 0, and voxelizing again changes nothing where nothing moved.
  - **Coverage.** A fragment counts only in the view along its triangle's dominant axis, which it takes from the derivatives of its position. That view shows the triangle at its largest, and there the triangle's depth changes by less than a voxel across a pixel. So no triangle needs a projection of its own, and no geometry shader is needed. Where the device has `Conservative_Raster`, a triangle smaller than a pixel still marks its voxel. A conservative rasterizer's fragments past a triangle's edges extrapolate its position, and in a view that sees the triangle edge-on, that position lands far from it: before the dominant-axis rule, Sponza's curtains scattered voxels through the open nave.
  - **Level of detail.** On the mesh shader path, a voxelization view (`VIEW_VOXELS`) takes each mesh's level whose error is at most half a voxel, at every distance (`VOXEL_LOD_NEAR`), from all of the mesh's meshlets, not from those that `select_meshlets.hlsl` picked for the camera. No meshlet is culled by its normal cone, which `cull_eye` tests against the camera. At the camera's level of detail, Sponza's voxelization took 22 ms a frame on an RTX 5090, as millions of small triangles reached the same coarse voxels and waited on each other's compare and swap. At the voxels' level of detail, it took 0.6 ms, and with one atomic for each texture, 0.2 ms.
- **Opacity.** The mask's texel records which of the voxel's eight half-size cubes a fragment covered. The voxel's opacity is the largest share of its cross-section that those cubes cover along any axis. A wall through a voxel fills half of its cubes but blocks every ray across it, so the share of the cubes would let half of the light through. Where a surface covers only part of a coarse voxel's cross-section, light gets through.
- **Injection** (`gi_voxel_inject.hlsl`). It runs on the compute queue for every level every frame, finest level first.
  - **The light.** Each voxel's light is the sum of three terms, times its opacity:
    - its emission;
    - its albedo over π, times the light of every directional light and of up to 16 punctual lights whose spheres reach the outermost box (`probe_lights`);
    - its albedo, times the light that bounces to it.
  - **The normal.** A light falls off with the cosine to the voxel's normal.
  - **Shadows.** Each light's shadow is a narrow cone toward it through the levels' opacity, out of the last box or to the light. So a voxel off the screen is shadowed as one on the screen is, with no shadow map and no ray.
  - **The bounce.** It is a 90° cone along the normal through the next level's light. This frame's injection has not reached that level yet, so its light is last frame's. Light therefore bounces once more each frame, and no second copy of the light is kept. The last level's bounce is the ambient light.
  - **Covered voxels.** A voxel that a finer level covers is left to the filter.
- **The filter** (`gi_voxel_filter.hlsl`). It runs for each level but the last. Each voxel of the next level that the level covers takes the eight voxels that it holds. Its opacity is what a ray across them sees: along each axis, the two voxels of each of the four columns block one after the other, and the axis whose columns block the most counts. Its light keeps the eight voxels' mean color. So a cone as wide as a coarse level's voxels sees the finer level's surfaces, filtered, and a wall one voxel thick still blocks it. With the mean opacity, a wall's opacity halved at each level. In Sponza, the filter's opacity took the share of the sky that the voxel cones see from 7.7 to 20 times the reference's (see [Comparison](#comparison)) to 4.0 to 8.4 times, and taking the last level for cones wider than its voxels, which the march had taken for past the clipmap, took it to 1.4 to 3.5 times at six of seven poses, and 0.34 at the last.
- **The gather** (`gi_voxel_gather.hlsl`).
  - **Cones.** Each pixel traces diffuse cones around its normal: 6 (4 at Low, 9 at Ultra), one along the normal and rings around it, each weighted by its cosine (`voxel_cones`). The rings lean 55° from the normal with a half angle of 30° (32° and 64° with 22.5° at Ultra), so no cone's edge lies along the surface, where a wide cone would sample the surface's own voxels. It also traces one narrow cone along its reflection.
  - **The march.** Each cone starts a voxel and a half of the point's level off the surface, past the trilinear reach of the point's own voxel, and marches front to back, in steps of half its width. Each step samples the level whose voxels are as wide as the cone, blended with the next level, or the last level once the cone is wider than its voxels, and corrects the sample's opacity for the step's length. A cone stops when 95% of its light is blocked, or past the last box. The ambient light fills the share of a diffuse cone that is not blocked, and the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) at the pixel's roughness the reflection cone's.
  - **Noise and history.** The cones turn about the normal by a noise of the pixel's that stays from frame to frame. With a turn that moved each frame, a still view's spread p99 was 5.0, since the histories hold too few frames to average it; with this one it was 1.7, and 0 once the voxels were order-free. Both lights blend into their histories by the velocity, as in the screen cascades' gather, over at most `history_frames` (8) frames.
  - **The view model.** Its pixels take the light around the camera.
  - **Reflection probes.** Each face of a capture gathers the cones at its own pixels, between its prepass and its forward pass, on the graphics queue (`add_voxel_capture_gather`, the `capture` branch of `gi_voxel_gather.hlsl`). Its points are relative to the probe, and `Gi_Constants.origin` moves them to the camera's frame, where the levels are. It keeps no history. A point outside every level takes the ambient light along its normal and the sky's cubemap along its reflection, and a probe outside every level has no gather (see [Reflection probes](lighting-and-shadows.md#reflection-probes)).
- **Debug view.** With the `Gi_Probes` [debug view](post-processing.md#debug-views), the gather also draws into `Gi_Frame.probe_view` the albedo of the first voxel that each pixel's view ray meets.
- **Waiting.** The voxel cones start when their pipelines are ready. Like every technique, they also need the screen cascades' pipelines. Until then, the frame takes the screen cascades. A frame that takes the screen cascades after a frame that had the voxel cones logs a warning with the reason. A material from before the `Voxelize` pass draws nothing in it.
- **Limits.**
  - A surface thinner than a voxel lets light through.
  - A voxel's light is the same in every direction. So a cone that grazes a wall can take the light of the wall's other side. The coarse levels leak the most.
  - Transparent surfaces, particles, ribbons and impostors are not voxelized.
  - The injection's shadows are as coarse as the voxels, and soft.
  - The reflection's cone is narrow, so on a glossy floor its light shows the finest level's voxels as squares, which the screen and split cascades' coarser directions blur away.
  - A voxel keeps one fragment's albedo and normal, not their mean, so a voxel where surfaces of different colors or facing different ways meet takes one of them.
  - Moving objects reach the coarse levels a few frames late, and the light that they block changes a few frames after that.
  - The pixels' light at Sponza's poses is 1.3 to 1.8 times the reference's at most poses, but 3.4 times in an enclosed gallery and 0.4 times on a wall in the open (see [Comparison](#comparison)).
