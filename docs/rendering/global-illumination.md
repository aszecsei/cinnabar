# Global illumination

The global illumination is screen-space radiance cascades, on the compute queue after the prepass (`lib/engine/render/gi.odin`). It replaces the constant ambient light with the light that reaches each pixel: the sky's, which nearby surfaces block, and the light that those surfaces reflect, which bounces on from frame to frame. The `Global_Illumination` settings set it, and it is on by default. Without its pipelines, or with `off`, the indirect light is the `Environment`'s ambient light. With the quality's `Split_Cascades` technique and `Ray_Query`, a grid of [world probes](#world-probes) gives the light that the screen does not see, and the [split cascades](#split-cascades) take the screen cascades' place. With `Voxel_Cones`, on any device, the [voxel cones](#voxel-cones) take their place.

- **Cascades** (`gi_common.hlsli`): the [quality](quality.md#levels)'s level gives their count, their probe spacing, their directions, the rays' steps and the histories' length (`GI_PARAMS`); the numbers here are High's. Five cascades. Cascade k has a probe every `spacing << k` pixels (8 to 128), on the surface at its tile's center, with `(directions << k)^2` directions over the sphere (4 across: 16 to 4096), folded into an octahedron. Each cascade therefore has about a quarter of the screen's pixels as texels, and all of them are one `RGBA16_Float` texture, each cascade in its own rows. Ultra's probes are as far apart as High's, with 8 directions across (64 to 16384), so each of its cascades has as many texels as the screen has pixels: 8 across at half the spacing would be four times as many, past the texture's size at 4K. A view so large that the cascades would not fit a texture 16384 texels on a side has its last cascades left out (`gi_fit`).
- **Intervals.** Cascade k's rays trace from one distance to the next, each interval four times as long as the one before. The first is `GI_INTERVAL_SCALE` (2) probe spacings long at the probe's depth with 4 directions across, and longer with more, as each direction is narrower: 4 spacings at Ultra, whose five cascades reach as far as six would with 4. So a cascade covers about as many pixels at every depth. For a surface 3 m away in a view 720 pixels high, the last cascade ends about 30 m away.
- **The rays** (`gi_trace.hlsl`) march their interval in the level's steps (8) through the depth buffer, from the probe's surface moved off it by two pixels. Where a step passes behind a surface by less than its thickness, the ray stops, and takes that surface's light from last frame's lit scene, where it was then. A ray that reaches its interval's end lets through what lies beyond. A ray that leaves the screen does too, or with the world probes takes their light where it left, and nothing from beyond. The rays' directions within their texels and their steps' offsets come from noise that moves every frame.
- **The merge** (`gi_merge.hlsl`), one pass for each cascade from the last to the first, adds to each texel what gets through it: the next cascade's light in the four finer directions that it splits into, at the four probes around it. Probes on the same surface count fully: their surfaces are within 3% of the view depth from the texel's tangent plane. Others count very little, and background has none. Beyond the last cascade, and where no probe of the next cascade has a surface, the light is the world probes' light from that direction at the probe's surface, or without them the ambient light's (see [Ambient light](sky.md#ambient-light)).
- **The gather** (`gi_gather.hlsl`) gives each pixel its diffuse light, the cosine-weighted mean of the first cascade's directions around its normal (`direction_weight`), where each direction counts by its solid angle too with 6 or more across; with 4, the texels are so large that the light and the cosine vary across each, and the cosine at the center alone gives the nearer mean, and its specular light, the second cascade's along its reflection, blended between the nearest directions. Each comes from the four probes around the pixel, weighted as in the merge and by how alike their normals are. Both blend into history textures by the velocity, at most the level's `history_frames` (12) frames. A history whose depth is not where this frame's point was last frame starts over. A pixel that no probe matches takes the world probes' light at its point, or the ambient light around its normal and the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) along its reflection, at the mip for its roughness from the prepass (`Frame.roughness`).
- **The lit scene** (`gi_lit.hlsl`): after the world pass, a half-resolution copy of the scene without its pre-exposure is kept as a history texture for the next frame's rays. It holds the indirect light too, so light bounces once more each frame. It also holds the fog in front of each surface.
- **The forward pass** reads the diffuse and specular light as `Indirect`, scaled by the ambient occlusion. The view model's pixels take the light around the camera: the world probes', or the ambient light and the sky's cubemap.
- **Limits:** without the world probes, the rays see only what is on the screen, so light from behind the camera or from hidden surfaces is missing, and a surface's light changes as other surfaces come into view. Specular light is as coarse as the second cascade's 64 directions (256 at Ultra), so reflections are blurred. Thin objects can block too much, since the depth buffer has no thickness.

## World probes

With `Gi_Technique.Split_Cascades` and `Ray_Query`, a grid of probes in world space traces the [ray scene](ray-scene.md#ray-scene) for the light that the screen does not see (`lib/engine/render/gi_probes.odin`, `gi_probes.hlsli`, `gi_probe_*.hlsl`). The cascades read it where they took the ambient light, so the forward pass's `Indirect` and `shade_indirect` do not change. A device without `Ray_Query`, or with `CINNABAR_DISABLE_FEATURES=Ray_Query`, has the cascades alone (see [Quality](quality.md#hardware-caps)).

- **Levels.** The probes are clipmap levels around the camera, each a box of `GI_PROBE_GRID` (32 × 16 × 32) probes. The first level's probes are 1 m apart (2 m at Low), and each level's twice as far apart as the one before; the quality's level gives the count, 2 to 4 (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS`). Every spacing is a power of two of meters, so it divides the render origin's 1024 m cell.
- **Following the camera.** Each level keeps its first probe's world cell, from the camera's `World_Position` with an arithmetic shift, so a negative position floors. The box moves when the camera is more than `GI_PROBE_RECENTER` (2) probes from its middle. A probe's storage is its world cell modulo the grid, so the box's move keeps every probe where it was, and only the probes whose cells it moved over reset: the reset pass sets their states to no updates, and the schedule updates them first. A teleport resets the whole level. Each frame gives each level's first probe relative to the camera from whole units, so it is exact anywhere.
- **Rebasing.** The levels follow the camera's world position, not the render origin, so a rebase moves no probe and resets none. The shaders add the camera's offset from the origin when they trace, as the shadow mask does.
- **Storage.** A probe keeps a tile of 6 × 6 octahedral texels with a one-texel border, in two `RGBA16_Float` textures: the radiance that reaches it from each direction with the mean distance of its rays' hits, and the cosine-weighted mean radiance around each direction with the mean squared distance. A buffer holds each probe's state: its updates, up to 255, and whether it is inside geometry. They are the renderer's own, made for the level's count of levels, about 16 MB a level.
- **Updates.** Each frame updates the level's budget of probes, 1024 to 8192 (`GI_PROBE_PARAMS`): first those that wait since their reset, the finest level's first, then each level's share of the rest in turn. The passes run on the compute queue, after the prepass and before the cascades:
  - **Trace** (`gi_probe_trace.hlsl`): each probe's rays, 32 or 64, a spherical Fibonacci set turned by a rotation that changes every update (the Halton sequence, uniform over rotations), trace the scene's surfaces with `trace_hit`, out to twice their level's box.
  - **Hit shading** evaluates each hit's material (see [Hit shading](ray-scene.md#hit-shading)).
  - **Light** (`gi_probe_light.hlsl`, with `hit_light` in `gi_hit_light.hlsli`): a miss sees the ambient light. A hit's light is its emission, the light of every directional light and of up to `GI_MAX_PROBE_LIGHTS` (16) punctual lights whose spheres reach the outermost box, the brightest by their light over their squared distance first, each shadowed by a ray toward it through the shadow casters, and the probes' light from earlier updates at the hit, so light bounces once more each update. A back face of a one-sided surface is black, and its distance negative.
  - **Screen** (`gi_probe_screen.hlsl`, with `screen_light` in `gi_hit_screen.hlsli`): a hit of a front face that the prepass's depth shows takes its light from last frame's lit scene, where it was then, as the cascades' rays do. So the probes see what the screen sees: the light of a small emissive surface, which few of a probe's rays hit, and the light that the cascades gave the surfaces around it. Only the hits off the screen depend on the probes' own rays. It is a pass of its own after the light; folded into the light's shader beside its probe lookups, it hung the GPU on an RTX 5090, for a reason not found.
  - **Update** (`gi_probe_update.hlsl`): each texel of the probe's tile blends its direction's share of the rays into what it held: the radiance in a narrow lobe, the cosine-weighted mean, and the distances in a narrower lobe. A probe takes its first update as it is, then blends more slowly, down to keeping `GI_PROBE_HYSTERESIS` (0.97) of its light. The border repeats the interior across the octahedron's folds. A probe that sees back faces with more than a quarter of its rays is inside geometry and counts for nothing.
- **Sampling** (`sample_probes`): a point moves off its surface by a quarter of the level's spacing, and takes the finest level whose box holds it, blended into the next over its outermost probe. The eight probes around it count by their trilinear weight, by whether they are in front of the surface, and by whether they see the point, from the mean and the variance of their rays' distances toward it (Chebyshev, as in Majercik et al., *Dynamic Diffuse Global Illumination with Ray-Traced Irradiance Fields*, JCGT 2019). A probe that was never updated, or is inside geometry, does not count. Where none counts, or outside every level, the cascades take the ambient light, and the gathers' specular light the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light).
- **Where the cascades take it.** A cascade ray that leaves the screen takes the probes' radiance along it where it left. The merge's light beyond the last cascade, and where no probe of the next cascade matches, is the probes' radiance along the direction at the probe's surface. The gather's pixels that no probe matches, and the view model's, take the probes' mean light around the normal and their radiance along the reflection.
- **Waiting.** The probes start once their pipelines, hit shading's and the ray scene's are ready, and a frame has draw items. The screen pass starts once the lit scene has a frame of history. Until then the cascades take the ambient light, and the levels stay where they were.
- **Limits.** The ray scene has no instance sets, patch surfaces or particles, so the probes do not see foliage, terrain or water. A probe inside geometry is turned off, not moved. Static content does not let the probes settle sooner: that waits for the GPU scene to update only changed slots ([#142](https://github.com/aszecsei/cinnabar/issues/142)). Light leaks where a wall is thinner than a probe's spacing.

## Split cascades

With `Gi_Technique.Split_Cascades` and `Ray_Query`, once the world probes run, the split radiance cascades take the screen cascades' place, after Jack Tollenaar's [*Split Radiance Cascades*](https://www.jacktollenaar.top/articles/split-rc.html) (`lib/engine/render/gi_split.odin`, `gi_split.hlsli`, `gi_split_*.hlsl`). Their probes are in world space, on the surfaces that the screen shows, and keep their light from frame to frame, so the light does not smear as the camera moves, and a surface that comes into view has its light already. Their rays trace the ray scene, so what blocks the light need not be on the screen, and a long interval is not a few steps through the depth buffer. The forward pass reads their light as it reads the screen cascades'.

- **Levels** (`GI_SPLIT_PARAMS`; the numbers here are High's). Each level of detail is a window of `SPLIT_WINDOW` (512) cells on each axis around the camera. The first level's cells are 0.25 m (0.5 m at Low, 0.125 m at Ultra), and each level's twice the one before's; there are 5 (4 at Low). Level L holds the distances from 24 cells of level 0 times 2^L to twice that, by the Chebyshev distance from the camera, so a cell covers about as many pixels at every distance, and the last `SPLIT_BAND` (half) of each level's distances blends into the next. Past the last level, the light is the world probes'.
- **Following the camera.** Each window's first cell is a cell of `World_Position`, from the camera's with an arithmetic shift, so a render-origin rebase moves nothing. It is the camera's cell less 256, a multiple of `SPLIT_SNAP` (16), so the camera is always near the middle. Each frame gives each window's first cell relative to the camera from whole units, and how far it moved.
- **Cascades.** 4 (3 at Low). Cascade k's probe of a cell is the cell's window coordinates shifted right by k, so its cells are 2^k times as large, with `(directions << k)^2` directions folded into an octahedron: 6 across (4 at Low and Medium, 8 at Ultra), so 36 to 2304. A probe's key is its window coordinates, its level and a high bit, in 32 bits. The first cascade's interval is a direction texel's width at the probe, `directions / sqrt(4π)` cells, and each interval is four times as long as the one before.
- **Tables** (`gi_split_table.hlsl`). Each cascade has a fixed count of slots: 16384 for the first (8192 at Low, 32768 at Ultra), and a quarter as many for each after it, but at least 256 (1024 at Ultra, whose last cascade is otherwise full on a slow walk). A hash table for each cascade, of twice as many entries, linearly probed for at most 32, finds a key's slot. Each frame empties the tables, and a thread for each slot moves its key by its window's move and puts it back, or frees it when it left the window or no ray reached it for its cascade's lifetime; a probe keeps its slot, and its light, while it lives. The lifetime is `SPLIT_LIFETIME` (600) frames while the table has room, so a surface that leaves the view has its light when it returns. When a key finds no room, by the stats of a frame or two ago, the lifetime halves each frame, to no less than `SPLIT_MIN_LIFETIME` (8): after a turn, the probes behind the camera were reached a frame ago, and until they are freed the probes in view have no slot. A table that is only nearly full keeps its probes: freeing them early brings each one back new as it returns, which flickered on Ultra, whose third cascade is about 92% full on a slow walk. Below three quarters full, the lifetime grows by one each frame, as fast as a probe ages, so growing frees nothing, and between the two it holds. Then a thread for each traced pixel claims its cell's probe at each cascade and each of its levels with a compare-and-swap, and the first to claim a key takes a free slot. A key that finds no room has no slot, and the merge and the gather take the light from beyond it.
- **Rays** (`gi_split_trace.hlsl`). Each frame traces one ray from a pixel of each tile of 2 × 2 pixels (4 × 4 at Low, 1 × 1 at Ultra), each pixel of a tile in turn. A tile widens while the rays are more than hit shading takes (`MAX_HIT_RAYS`), so Ultra at 4K traces one pixel of each 2 × 1. The ray starts at the pixel's surface, two pixels off it, not at a probe's center, and its direction is uniform over the sphere: the R2 sequence by the frame, offset for each tile, which repeats after `RAY_CYCLE` (16) frames. So a still view's probes settle on a fixed set of rays, as a radiance cascade's do. Rays that never repeat keep moving each bin's mean, and flicker across whole walls. It reaches as far as the last cascade of the pixel's coarser level, and hit shading and the world probes' [light and screen passes](#world-probes) give its hit's light.
- **The deposit** (`gi_split_deposit.hlsl`) splits each ray by its hit's distance, at each of the pixel's levels: the cascade whose interval holds the hit counts the ray and adds the hit's light, in its bin of the ray's direction at the probe of the pixel's cell, and each cascade before it counts the ray as passing through. A ray past the last cascade passes through them all. The light is pre-exposed and added in fixed point, in units of 1 / `SPLIT_RADIANCE_SCALE` (1024) up to `SPLIT_RADIANCE_MAX` (64), with integer atomics and a dither that keeps dim light's mean. A back face within four pixels means that the ray started inside geometry, and the ray counts for nothing.
- **The merge** (`gi_split_merge.hlsl`), one pass for each cascade from the last to the first, a group for each 64 bins of each active probe, dispatched by the counts that the tables wrote. Each bin takes its frame's rays into its history: the share that passed through and the mean light of the rest, blended by the rays against the history's, which decays by `1 - 1 / history_frames` (12) with each frame that rays reach the bin, and holds at most `SPLIT_MAX_COUNT` (256) rays. A new bin starts as what the world probes see along its direction from the probe, occluders and all, with nothing passing through, and counts as half a ray, so its first few rays outweigh the guess; starting it as passing through let a probe with no rays yet see through walls. A bin that no ray reaches keeps its history, so the next ray moves it by a ray's share of `history_frames` rays and does not replace it; a bin that few rays reach therefore follows a change more slowly, in proportion. Then it adds the light that passes through it from the next cascade: the four finer directions that it splits into, at the eight probes around it, three to one on each axis, each by its confidence, `rays / (rays + 8)`, whose rays decay as a bin's do, only in frames that rays reach the probe, so a probe that goes out of view does not lose its weight and jump back when it returns; and each of those bins by whether rays reached it, as one that none did holds only a new bin's guess. Where no such probe has light, and beyond the last cascade, it is the world probes' radiance along the direction from where the bin's interval ends. From the probe's center, it would hold again the occluders that the cascades already traced: with both, Sponza's interiors had a quarter to two fifths of the sky that rays from their surfaces see. The first cascade's probes also write their tile of an atlas, 6 × 6 octahedral texels with a border, as the world probes' are: the cosine-weighted mean of their light around each direction (`direction_weight`).
- **The gather** (`gi_split_gather.hlsl`): each pixel's diffuse light is the atlas's at the first cascade's eight probes around it, along its normal, and its specular light the second cascade's merged light along its reflection, blended between the four nearest directions. Each probe counts by its trilinear weight, by how much it is in front of the surface, as the world probes do, and by its confidence, and the levels blend by the distance. The world probes' light counts a hundredth as much as one probe everywhere, so a place that no probe has reached yet fades in from it, and it counts fully past the last level. Where the world probes have no light, the specular light is the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) at the pixel's roughness. No history follows the pixels.
- **Memory.** The tables, bins and atlas take about 19 MB at Low, 49 MB at Medium, 99 MB at High and 418 MB at Ultra, made for the level when the split first runs at it. The rays' buffers take 84 bytes for each traced pixel, with hit shading's: 74 MB at High at 1440p. The diffuse and specular histories are still made, though the split does not read them.
- **Debug view and stats.** With the `Gi_Probes` [debug view](post-processing.md#debug-views), the gather also draws each pixel's probe into a texture of its own, `Gi_Frame.probe_view`, not into the light, which the lit scene keeps for the next frames' rays. After the merge, the tables' counters are copied to the next of `SPLIT_STATS_COPIES` (3) readback buffers, and the frame start takes each copy that came back, as the [culling stats](gpu-scene.md#occlusion-culling) are taken: `gi_split_stats` gives each cascade's probes that rays reached, its slots, and the keys that found no room, a frame or two ago, or none when the split cascades did not run last frame. The editor's Resource Monitor shows them.
- **Waiting.** The split cascades start once their pipelines are ready, and run when the world probes run this frame and hit shading has room for their batch. Until then, the frame takes the screen cascades. A frame that takes them after a frame that had the split cascades logs a warning with the reason, since the whole frame's indirect light changes.
- **Limits.** Light leaks where a wall is thinner than a cell, since the rays from both of its sides reach the same probes. The ray scene has no instance sets, patch surfaces or particles, so the rays pass through foliage, terrain and water. Each hit that the screen does not show casts a shadow ray toward every directional light and each listed punctual light, so the hits' light costs most at Ultra.

## Voxel cones

With `Gi_Technique.Voxel_Cones`, voxel cone tracing takes the screen cascades' place. It follows Crassin et al., *Interactive Indirect Illumination Using Voxel Cone Tracing*, on a clipmap of 3D textures around the camera, as NVIDIA's VXGI keeps it, not on a sparse octree (`lib/engine/render/gi_voxel.odin`, `gi_voxel.hlsli`, `gi_voxel_*.hlsl`, `material_voxelize.hlsli`). The rasterizer voxelizes the scene, and the voxels light themselves. So the technique needs no `Ray_Query`, and it sees what is off the screen. No preset chooses it: a quality does, or the settings file's `quality.gi = Voxel_Cones`. The forward pass reads its light as it reads the screen cascades' light.

- **Levels** (`GI_VOXEL_PARAMS`; the numbers here are High's). There are 5 levels (6 at Ultra). Each level is a box of `res`³ voxels, where `res` is 128 (64 at Low, 96 at Medium). Level 0's voxels are 0.125 m (0.25 m at Low), and each level's voxels are twice as large as the level before. So High's boxes are 16 m to 256 m across. Each voxel size is a power of two of meters. A box's first cell is a cell of `World_Position`, from the camera's position with an arithmetic shift, so a render-origin rebase moves nothing.
- **Following the camera.** A box moves when the camera is more than `VOXEL_SNAP` (8) of its voxels from its middle. Its first cell after the move is a multiple of 8, so a box covers whole voxels of the next level. Each level's storage is toroidal: a voxel's texel is its cell modulo `res`, so the textures wrap. The cones sample them with a sampler that repeats, so the filtering works across the seam. A box that moves holds no surfaces until it voxelizes again, which it does in the same frame.
- **Storage.** Each level has five 3D textures of its own, which the renderer owns:
  - four `R32_Uint` textures, for the surfaces' albedo, normal, emission and coverage mask;
  - one `RGBA16_Float` texture, for the light that the voxels send out, premultiplied by their opacity.

  That is 24 bytes a voxel: 30 MiB at Low, 101 MiB at Medium, 240 MiB at High and 288 MiB at Ultra (`voxel_bytes`). The textures are made for the level the first time the voxel cones run at it.
- **Voxelization** (`material_voxelize.hlsli`, the `Voxelize` material pass).
  - **When.** A level voxelizes when its box moved. One more level voxelizes each frame in turn, so a moving object is in every level within as many frames as there are levels.
  - **Views.** `begin_gi` decides which levels voxelize, before the scene's views are made. Each level that voxelizes has three orthographic views, one along each axis, after the shadow views. They cull and draw the scene's opaque bins as any view does, instance sets and patch surfaces included.
  - **Passes.** A compute pass first empties the level's surfaces. Then one render pass with no attachments draws the views at twice `res` across, so that a pixel is half a voxel. It uses the prepass's vertex or mesh stages and each material's `ps_voxelize`.
  - **Fragments.** `ps_voxelize` evaluates the material and offers the fragment to the voxel that holds it, with one atomic for each texture. The albedo is what `shade_indirect` gives from a white surrounding, as hit shading takes it, in 8 bits a channel; the normal is folded into the octahedron, in 12 bits a coordinate. Each is led by the fragment's key, a hash of the quarter of a voxel that it is in (`voxel_key`), and `InterlockedMax` keeps the largest, so a voxel holds one fragment's albedo and normal, and the same fragment's whatever the order the fragments come in. The emission is the largest RGB9E5 word, and the mask takes the fragment's half-size cube with `InterlockedOr`.
  - **Why one fragment.** Crassin and Green (*Octree-Based Sparse Voxelization Using the GPU Hardware Rasterizer*) keep running means in a compare-and-swap loop. Those take the fragments in the order that they come, so a level that voxelized the same surfaces again held slightly different means each time. With a level voxelizing each frame in turn, a still view of Sponza flickered at a spread p99 of about 1.7. The order-free voxels leave it at 0, and voxelizing again changes nothing where nothing moved.
  - **Coverage.** A fragment counts only in the view along its triangle's dominant axis, which it takes from the derivatives of its position. That view shows the triangle at its largest, and there the triangle's depth changes by less than a voxel across a pixel. So no triangle needs a projection of its own, and no geometry shader is needed. Where the device has `Conservative_Raster`, a triangle smaller than a pixel still marks its voxel. A conservative rasterizer's fragments past a triangle's edges extrapolate its position, and in a view that sees the triangle edge-on, that position lands far from it: before the dominant-axis rule, Sponza's curtains scattered voxels through the open nave.
  - **Level of detail.** On the mesh shader path, a voxelization view (`VIEW_VOXELS`) takes each mesh's level whose error is at most half a voxel, at every distance (`VOXEL_LOD_NEAR`), from all of the mesh's meshlets, not from those that `select_meshlets.hlsl` picked for the camera. No meshlet is culled by its normal cone, which `cull_eye` tests against the camera. At the camera's level of detail, Sponza's voxelization took 22 ms a frame on an RTX 5090, as millions of small triangles reached the same coarse voxels and waited on each other's compare and swap. At the voxels' level of detail, it took 0.6 ms, and with one atomic for each texture, 0.2 ms.
- **Opacity.** The mask's texel records which of the voxel's eight half-size cubes a fragment covered. The voxel's opacity is the largest share of its cross-section that those cubes cover along any axis. A wall through a voxel fills half of its cubes but blocks every ray across it, so the share of the cubes would let half of the light through. Where a surface covers only part of a coarse voxel's cross-section, light gets through.
- **Injection** (`gi_voxel_inject.hlsl`). It runs on the compute queue for every level every frame, finest level first.
  - **The light.** Each voxel's light is the sum of three terms, times its opacity:
    - its emission;
    - its albedo over π, times the light of every directional light and of up to 16 punctual lights whose spheres reach the outermost box (`probe_lights`);
    - its albedo, times the light that bounces to it.
  - **The normal.** A light falls off with the cosine to the voxel's normal.
  - **Shadows.** Each light's shadow is a narrow cone toward it through the levels' opacity, out of the last box or to the light. So a voxel off the screen is shadowed as one on the screen is, with no shadow map and no ray.
  - **The bounce.** It is a 90° cone along the normal through the next level's light. This frame's injection has not reached that level yet, so its light is last frame's. Light therefore bounces once more each frame, and no second copy of the light is kept. The last level's bounce is the ambient light.
  - **Covered voxels.** A voxel that a finer level covers is left to the filter.
- **The filter** (`gi_voxel_filter.hlsl`). It runs for each level but the last. Each voxel of the next level that the level covers takes the eight voxels that it holds. Its opacity is what a ray across them sees: along each axis, the two voxels of each of the four columns block one after the other, and the axis whose columns block the most counts. Its light keeps the eight voxels' mean color. So a cone as wide as a coarse level's voxels sees the finer level's surfaces, filtered, and a wall one voxel thick still blocks it. With the mean opacity, a wall's opacity halved at each level. In Sponza, the filter's opacity took the share of the sky that the voxel cones see from 7.7 to 20 times the reference's (see [Comparison](#comparison)) to 4.0 to 8.4 times, and taking the last level for cones wider than its voxels, which the march had taken for past the clipmap, took it to 1.4 to 3.5 times at six of seven poses, and 0.34 at the last.
- **The gather** (`gi_voxel_gather.hlsl`).
  - **Cones.** Each pixel traces diffuse cones around its normal: 6 (4 at Low, 9 at Ultra), one along the normal and rings around it, each weighted by its cosine (`voxel_cones`). The rings lean 55° from the normal with a half angle of 30° (32° and 64° with 22.5° at Ultra), so no cone's edge lies along the surface, where a wide cone would sample the surface's own voxels. It also traces one narrow cone along its reflection.
  - **The march.** Each cone starts a voxel and a half of the point's level off the surface, past the trilinear reach of the point's own voxel, and marches front to back, in steps of half its width. Each step samples the level whose voxels are as wide as the cone, blended with the next level, or the last level once the cone is wider than its voxels, and corrects the sample's opacity for the step's length. A cone stops when 95% of its light is blocked, or past the last box. The ambient light fills the share of a diffuse cone that is not blocked, and the sky's [cubemap](sky.md#specular-light) at the pixel's roughness the reflection cone's.
  - **Noise and history.** The cones turn about the normal by a noise of the pixel's that stays from frame to frame. With a turn that moved each frame, a still view's spread p99 was 5.0, since the histories hold too few frames to average it; with this one it was 1.7, and 0 once the voxels were order-free. Both lights blend into their histories by the velocity, as in the screen cascades' gather, over at most `history_frames` (8) frames.
  - **The view model.** Its pixels take the light around the camera.
- **Debug view.** With the `Gi_Probes` [debug view](post-processing.md#debug-views), the gather also draws into `Gi_Frame.probe_view` the albedo of the first voxel that each pixel's view ray meets.
- **Waiting.** The voxel cones start when their pipelines are ready. Like every technique, they also need the screen cascades' pipelines. Until then, the frame takes the screen cascades. A frame that takes the screen cascades after a frame that had the voxel cones logs a warning with the reason. A material from before the `Voxelize` pass draws nothing in it.
- **Limits.**
  - A surface thinner than a voxel lets light through.
  - A voxel's light is the same in every direction. So a cone that grazes a wall can take the light of the wall's other side. The coarse levels leak the most.
  - Transparent surfaces, particles, ribbons and impostors are not voxelized.
  - The injection's shadows are as coarse as the voxels, and soft.
  - The reflection's cone is narrow, so on a glossy floor its light shows the finest level's voxels as squares, which the screen and split cascades' coarser directions blur away.
  - A voxel keeps one fragment's albedo and normal, not their mean, so a voxel where surfaces of different colors or facing different ways meet takes one of them.
  - Moving objects reach the coarse levels a few frames late, and the light that they block changes a few frames after that.
  - The pixels' light at Sponza's poses is 1.3 to 1.8 times the reference's at most poses, but 3.4 times in an enclosed gallery and 0.4 times on a wall in the open (see [Comparison](#comparison)).

## Comparison

The radiance cascades and the voxel cones, measured in Intel's Sponza with its curtains on an RTX 5090, in the editor's viewport of 911 × 481, with no anti-aliasing (`noTemporalAa`, `antiAliasing=Off`), the scene's sun, and each technique at the quality's level through `quality.gi`. The screen cascades ran on the same device; they use no ray queries, so a device without them runs the same technique, though its shadow maps in place of the traced shadows change the light that the cascades gather. The scripts that took them are not in the repository.

- **Accuracy.** Every hit or voxel sends out no light and the sky is 1, so a pixel's diffuse light is the share of the sky that its surface sees. The reference traces 32 cosine-weighted rays from each pixel to 64 m. Each number is the technique's mean over the reference's, after sRGB to linear, at seven poses, and the mean absolute error of 16 × 16 pixel blocks.

- **Stability.** In 8-bit codes of the lit view, from `flickerstat`: the p99 of each pixel's spread and of its largest step, still (40 screenshots), on a walk of 1.5 m out and back (16 returns), and turning by up to 0.6 radians and back (16 returns). The voxel cones' gather and the screen cascades' passes are per pixel, so at 1440p they cost about 8 times as much; the split cascades' rays are one for each tile of pixels, and the voxel cones' voxelization and injection do not depend on the view's size. - **Memory.** The split cascades' tables, bins and atlas take 19, 49, 99 and 418 MB from Low to Ultra, and the world probes about 16 MB a level beside them. The voxel cones' levels take 30, 101, 240 and 288 MiB.
- **What each needs and sees.** The split cascades and the world probes need `Ray_Query`, and see what the ray scene has, which leaves out instance sets, patch surfaces and particles. The voxel cones need nothing, and the rasterizer voxelizes instance sets and patch surfaces too. The screen cascades see only the screen.
- **Summary.** The split cascades are the most accurate, by two to four times in block error, and the cheaper of the two world-space techniques up to High; at Ultra, the voxel cones are. The voxel cones are the most stable, still and after a turn, and cost 0.7 to 1.3 ms at every level, but leak through the walls of enclosed spaces, where they are up to 3.4 times as bright as the reference. Against the screen cascades, which they could replace on a device without `Ray_Query`, they keep the light of what is off the screen and do not change as the view turns, for 0.6 to 1.1 ms more at this size and 30 to 288 MiB. No preset chooses them yet.
