# 2D

What a 2D game draws with: an orthographic camera, and sprites from sprite sheets. The camera is in [Frame](frame.md#orthographic-views), and the sheets are in [Content: Sprite sheets](../content/kinds/sprite-sheets.md). This document is the sprites: `lib/engine/render/sheet_sprites.odin`, `lib/engine/sprite` and `sheet_sprite.hlsl`.

## Sprite renderers

- **`Sprite_Renderer`** (stable ID 56) draws one frame of a sprite sheet at its entity's `Transform`, as a quad that faces the camera. `DEFAULT_SPRITE_RENDERER` is white, opaque, playing, at speed 1.

| Field | What it is |
|---|---|
| `sheet` | The sprite sheet asset |
| `frame` | The frame that shows while no animation plays: an index of the sheet's frames |
| `animation` | The animation that plays: an index of the sheet's animations plus 1, and 0 for none |
| `time` | How far into the animation it is, in seconds. The `animate sprites` system writes it |
| `speed` | How fast the animation plays: 1 is the sheet's rate |
| `playing` | Whether the animation moves on |
| `color`, `opacity` | Multiply the texture's color and its opacity |
| `layer`, `order` | What it draws over (see [Sorting](#sorting)) |
| `lit` | Whether the scene's lights light it (see [Lit and unlit](#lit-and-unlit)) |
| `pixel_snap` | Draw it on whole pixels (see [Pixel snapping](#pixel-snapping)) |
| `flip_x`, `flip_y` | Mirror the frame about its pivot |

- **The quad** is the frame's size in texels over the sheet's `pixels_per_unit`, in meters, times the `Transform`'s x and y scales. It is placed so the frame's pivot is at the entity's position. It always faces the camera: its sides are along the view's right and up, turned about the view's axis by as much as the `Transform` turns about its own z. So in a 2D scene, whose camera looks along +z, a sprite is in the xy plane and turns as its entity does, and in a 3D scene it is a billboard.
- **These are not the compute sprites.** The `Sprite` of [Transparency](transparency.md#sprites) is a soft square that the particles append and that moment-based transparency orders. A `Sprite_Renderer` is a textured quad of an entity, drawn in a sorted order.

## Sorting

Each view sorts its sprites on the CPU (`sheet_sprite_before`), and draws them in that order, so each covers the ones before it:

1. By `layer`, from the lowest.
2. Then by `order`, from the lowest.
3. Then by depth along the view's axis, from the farthest.
4. Sprites that are equal in all three keep the order in which the world gives them.

The sprites test against the scene's depth, so the opaque world covers a sprite behind it, and they do not write it, so one sprite never hides another by depth: only the order decides.

## Drawing

- **Extraction** (`begin_sheet_sprites`): each view takes the `Sprite_Renderer`s that are not hidden and not clear, whose sheet is cooked and whose texture is resident, up to `MAX_SHEET_SPRITES` (65536). It writes one `Sheet_Sprite_Record` each into the upload ring, relative to the view's camera, in the sorted order. A sheet is loaded the first time that a sprite shows it, and a sheet that is not cooked is asked for once.
- **The pass** (`sheet sprites`) is one draw of six vertices for each sprite, after the transparent surfaces and before post-processing. The vertex shader pulls its sprite's record. The blend is premultiplied alpha.
- **The texture** is the sheet's atlas, read with the sheet's filter: a linear sampler or a nearest one, both clamped.
- **The fog** dims a sprite and adds its light, as it does for the transparent surfaces, and the color is pre-exposed.
- **A scene of sprites alone** has no other draws. The frame still writes the camera's constants for it (`Frame.wants_sheet_sprites`).
- **Every view** draws the sprites: an extra view extracts and sorts them for its own camera.

## Lit and unlit

- **Unlit** (the default): the texture's color times the sprite's color, as it is.
- **Lit:** every directional light and the punctual lights of the pixel's cluster, with their shadow maps, and the ambient light. A sprite scatters a light at a fixed cosine (`SHEET_SPRITE_COSINE`, 0.5), from whichever side the light is, because a 2D scene's lights are in the plane of its sprites, where a surface's own cosine would be 0. A light with a channel of the traced shadow mask does not shadow sprites.

## Pixel snapping

A sprite with `pixel_snap` draws with its pivot on a corner of the screen's pixels, and without the camera's jitter. When a texel is a whole count of pixels wide, each texel then covers whole pixels, and with the sheet's `nearest` filter pixel art stays sharp as the camera moves. A texel is a whole count of pixels wide when the orthographic camera's `height` is the view's height in pixels, over `pixels_per_unit`, over a whole number.

## Animation

- **The `animate sprites` system** (`lib/engine/sprite`, stage `Update`) adds the frame's time, times `speed`, to the `time` of each `Sprite_Renderer` that is `playing` and has an animation. A game calls `sprite.register` in `init` and `on_reload`.
- **The frame that shows** is a function of the animation and the time (`render.sprite_frame`, `format.sprite_sheet_frame_at`): each frame shows for its own duration, or for one over the animation's rate. A looping animation starts again, and one that does not loop stays on its last frame.
- **By name:** `sprite.play` finds an animation by its name in the sheet and starts it, `sprite.set_frame` shows a frame by name, `sprite.animation_name` gives the name back, and `sprite.finished` says whether an animation that does not loop is over. A component cannot hold a name, so `Sprite_Renderer.animation` is the index.

## Scripts

`sprite.register_scripts` defines the `Sprites` library and exposes `Sprite_Renderer` to scripts.

| Function | What it does |
|---|---|
| `Sprites.play(entity, name, restart?)` | Plays the animation of that name. One that plays already goes on, unless `restart` is true |
| `Sprites.stop(entity)` | Holds the frame that shows |
| `Sprites.set_frame(entity, name)` | Shows the frame of that name, in place of an animation |
| `Sprites.animation(entity)` | The name of the animation, or nil |
| `Sprites.finished(entity)` | Whether an animation that does not loop is over |

A name that the sheet does not have is an error. While the sheet is not cooked, the calls do nothing.

## The editor

- **Picking:** with the frame's IDs kept, a second pass (`sheet sprite ids`, `sheet_sprite_ids.hlsl`) draws the same quads into the ID buffer and the depth, where a sprite's opacity is at least 0.1. So a click picks a sprite, and the outline follows its shape.
- **In Edit**, the world's time stands still. A selected sprite plays its animation by the host's time, as a selected particle system does (`Frame_Desc.playing`), so its animation shows without Play.

## The sample

The sample's `scenes/sprites.scene`, which F7 in mygame cycles to, is a 2D scene under an orthographic camera that shows 9 m from bottom to top, so a texel of its sheets, at 16 to a meter, is 5 pixels at a height of 720.

- **The sheets** are in `content/sprites`: `hero.sprite_sheet` packs nine images of 16 by 16 texels into one atlas, with an `idle` and a `walk` animation, and `coin.sprite_sheet` cuts a strip of four frames out of `coin.png` with a grid, with a `spin`. Both read with the nearest filter.
- **The hero** walks from side to side over a row of tiles. `scripts/sprite_walker.luau` moves it, flips it at each end with `SpriteRenderer.flip_x`, and plays `walk` and `idle` by name with `Sprites.play`.
- **The cards** show the sorting: red is nearest the camera and in the lowest layer, so the others cover it; green and blue share a layer, and blue's order puts it over green; yellow is the farthest, in the highest layer, and half clear over them all.
- **The crates** show the lighting: the upper row is lit, and is bright near the lamp, a `Point_Light` in front of its middle, and dark away from it; the lower row is unlit, and shows the texture as it is.
- **The volume** sets a manual exposure, the PBR Neutral tone mapper and no anti-aliasing, so the unlit sprites keep their colors and the snapped ones stay sharp.

## Limits

- A sprite writes no velocity, so temporal AA and FSR can leave a trail behind a sprite that moves or animates. A 2D game sets its anti-aliasing to FXAA, SMAA or Off.
- A sprite casts no shadow, and is not in the reflections or the global illumination.
- The auto exposure changes how bright an unlit sprite shows. A 2D game that wants the texture's own colors sets a manual exposure.
