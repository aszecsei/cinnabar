# Volumes

Volumes give settings a place in the world: the post-processing at the camera, the fog, a game's own settings. The renderer's settings and post effects are built on them.

Some settings change with where a point is: the post-processing and the fog at the camera. A volume gives them a place. `lib/engine/volume` has no other engine package below `ecs`, so any feature can use it.

- **`Volume`** (stable ID 42) gives its entity a shape: `Global`, everywhere, or a `Box` or a `Sphere` at its presented pose. A box's extents are half its size along the entity's axes, times its scale. A sphere's radius is `extents.x` times the largest of its scale. An extent of 0 is `DEFAULT_EXTENT` (5 m).
- **Weight:** inside the shape, the volume has its weight (0 means 1). Outside it, the weight falls off to 0 over the blend distance. To turn a volume off, deactivate its entity.
- **Settings components** go on the same entity, such as the renderer's `Bloom` or `Fog`. Each setting is a `volume.Value(T)`: a value and whether the volume overrides it. An overridden value is used as it is, so 0 is 0. A setting that a volume does not override comes from the volumes below it, or from the default, and the volume keeps its value for when it overrides it again. So a small volume can change one setting and keep the rest, and turning an override off and on compares the two.
- **Defaults:** a settings component registers its defaults with `ecs.register_component(registry, T, id, DEFAULT_T)`. The volumes blend from them, and a scene starts the component from them, so a volume added in the editor shows the defaults, overriding nothing.
- **Overrides in scenes:** the field walker shows a `Value` as one field with the value's kind, and the editor shows a check box before it. A scene that sets the field overrides it. To keep a value without overriding it, the scene also sets `<field>.override = false`, before or after the value. A reset removes both, so the setting goes back to its default. A game that makes a volume in code sets `override` itself. Any struct of exactly `override: bool` and `value: T` is a setting this way.
- **Order:** the volumes that reach a point apply from the bottom: by priority, then the global ones, then the larger before the smaller, then by entity index. Each moves the settings that it overrides toward its own by its weight. Numbers, vectors and colors move in between, a position along the line, and a bool, an enum or an integer changes at a weight of a half or more.
- **API:** `Volumes` is a registration, made again in `on_reload`. `gather` finds the volumes that reach a point, skipping the editor's hidden entities, and `blend` gives a settings component from them, with whether any of them has it. `sample` does both. They allocate nothing, so jobs can call them. At most `MAX_INFLUENCES` (64) volumes reach a point.

The renderer's [Settings](../rendering/frame.md#settings) and a game's [post effects](../rendering/post-processing.md#post-effects) are settings components in this sense, and the editor's [Properties](../editor/shell-and-panels.md#properties) panel shows the override check boxes.
